# 2026-05-15 — What I am afraid of

A list. I want to write it down because the fears live smaller when they are written.

1. We will open and the food will not be ready. The team will not be tight. The diners will leave disappointed.

2. A diner will get badly sick from something I cooked. They will be hospitalized. They will sue. The story will follow Lark for years.

3. Beto will leave. He will get a better offer (he will) and the kitchen will lose its center.

4. The lease overrun is bigger than I am admitting. We will run out of cash in month 8.

5. Mateo and I will fight in a way we can't recover from. The restaurant will become a way we punish each other.

6. The press will be cruel. A bad review in Eater or Willamette Week will define us before we have time to build.

7. My mother will get sick. I will have to choose between the restaurant and her. The restaurant will not understand.

8. The opening will go well and then in month 4 we will collapse. The energy will leave the team. The food will slip.

9. I will become the chef I have always disliked: defensive, performative, distant. I will not even notice.

10. We will succeed and I will not feel anything. The years of work will end in a flat feeling. The restaurant will become a job.

11. A staff member will accuse me of something I did not do. The accusation will become a story. The reputation cost will be real.

12. The Pacific NW supply chain will fail. Climate change is bringing unpredictable seasons. The salmon will not come this year. The matsutake will not come. We will pivot and pivot and pivot.

13. We will be good but not great. We will be a B+ restaurant in Portland. I will know it. I will not be able to fix it. We will close in year 4 quietly.

That's 13. Not all the fears, but the ones I can name.

What I want to say about the list: every fear is the result of caring about something. The opposite of fear is not bravery. The opposite of fear is indifference. I am afraid because I care. The fear is the cost of caring.

I do not want to stop being afraid. I want to manage the fear so it does not stop me.

The way I manage fear, I have learned, is to do the next thing. The next thing tomorrow morning is: meeting with Patrick about the matsutake season forecast. I will go to that meeting. I will not think about the 13 fears during the meeting.

After the meeting, the next next thing.

This is the work. This is what I signed up for.

— Aiya
