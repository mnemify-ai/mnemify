# 2026-05-03 — Mateo and me

We had our first real argument about the restaurant tonight.

It was about a wine pairing for the cheese course. He wanted a tawny port. I wanted a Madeira. We have been going back and forth for two weeks. Tonight at home it became personal.

He said: "you don't trust my palate."

I said: "I trust your palate. I think this specific pairing is wrong."

He said: "you do not understand that the cheese course is a wine moment, not a food moment."

I said: "the cheese course is BOTH and I get the food side better than you."

It escalated.

We stopped. We did the thing we agreed to do. We left the table. He went outside. I stayed at the table. After 10 minutes he came back. We restated the actual disagreement. He won. The Madeira is great but the tawny is great too and the choice is his.

I conceded. He was correct that this is his domain.

We then drank a glass of the tawny he wants to pour. It is a beautiful wine. I am being stubborn. The cheese course will be paired with the tawny.

What I want to write down:

Working with the person I love is harder than I expected. We have rules: no business at home, no relationship at work. The rules are theoretical. In practice the lines blur.

The blur tonight was about a wine pairing. Tomorrow it could be about something bigger.

I think we are good at this. We have the discipline to stop, separate, restart. We have the trust to fight without it being destructive.

But I am aware that running a restaurant with your partner has destroyed many relationships. I am not naive. I am paying attention.

What I noticed tonight: when we fought, I felt the loneliness of running a restaurant alone for the first time. Like, what would I do if Mateo and I were not OK? Then what?

The answer is: keep the restaurant going. The restaurant is bigger than us individually. The team depends on it. The investors depend on it. The diners coming in July depend on it.

But that is a hard answer. The honest answer is: if Mateo and I are not OK, the restaurant becomes very hard to run.

This is what Diane warned me about a year ago. I dismissed it then. I am taking it more seriously now.

What I want to say to Mateo and have not yet said: thank you for being here. Thank you for not being precious about your wine knowledge. Thank you for arguing with me without trying to win. Thank you for the tawny.

I will say it tomorrow. I will say it tomorrow.

— Aiya
