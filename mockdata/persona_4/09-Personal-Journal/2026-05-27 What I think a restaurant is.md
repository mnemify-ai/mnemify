# 2026-05-27 — What I think a restaurant is

Reflective entry. I wrote a version of this in culinary school 16 years ago and want to write a 2026 version. The two versions in conversation.

A restaurant is a place where someone makes food and someone eats it. The food is made carefully and the eating is welcomed. That's the irreducible thing. Everything else is decoration around it.

A restaurant is a relationship. The chef and the diner do not know each other. They meet through the food. The chef tries to anticipate what the diner needs. The diner trusts the chef enough to let go.

A restaurant is a discipline. The chef has learned a craft over years. The craft makes the food possible. Without the craft, the food is improvisation. Improvisation can be beautiful but it is not what a restaurant is.

A restaurant is a team. The chef alone cannot run a service. The team alone cannot run the food. Both are required. The team is the engine. The chef is the orientation.

A restaurant is a place. The physical space matters. The smell of the kitchen, the wood of the counter, the light at 6pm. The diner is in a place, not a concept.

A restaurant is a ritual. The arc of a meal — the sit, the water, the bread, the first taste, the meal, the last taste — is a ritual that the diner is invited into. The ritual creates meaning. Without it the food is just food.

A restaurant is a community. The team eats together. The diners share the room. Over time, the regulars come and the room becomes a community. The community is not the goal but it is a measure.

A restaurant is a business. It has to make money to stay open. It has to pay its team. It has to pay its rent. The business reality limits the artistic reality. The art has to fit within what the business can sustain. This is a constraint, not a tragedy.

A restaurant is a teacher. The chef teaches the diner what the food is and could be. The team teaches each other what excellence looks like. The diner sometimes teaches the chef what they did not expect to learn.

A restaurant is a small thing in the world. The diner ate dinner. The diner went home. The diner's life went on. The restaurant is not the diner's life. The restaurant is a small good thing among the many small things in the diner's day. The restaurant does not need to be more than this.

The version I wrote at 22 was more grandiose. I thought a restaurant was a "vehicle for cultural exchange" and a "love letter to a region." I cringe at those phrases now.

The version at 38 is simpler. A restaurant is a place where someone makes food and someone eats it. We do that with care. We pay for it. We close at the end of the night. We open again the next.

That is enough.

It is more than I knew at 22.

— Aiya
