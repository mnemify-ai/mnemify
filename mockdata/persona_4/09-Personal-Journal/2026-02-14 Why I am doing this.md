# 2026-02-14 — Why I am doing this

Friday, late at night, the build-out site. Beto left two hours ago. I should have left too. I am sitting on a crate in the half-built dining room with a beer.

I want to write down why I am doing this. So that when I am tired (which is every day now) I can come back to this and remember.

I am doing this because I want a place. A specific place that I made, where the food is what I think food should be, where the people are taken care of, where the diner who walks in is seen.

I have worked in 11 restaurants. None of them was mine. Even the good ones — even Atomix, where I learned the most — they were someone else's vision. I executed. I was useful. I was praised.

But I always knew there was a thing I wanted to do that I could not do in another chef's kitchen. The thing was: I wanted to make a small restaurant where every choice was specifically mine and Mateo's and the team's, and no one else's. A place where the discipline was Japanese and the ingredients were Pacific NW and the spirit was — I don't know what to call it. Unfussy hospitality, maybe.

I am 38. My mother was 38 when she opened the small Japanese grocery in Seattle that she ran until 2018. I am the same age she was. I am thinking about this a lot.

She did not want me to be a cook. She wanted me to be a doctor or a professor. I went to culinary school against her will. She did not speak to me for 6 months. Then she came around. Now she is proud.

She will fly down for the opening. She is 70. She still moves like she is 50. She will sit at the counter and watch me work. I want her to see what I built.

I am doing this for her too. Not as a debt to her but as a gift. She gave me her seriousness about food. I am building a restaurant on it.

My father has been dead 7 years. He would have been at every soft opening dinner. He would have taken photographs. He would have made the staff laugh. I miss him every day. The restaurant is a place his absence is sharpest.

I am doing this for him too. He believed I would do this. He told me to. He didn't live to see it.

Mateo is asleep at home. He has been my partner for 8 years. We have been planning this place since year 2 of our relationship. The fact that we are now doing it together is the most fortunate thing in my life.

I am doing this with him because he is the partner I want. Not "for" him. With him.

I am doing this for the team. Beto, Caro, Lila, Theo, Inez, Jasper, Sienna, Kai, Erin, Floor Theo, Lia. They are not employees. They are the people we are building this with.

I am doing this for the diner. The diner who comes to Lark wanting a good meal, who paid $185, who has expectations and hopes. I want to give them more than they expected.

I am doing this because if I don't do it, I will be 50 and still working in someone else's kitchen wondering what I would have made.

That last sentence is the truest one. I am scared of being 50 and regretting not having tried.

So I am trying.

The bills are real. The lease is real. The 12-hour days are real. The fear is real.

But the kitchen is half-built and beautiful. The team is coming together. The menu is taking its shape. The suppliers are aligned. The soft opening is 4 months away.

I am tired. I am hopeful. I am going to drink the rest of this beer and go home.

I want to remember this night when the opening is hard.

— Aiya
