# 2026-03-22 — The hardest week

This week was the hardest of the build-out.

Tuesday: HVAC inspector failed us on three items. Contractor was furious; said the items were not in scope. I had to mediate. Mateo and I bought 6 hours of additional contractor time.

Wednesday: Permit revision came back demanding a $4,500 fire suppression upgrade we had not budgeted. I called Diane. She did not yell. She said "this is the kind of thing that happens. Budget for the next surprise."

Thursday: Inez (line cook) had a family emergency and we lost her for 48 hours. Beto and I covered her prep.

Friday: I cried in the walk-in.

Friday afternoon I went to the walk-in to count the lamb shoulders for the weekend's testing. Anderson's shipment had been 4 lbs short and I was thinking about it. I closed the door behind me. I was alone. I sat on the floor and cried for 10 minutes.

It was not about Anderson's shoulder. It was about everything. The HVAC, the permit, Inez, the weight of holding all of this together with Mateo and Beto and Diane all looking at me.

I came out and washed my face. The team was prepping. Nobody saw.

I am writing this down because I want to remember that I cried in the walk-in. Not because it was shameful. Because it was true.

The next day was Saturday. We had a private dinner for 8 (Diane's friends). I cooked. The dinner went well. Diane caught my eye after the main course and said "you OK?" I said yes. She said "you don't have to be."

I love her.

The week ended. Sunday I slept 11 hours. Mateo made me coffee. He didn't say anything. He just brought it.

This is the rhythm now. Hard days. Recovery. Hard days. Recovery.

I think most chefs don't write about this. The cooking memoirs all have the heroic moments and the funny stories. The actual texture of building a restaurant — the failure of the inspector, the lost ingredient, the cry in the walk-in — gets edited out.

It should not be edited out. The truth of this work is: it is mostly hard, sometimes beautiful, and the beautiful moments are what make the hard ones bearable.

The lamb shoulder for the testing came in fine the following Tuesday.

— Aiya
