# 2026-04-05 — On the lamb course

The lamb course is locked. As locked as anything is right now.

Anderson Ranches lamb shoulder, slow-cooked in dashi for 4 hours, then finished with a glaze of fermented black garlic. Plum (a salt-cured ume) and a single dab of yuzu kosho on the plate. Served with a small spoon of grain (farro, cooked in lamb dashi, finished with brown butter).

It took 47 versions. I am not exaggerating.

The lamb was always going to be the lamb. We picked Anderson Ranches in November and tested 6 ranches before. The lamb itself is not the question.

The question was: what do we do with it?

Version 1: traditional Provençal lamb. Beto laughed. "This is what they serve at Cafe Boulud."

Version 7: lamb tartare with Japanese seasonings. Cold. Wrong texture for the moment in the menu.

Version 12: lamb wrapped in shiso leaf, like a Korean ssam approach. Almost worked. The leaf got soggy in plating.

Version 19: glazed with a teriyaki-style sauce. Too sweet. The lamb pushed back against the sweetness in the wrong way.

Version 25: traditional shoulder with no Asian element. Beautiful but didn't belong on this menu.

Version 31: the dashi braise idea. Beto's suggestion. We tested the braising liquid 8 times.

Version 38: with mountain pepper crust. Almost made it. Lost in version 41 because the pepper distracted from the fermented black garlic.

Version 44: fermented black garlic locked in. Plum locked in. Just refining the plating.

Version 47: yuzu kosho added. The acid pop the dish needed.

I want to write down why this matters. The lamb course is the climax of the menu. After cold courses, after the soup, after the mushroom and the fish, the diner needs something rich. The lamb has to land.

Most tasting menus put the lamb out and let the meat carry the dish. We don't do that. We treat the lamb the way I would treat a piece at Atomix — surrounded by smaller marks, balanced against tension. The plum is the foil for the lamb's richness. The yuzu kosho is the spark. The farro is the bed.

The first time I cooked this version for the team, Beto ate his portion slowly. Then said: "this is the dish." That meant something. Beto does not give cheap compliments.

Lila ate hers and said: "I am going to make a dessert that talks to this."

I am proud of this dish. I have not made a dish I am this proud of since the trout course at Atomix that became a small signature.

The course is locked. We will not change it before opening unless something forces us to.

The lamb course is also where the menu can go wrong on a bad night. If the lamb is overcooked, if the sauce breaks, if the plating drags — the climax collapses. I am training every cook to handle this course personally before they own it. Beto is the only one I will let plate it during opening week. After that we cross-train.

The course will live on the menu through fall. By winter we will probably swap to duck. The lamb course will return in spring. It is part of the year.

47 versions. Worth every one.

— Aiya
