# Press Strategy

**Status:** Active
**Date:** 2026-05-22

How we think about press for Lark. Quiet but deliberate.

## The principle

We do not chase press. We do not refuse it. We make a restaurant that's worth writing about and let writers write what they write.

That said, we are not naive. Press matters. Some press matters more than others.

## What we want from press

- Reviews in publications our target diners read
- Mentions in local lists ("best new restaurants in Portland")
- Eventual national coverage (Eater, NYT, James Beard nominations)
- Honest reflection of what we are

## What we don't want

- A premature review before we are ready
- A puff piece that misses what we're actually doing
- A bait-and-switch where the review focuses on me as a "story" rather than the food
- "Female Asian chef" framing that I find reductive

## The publications we care about (in order)

1. Eater Portland — local taste-makers
2. The Oregonian — broad reach in Portland
3. Willamette Week — local culture
4. Portland Monthly — long-form, considered
5. Eater national — bigger reach
6. New York Times (Bittman, Frank Bruni-style review writers) — when they come, they come
7. Bon Appetit, Food & Wine — magazines with national reach
8. James Beard Foundation — for awards consideration

We focus the first 6 months on the first 5.

## How we time press visits

- No press visits in soft opening (we will not be ready)
- Press preview week (early July) for local writers who want a sense of what's coming
- Open to reviews starting 6 weeks after July 12 (late August)
- Major reviewers usually visit 3-5 times before writing; we honor anonymity

## How we know a reviewer is in

- We don't. That's the point.
- Caro might recognize someone from a photo (a few are easy to spot)
- We treat every table the same. The discipline matters.

## What I will not do

- Send press an "off-menu" extra
- Comp press
- Try to get a review before we're ready
- Pay for placement (this happens; we won't)
- Court Instagram influencers in a way that compromises the room

## What I will do

- Respond to press inquiries within 48 hours
- Provide good photographs of the food when asked
- Arrange a quick chef interview when relevant
- Be honest in interviews (no PR-speak)

## My PR person (or lack thereof)

I do not have a PR firm. I considered hiring one (recommended by Diane's network) but decided against for year 1.

Reasoning:
- The story is the food; PR doesn't help with that
- I can manage 4-5 press inquiries a month myself
- The PR fees ($3,500/month) are better spent on the team
- I prefer direct relationships with writers

I may revisit in year 2 if national press emerges.

## The pitches I would write

For a writer doing a Pacific NW story:
"Lark is a 28-seat tasting menu restaurant in inner SE Portland by a chef who came up at Atomix and Single Thread and grew up between Seattle and Tokyo. The food is Pacific NW seafood and produce, treated with Japanese precision and unfussy heart."

For a writer doing a Portland scene story:
"Aiya Tanaka-Brown is opening Lark in July 2026 — a tasting menu spot built around what's actually in Oregon this week, not a fantasy of what should be."

For a writer doing a chef profile:
"Aiya cooked at Atelier Crenn (SF), Single Thread (Healdsburg), and Atomix (NYC) over 12 years before settling in Portland with her partner Mateo, a sommelier, to build Lark."

I don't pitch these. I keep them ready in case I need them.

## Social media

I do not run Lark's Instagram personally. Lila does, with my review.

- One post per week during pre-opening
- Two posts per week post-opening
- Photos of food (rarely of me; sometimes of the team)
- No behind-the-scenes drama
- No "look at our cool space" content
- No video content (the food is not video food)

We do not pay for posts or boosts.

## The negative review I will eventually get

A bad review will come. The math says so. When it does:

- I will read it
- I will think about whether it's right
- If it's right (in part), I will adjust
- If it's wrong, I will not respond publicly
- The team will not respond publicly
- We will move on

I have seen chefs publicly fight reviews. It always looks bad. Every time.

## Personal note

The press matters in measurable ways and in immeasurable ways. The reservation flow goes up after a good review. The team's morale goes up after recognition. The investors are happy.

But the press is not the goal. The food and the team are the goal. Press follows. If you chase it, you lose.

I am holding to that.
