# Instagram Strategy

**Status:** Active
**Date:** 2026-05-15
**Owner:** Lila Chen

How we run Lark's Instagram. Lila owns it.

## Account

@lark.pdx — handle live as of May 2026.

We use Instagram as our primary social presence. No TikTok. No Twitter/X. No Facebook. Threads possibly later but not a priority.

## Posting cadence

Pre-opening: 1-2 posts per week
Post-opening: 2-3 posts per week
Stories: more frequent (3-5 per week), less curated

We do not post daily. The feed is curated.

## What we post

### Food (60% of posts)
- A dish, photographed by Jamie or by Lila on iPhone
- Clean composition, natural light
- Minimal caption: name of dish, sometimes a sourcing note

### Team (15% of posts)
- Cooks at work, FOH at work
- Caro caught in candid moments
- Beto laughing at family meal
- A small portrait series planned

### Sourcing (15% of posts)
- A photo from Patrick's mushroom haul
- Anderson's lamb arriving
- Helen's vegetables on prep day
- This is content the diners love

### Process (10% of posts)
- The pickle pantry
- A sauce being made
- Bread coming out of the oven
- Less curated; more authentic

## What we don't post

- Selfies of me
- Behind-the-scenes drama
- Reactions to reviews (positive or negative)
- Competitor comparisons
- Trends (we don't chase them)
- Customer photos without permission
- Diners' faces ever

## Tone

Lila has voice notes from me:

"Treat each post like a small letter to a friend who's curious about what we're doing."

"No food puns. No 'foodie' language. No 'umami bomb' or 'flavor explosion.'"

"It's OK to be quiet."

"When in doubt, say less."

## Hashtags

Minimal use. We are not trying to be discoverable through hashtags.

Standard set:
#larkpdx
#portlandfood
#tastingmenu

Optional:
#oregonfish (when relevant)
#oregonproduce (when relevant)
#pdxchef (rare)

## Stories

Less curated than the feed. Used for:
- Daily menu sheet (sometimes)
- Family meal photos
- Suppliers in action
- A glimpse of service prep

Stories don't have to be perfect. They have to feel like a real day.

## What we do on review nights

Nothing.

Reviews come out. We do not post about them. We do not respond. We do not share them.

If a diner shares a great review on their own, we may like it. We don't repost.

This is unusual. Most restaurants thirst for the review attention. I think it's gauche.

## Engagement

We respond to comments occasionally, especially questions.
We do not run giveaways or contests.
We do not collaborate with food influencers.

If a food writer or local figure follows us, we follow back if they make sense.

## Direct messages

Caro and Lila both have access. Inquiries are routed:
- Reservation questions → "please use our reservation link"
- Press inquiries → caro@lark-pdx.com
- Sourcing/business → me directly

Response time: 24 hours during work days.

## What I think the goal of social is

To give a small window into the work. Not to convert. Not to grow followers. Not to be famous.

If someone wants to come, they will find us through the meal first, then check Instagram, and see that it matches.

## Metric I care about

Not follower count. Not likes. Not reach.

The metric: do our reservation customers say "I saw something on Instagram and wanted to come"? If yes, the Instagram is doing its job.

## What I will probably regret in 6 months

Saying no to TikTok. The platform's value for restaurant content is real, especially for younger diners. We may relent. Lila is open.

## Personal note

Social media is the thing I am most uncomfortable with in modern restaurant ownership. I don't post anything personal anywhere. I find performing for a feed exhausting.

But I respect what Lila brings. She has a good eye. She doesn't over-curate. The account is going to feel like Lark, not like a Lark marketing arm.

The line: it should be a window, not a billboard.
