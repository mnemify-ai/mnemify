# Eater Interview Prep

**Status:** Interview scheduled June 28
**Date:** 2026-05-26

Notes ahead of my Eater Portland interview with Karen Brooks (editor).

## What I know about Karen

- Editor at Eater Portland since 2019
- Previously food critic at Portland Monthly
- Knowledgeable about Pacific NW restaurant scene
- Honest in her reviews; doesn't soft-pedal
- Came to a private dinner I cooked in December; she liked it

## What she has asked for in advance

- 45-minute interview at the restaurant
- A taste of one course (just to write about texture; not a meal)
- Photo of the space (no portrait; she'll arrange separately)
- Backstory on the team

## What I expect to be asked

### About me
- Path from Seattle to Atelier Crenn to Single Thread to Atomix to Portland
- Why Portland (not SF, not NYC)
- Mother's influence and Japanese discipline
- Father's influence and the Black American photographer parentage
- Why now (age 38, after years of training)

### About the food
- The menu shape (12 courses, $185)
- The Pacific NW + Japanese framing (and why this doesn't feel forced)
- The relationship with suppliers (Patrick, Anderson, etc.)
- The signature courses (smoked trout, lamb, dessert)
- The seasonal turn (fall menu in September)

### About the business
- Diane Park as lead investor
- Year 1 financial expectations (broad, not specific)
- Whether I plan to expand (probably no in year 2)
- The 7-year lease

### About me personally
- Mateo as partner in life and work
- What I do when I'm not cooking (reading, hiking)
- What I think of the Portland scene (positive without being sycophantic)

### The hard questions she might ask
- "Why are tasting menus alive in 2026?" (good question; I have an answer)
- "Why do you charge $185?" (price defense)
- "What's the difference between Lark and the dozen other 'farm-to-table' Pacific NW places?" (I have a strong answer here; the distinction is the specificity and the technical training)
- "Is the 'female Asian chef' framing reductive?" (I will name this and reframe)

## What I want to say (key messages)

1. The food is what's here this week. It is a restaurant of place, treated with discipline.
2. The team is the work. Beto, Caro, Mateo, Lila — they are the restaurant as much as I am.
3. We are not trying to be Michelin. We are trying to be exactly Lark.
4. The price is honest. The menu is the meal you are getting.
5. The suppliers are named because they should be. Patrick is not "a local forager"; he's Patrick.
6. The Japanese influence is a discipline, not a theme. We are not a Japanese restaurant. We use Japanese techniques because they make Pacific NW ingredients better.

## What I will not say

- Anything negative about another restaurant in Portland
- Anything that sounds like comparing myself to bigger-name chefs
- Anything overly philosophical (Karen will cut it)
- Anything about a specific other chef being my "mentor" who might be offended I named them
- Anything about my mother that feels like leveraging her story

## Quotes I have ready (in case she wants them)

"Portland has the best produce on the West Coast and a quietly serious dining culture. It's home now."

"Every cook here is named in our press release and on the wall in the kitchen. The team is the meal."

"We don't have a secret. We have a menu and we cook it well."

"I came up at Atomix. I learned discipline there. I came back to Pacific NW ingredients with that training. That's the formula. It's not complicated."

(I will not deliver these as canned quotes. I will say them naturally when the moment fits.)

## What I won't show her

- The financials
- The investor deck
- Anything about Diane that she hasn't already said publicly
- The opening menu in full (we have not announced courses yet; she will see a few during her preview meal)

## After the interview

Karen will draft. The piece will probably run mid-July. She will fact-check before publishing. I will respond to fact-checks within 24h.

I do not get to read the piece before it runs. I do not request to. This is journalism.

## What I am nervous about

That the piece will frame me as "a story" rather than as a chef. The "Hokkaido mother, Black American father, chef who came back to Portland" narrative is easy to write and hard to escape.

I will try to redirect toward the food and the team whenever I sense the framing tipping.

## Personal note

This is my first press interview as a chef-owner. Every chef I respect has done these interviews and most have stories of being misframed.

The best advice I got was from Daniel Boulud (briefly, at a benefit dinner in NYC): "Talk about the food. If you talk about the food, the writer will write about the food. If you talk about yourself, the writer will write about you. Choose."

I am choosing to talk about the food.
