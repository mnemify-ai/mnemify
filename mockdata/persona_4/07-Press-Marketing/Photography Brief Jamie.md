# Photography Brief — Jamie Wright

**Status:** Shot list approved; first session May 30
**Date:** 2026-05-18
**Photographer:** Jamie Wright

Brief for the opening photo set. Jamie shoots all our opening press images plus the launch Instagram set.

## The two shoots

### Shoot 1: Food (May 30, full day)
- 8 dishes from the opening menu (the ones that are visually strong)
- Plus 2 additional shots: the bread course, the cheese course
- Two angles per dish (overhead, 3/4)

### Shoot 2: Space and team (June 6, half day)
- Wide shots of the space (4)
- Counter perspective (2)
- The kitchen during prep (3)
- The team during family meal (3)
- One portrait of me (insisted on by Jamie; I am uncomfortable but will allow)

## What I want in the food photos

- Plate fills the frame at the right scale
- Plate on the actual surface it sits on at Lark (raw wood counter or stone slab; not a stylized prop)
- Natural light only (Jamie has a corner of the kitchen near the windows with good light at 11am)
- Color tones that match the brand palette (charcoal, cream, moss, with the burnt orange accent appearing in some dishes)
- The plate looking like food, not styled food
- Drips and small imperfections are fine

## What I don't want

- "Food porn" tight crops
- Vertical-format square crops (we shoot wide, we can crop)
- Steam tricks
- Misting and over-styled garnishes
- Heroic backlit drama
- A photographer's signature aesthetic overpowering the food

## What I want in the space photos

- The room feeling warm and small
- The counter visible
- The kitchen barely visible behind (we are not a "chef's table" theater)
- The light feeling like 5pm when service is about to start
- No people in the wide shots
- People in the medium shots (cooks at prep, team at family meal)

## What I want in the team photos

- Each person at their actual station
- Caught in actual work, not posed
- No forced smiles
- Diversity of the team represented honestly

## What Jamie said

Jamie's feedback on my brief:

"I love how clear you are about what you don't want. Almost all my food clients give vague feedback. You're being specific. That makes my job easier."

"I will push back on one thing: you need at least one portrait of yourself. Press will ask for it. The chef-as-image matters whether you like it or not. Let me make it look like you, not like a 'chef portrait.'"

I agreed.

## The portrait

Jamie's approach for the portrait:
- Black-and-white, medium format film
- In the kitchen, at my actual station (the pass)
- Wearing a clean kitchen coat with no styling
- Not smiling, not glowering
- Looking at the camera

She wants to shoot at the end of family meal when I am actually working. I am uncomfortable with that. We agreed to shoot in the empty kitchen, but in my actual clothes.

## Output

- 60-80 final images
- Delivered in two formats: print quality (300 dpi) and web (1500px max)
- Delivered as flat color JPEGs, no styling presets
- Color profile sRGB

## Licensing

- I own the photos for restaurant use
- Jamie retains licensing for her portfolio and selected editorial use
- Press use requires my approval (we won't say no, but we want to know)

## Cost

- Total: $4,800 for both shoots and full delivery
- Below market for her work (she's done work for Bon Appetit and Saveur)
- She gave us a discount because she loves the concept

## What I plan to use the photos for

- Press kit (provided to writers on request)
- Newsletter (sparingly)
- Website (one photo only, the wordmark, not food)
- Instagram (one new dish photo every other week)
- Future cookbook or media (if we go there)

The photos are not for marketing. They are for accuracy.

## Personal note

I have looked at food photography my whole career and almost all of it is overstyled. The plate looks better in the photograph than it does on the table. That is dishonest.

Jamie's approach: the plate looks how it looks. The light is honest. The frame respects the food.

This is rare. I am lucky to have found her.
