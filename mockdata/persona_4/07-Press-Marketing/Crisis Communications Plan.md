# Crisis Communications Plan

**Status:** Draft
**Date:** 2026-05-23

What we do if something bad happens. Borrowing from Diane's playbook.

## The principle

If something goes wrong, we own it quickly, honestly, and quietly. We do not avoid. We do not spin.

The PR theory: most crises die because the restaurant handles them well. The crises that linger are the ones the restaurant tried to hide.

## The categories of crisis

### Food safety incident
A diner gets sick and attributes it to us. Possible food poisoning, allergic reaction, foreign object.

Protocol:
1. Caro contacts diner within 24h; expresses concern; offers cover of medical costs
2. Kitchen reviews that night's prep, holding temps, supplier logs
3. We notify the broker (Wendy)
4. If a public claim is made, we acknowledge and respond briefly: "We are taking this seriously, reviewing our processes, and in contact with the diner. We will not comment further until the review is complete."
5. We do not deny without facts
6. Internal review completed within 5 days
7. If we are at fault, we communicate corrective action
8. If we are not, we say so with evidence

### Staff misconduct
A staff member behaves badly toward a diner or another staff member.

Protocol:
1. Caro investigates within 24h
2. Staff member is suspended pending review if serious
3. If misconduct confirmed, action taken (warning to firing)
4. If a diner posts about it, we respond: "We are aware. We have taken action. Our standards are X. We will not detail private personnel matters."
5. We do not name staff members publicly even if they were terminated

### Online review storm
A wave of bad reviews following a single incident or perceived slight.

Protocol:
1. Read the reviews
2. Identify whether the issue is real (and what we can fix) vs. coordinated
3. If real: address quietly in operations; do not respond publicly to each review
4. If a single account is leading the charge: do not engage
5. We do not respond to individual reviews ever (positive or negative)
6. We do not solicit reviews to "balance" negativity

### Press piece we disagree with
A reviewer writes something inaccurate or framed negatively.

Protocol:
1. Read the piece carefully
2. Identify factual errors vs. opinion
3. Factual errors: contact the publication with corrections; ask for amendment
4. Opinion we disagree with: do not respond publicly
5. We do not write angry letters to the editor
6. We do not subtweet the reviewer
7. We do not encourage our diners to defend us

### Internal team conflict that becomes public
A staff member leaves and tells their story publicly.

Protocol:
1. Internally, we know what happened
2. We do not respond publicly to characterizations of internal matters
3. If the staff member's account is materially false and threatens the business, we may issue a single statement
4. We do not respond to social media campaigns against us by former staff
5. We do not encourage current staff to defend us publicly

### Health code violation
We have a real or perceived health code violation publicized.

Protocol:
1. Whatever the violation was, we fix it within 24h
2. We respond publicly with: "We were cited for X. We have addressed it. We hold ourselves to the highest standards and are reviewing our processes to ensure this does not recur."
3. We do not minimize or contextualize
4. We invite the inspector back
5. We document the fix

### Catastrophic kitchen incident
Fire, flood, equipment failure that affects service.

Protocol:
1. Safety of staff and any present diners first
2. Notify Caro who notifies the team
3. Notify the broker
4. Email all upcoming reservations within 6 hours
5. Public statement: "We are closed for X days due to a kitchen incident. All reservations during this period are being rescheduled or refunded. We will share an update when we reopen."
6. No additional detail until we have a clear timeline

### A diner has a medical emergency at the restaurant
Heart attack, severe allergic reaction, fainting.

Protocol:
1. Call 911 immediately
2. Staff trained in basic first aid; AED on-site
3. Caro coordinates with EMS
4. We clear adjacent tables discreetly if needed
5. We do not bill the table; we may comp the table next to them if disrupted
6. We do not publicize; we do not discuss with media

## Spokesperson

In a crisis, the spokesperson is me. Not Caro. Not Diane. Not a PR firm.

I speak briefly, with prepared language, and only when needed.

Diane may speak as an investor if asked but defers to me.

Beto, Lila, the kitchen team, and the FOH do not speak to press during a crisis.

## What we do internally during a crisis

- Daily 10-minute check-in with senior team
- Maintain morale; the team needs to know we are handling it
- Do not blame staff publicly even when the issue was a staff mistake
- The team is told what is happening so they do not learn from press

## What I think the worst-case scenario is

The worst-case is a serious foodborne illness incident that lands a diner in the hospital with permanent harm.

This is the scenario I plan most carefully for. Our prep protocols, holding temps, supplier vetting, and allergen management are all aimed at preventing it. The insurance covers if it happens. The reputation cost would be severe.

If it happens, I will be on the phone with the diner's family the day of. I will visit the hospital. I will own it.

## Personal note

Diane gave me a story about a restaurant she ran 15 years ago. A diner had a severe allergic reaction to a peanut trace in a dessert. The dessert was supposed to be peanut-free. The kitchen had cross-contaminated.

Diane went to the hospital that night. She sat with the family for hours. She covered all medical costs and gave them a year of free dining. She fired no one (the cook had made an honest mistake; the protocol had failed, not the person).

The family became friends. The story never became press.

Diane's principle: handle it like a human, not like a PR target.

I want to handle every crisis that way.
