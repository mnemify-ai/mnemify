# Newsletter Plan

**Status:** Drafted; first send June 1
**Date:** 2026-05-20

An email newsletter from Lark. Quarterly. Quiet.

## The premise

A short email four times a year that goes to people who want to know what we're cooking.

Not a marketing channel. A communication with the people who care.

## The cadence

- Pre-opening: one announcement (June 12)
- Quarterly thereafter: end of October (fall), early February (winter), April (spring), July (summer)

Four sends a year. That's it.

## The list

- Anyone who opts in via the website
- Reservation customers who opt in (default opt-out; this matters)
- People I have personally met at private dinners and asked

Pre-opening list: ~340 people (largely from private dinner attendees over the past 3 years, plus an industry network).

I expect it to grow to ~1,200 by year 2.

## What goes in a newsletter

1. A short opening paragraph (what's happening in the kitchen)
2. The current menu (titled list of courses, no descriptions)
3. One supplier highlight (Patrick this issue, Anderson next)
4. A short note from Mateo on what he's pouring
5. Practical information (reservation news, schedule changes)
6. Sign-off

Length: 600-800 words total. Reading time under 4 minutes.

## What I won't put in a newsletter

- Photos other than maybe one
- Marketing graphics
- Links to social media
- Reviews or press mentions
- "Buy something" prompts
- Pop-ups

The newsletter is plain text. It looks like a letter from a friend.

## Voice

Same voice as the brand. Direct, warm, specific.

Sample paragraph:

"This week we got the first sturgeon of the season from Captain Lou in Astoria. The fish is enormous. We're curing a small section for our cold course and saving the rest for staff. The menu changes Tuesday."

(That's a hypothetical — sturgeon supply has been our actual issue. The sentence structure is what I'm modeling.)

## Email service

I use Buttondown. Simple, plain-text-friendly, costs $9/month.

I will not use Mailchimp. The dashboards and "engagement metrics" make me write toward those numbers. Buttondown does less, on purpose.

## Open rate goals

I don't have any.

Mateo wants to track open rate. I think tracking distorts the writing. We compromise: Buttondown does basic tracking. I do not look at the dashboard.

## Unsubscribes

Easy. Footer link to unsubscribe. We respect every unsubscribe immediately.

If someone unsubscribes, they don't get re-added by future reservations. The system handles this.

## The first send (June 1)

Subject line: "Opening July 12"

Body: 600 words. The story of why we are opening Lark, the menu shape, the team, the reservations link.

Plain text. One photo (the wordmark only).

## Personal note

I think email newsletters are the last honest channel of communication with diners. Instagram is performance. Email is direct.

My favorite restaurant newsletters are quiet, infrequent, and specific. (Coi's old newsletter under Daniel Patterson was a model.) I want to do that.

If we end up with 1,200 readers in year 2 who each read every issue carefully, that is a much more valuable audience than 50,000 followers who scroll past us. The newsletter is for the careful readers.
