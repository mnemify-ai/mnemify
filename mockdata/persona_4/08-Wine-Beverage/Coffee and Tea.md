# Coffee and Tea

**Status:** Set
**Date:** 2026-05-21

The coffee and tea program. Often an afterthought at tasting menu restaurants. We are giving it real thought.

## The premise

The diner spends $185+ on a meal. They deserve a real cup of coffee or tea at the end. Not a mediocre brew that breaks the spell.

## Coffee

### Service
French press at the table, brewed to order. 16 oz for two people. Pour-over for solo diners (8 oz).

We are not running an espresso machine. The maintenance, the barista training, the variance — not worth it for our service volume.

### Bean
Sweet Bloom (a roaster in Lakewood, CO, but available in Portland). Their seasonal single-origin blend.

We rotate beans quarterly. Current: an Ethiopian Yirgacheffe (light, citric, perfect after dessert).

We are not using a Portland roaster. This is intentional. Portland has many great roasters and choosing one is political. We chose a coast-affiliated roaster (Sweet Bloom is in Colorado, technically) to sidestep.

### Cost
$6 per service. We pay $24/lb for the beans; each service uses ~30g of coffee.

### Decaf
A Swiss water-process decaf from Heart Coffee Roasters. Real decaf, not bottom-shelf.

### Brewing
Brewed in the kitchen by a designated cook on dessert service. Same grind, same dose, same water temp.

I bought a Marco Über boiler ($1,800) so the water is always at 200°F. The difference is meaningful.

## Tea

### Service
Loose-leaf, brewed at the table. Small tea pot, pre-warmed, with a 4-minute timer the server flips.

### The list (10 teas)

#### Green tea
- Gyokuro (Uji, Kyoto)
- Sencha (Kagoshima)
- Hojicha (roasted Sencha)

#### Black tea
- Yunnan Gold (Yunnan, China)
- Keemun (Anhui, China)
- Assam (single estate, Tezpur)

#### Oolong
- Tieguanyin (Anxi)
- Da Hong Pao (Wuyi)

#### Herbal/Special
- Lapsang Souchong (smoked black, lovely after the lamb course)
- Roasted Rice Genmaicha (Japan, accessible)

That's it. 10 references.

### Sourcing
- Japanese teas through Mutual Trading (same channel as our sake)
- Chinese teas through Smith Teamaker (Portland)
- Indian through specialty importer

### Service temperature
Each tea has a specific water temperature. The kitchen has a multi-temp kettle.

- Gyokuro: 140°F
- Sencha: 160°F
- Hojicha: 200°F
- Black tea: 200°F
- Oolong: 195°F

Most American restaurants serve all tea at 212°F. This destroys most tea. We will not.

## Service flow

After the dessert course:
- Server asks: "coffee or tea?"
- Server places the order with the kitchen
- Coffee or tea arrives within 4 minutes
- For tea: timer is started at the table
- Refills offered once

## Cost

Coffee and tea revenue is modest. We charge $5-8 per service. The cost per service is ~$1.50.

But coffee and tea are not profit drivers. They are quality drivers. The diner who has a beautiful Yirgacheffe French press at the end of dinner remembers Lark differently than one who had a mediocre brew.

## What we don't do

- Espresso (no machine)
- Boba/bubble tea (gimmick)
- Iced coffee or iced tea after a 12-course meal (we do not serve these unless requested)
- Sweetener at the table (sugar is available; we don't push)

## What we will eventually do

- Source a small batch of a specific roast just for us (Sweet Bloom is open to this)
- Develop a tea pairing for an after-dinner add-on (currently in concept)
- Brew Japanese matcha in season (October-March)

## Personal note

My mother is particular about tea. She would walk into Lark and look at our list and probably approve. She would notice we serve gyokuro at 140°F, not boiling. She would notice the kettle.

That matters to me.

For coffee: I am not a coffee specialist. I love a good cup. I worked with a Portland coffee mentor (Aly Garcia, Heart roaster) to refine our program. She tasted our French press setup and approved.

A meal that ends with bad coffee is a meal that breaks the spell. We are not letting it.
