# Wine Program Philosophy

**Status:** Set by Mateo
**Date:** 2026-05-12
**Author:** Mateo (with my approval)

The principles behind the wine program at Lark.

## The premise

Wine should support the food, not perform around it. The pairing should make the diner taste the food more clearly, not display the sommelier's cleverness.

This is Mateo's principle. I agree with it completely.

## The shape of the program

- A by-the-glass list of 12-14 wines + sake + beer
- A 12-pour tasting pairing
- A 6-pour curated pairing
- A 3-pour starter pairing
- A full bottle list of ~80 references

Total wine inventory at steady state: ~$35K.

We are intentionally small. A larger list would mean dead inventory and slower service.

## The regions we focus on

Primary regions (60% of the list):
- Oregon (Willamette Valley pinot noir, Eola-Amity chardonnay, Mt. Hood Riesling)
- Burgundy (white and red, classical anchor)
- Japan (sake, primarily, plus a few Japanese wines from Yamanashi)

Secondary regions (30%):
- Loire (Sancerre, Chenin, Cabernet Franc)
- Rhône (Northern only; Syrah)
- Champagne (a small selection for celebration)
- German Riesling (small selection)

Tertiary regions (10%):
- Sherry (small selection)
- Madeira (cheese course)
- Pedro Ximénez (dessert)

We do not have:
- Bordeaux beyond two reference bottles
- California beyond two reference bottles
- New World blockbusters
- "Natural wine" focus (we have some natural wines but it's not our identity)
- Champagne replicas

## Why these choices

Mateo's principle: pair what grows where. Pacific NW food is suited to cool-climate, mineral-driven, less oaky wines. That points to:
- Oregon (local, brilliant)
- Burgundy (the classical model for cool-climate)
- Loire (for crisp whites and elegant reds)
- Japan (sake, which is the natural pairing for the Japanese-influenced courses)

Big extracted wines from California or Bordeaux do not belong on this program. They would dominate the food.

## Pairing approach

Mateo pairs by:
1. Tasting the dish
2. Identifying what the wine should do (cleanse, support, complement, contrast)
3. Pulling 3-4 candidates from the cellar
4. Testing
5. Choosing
6. Documenting

He builds the pairings as the menu changes. The pairing for the lamb course (Syrah from Côte-Rôtie) was tested against 9 alternatives.

## What we pour

Pours are 3 oz for the 12-pour pairing. That's 36 oz of wine over a meal = 2.4 bottles per pairing. Sounds like a lot. Spread over 2.5 hours, it's enjoyable, not punishing.

The 6-pour is 5 oz pours = 30 oz total.

The 3-pour is 6 oz pours = 18 oz total.

We do not stretch pours. Mateo measures.

## Stems

Three glasses per setting:
- Universal red/white (Zalto or Riedel Vinum)
- Champagne flute (rarely used)
- Specialty glass for the lamb (a Burgundy bowl)

Sake gets specific cups (matched to the sake style).

We invested $9K in glassware. Will replenish at ~$200/month from breakage.

## The bottle list

80 references. Hand-selected. No filler.

Bottles are roughly:
- 25 Oregon
- 20 Burgundy
- 10 Japan (sake)
- 8 Loire
- 5 Rhône
- 5 Champagne
- 7 other (sherry, German, Italian, specific producers)

Mateo can speak to every bottle. So can I, after a year of conversation.

## What's not on the bottle list

- Wines we cannot stand behind
- Wines that drive a high markup but don't serve the food
- Wines from producers we have ethical issues with
- "Power producers" that are just brand

This means our list is shorter than a comparable restaurant. We are OK with that.

## Wine cost

Target wine cost: 32% of wine revenue.

This is achievable because:
- Our bottle list is small and we know what sells
- We pour the same bottles in the by-the-glass program
- We do not have dead inventory
- We do not over-order

## What I love about working with Mateo on this

Mateo is more patient than I am about wine. He will spend three hours on one course pairing. He tastes 40 wines a week. He is constantly updating his palate.

I trust him completely with this. He has built a program I am proud of.

## Personal note

Mateo and I argue about wine more than about anything else. We have different ideas about pairing structure. He is right more often than I am.

The wine program is more his than mine. I am OK with that. It is one of the reasons he is here.
