# Beer and Cider List

**Status:** Locked
**Date:** 2026-05-20

The beer and cider offering. Small, specific, Pacific NW-focused.

## The list

### Beer (5 references)

1. **Breakside Pilsner** (Portland) — clean, low ABV (4.8%). The everyday pour.
2. **Upright Five** (Portland) — saison, dry, food-friendly.
3. **Hair of the Dog Adam** (Portland) — old ale, complex, for the cheese course.
4. **Asahi Super Dry** (Japan) — clean Japanese lager.
5. **Wayfinder Hell** (Portland) — German-style helles, beautiful with the bread course.

### Cider (3 references)

1. **2 Towns Brightcider** (Corvallis) — semi-dry, apple-forward.
2. **Reverend Nat's Hallelujah Hopricot** (Portland) — hopped apricot cider, distinctive.
3. **Eden Heirloom Blend** (Vermont, but distinctive enough to import) — barrel-aged, for slow drinking.

Eight total. That's it.

## Why this list

Each entry has a reason:
- Breakside Pilsner: someone wants a beer, this is the right pour
- Upright: dry saison pairs with the mushroom course better than most wines
- Hair of the Dog: actually substitutes for a wine pairing with the cheese course
- Asahi: a Japanese touch; diners who like Japanese beer get it
- Wayfinder: the bread course
- Brightcider: someone wants something light and fruity
- Reverend Nat's: someone wants something interesting and adventurous
- Eden: the deep cider for cider geeks

## Pricing

- Beer: $8-12 per pour
- Cider: $9-14 per pour

These are mid-range Portland prices. We are not cheap. We are not gouging.

## Pairings

Beer/cider does not appear in our default pairing program. But for diners who prefer beer or cider, Mateo can construct a substitute pairing.

Example beer/cider pairing for the 6-pour:
- Asahi (opening bite)
- Wayfinder Hell (bread course)
- Upright Five (mushroom course)
- Reverend Nat's Hallelujah Hopricot (cheese course)
- Eden cider (pre-dessert)
- Hair of the Dog Adam (dessert)

We offer this on request. It is not on the standard menu.

## What we don't have

- Hazy IPAs (Portland staple, but they don't suit our food)
- Big imperial stouts
- Sour beers (the food has plenty of acid already)
- Macro lagers (Bud, Coors, etc.)
- Hard seltzer

We are not the place for those. If someone really wants a hazy IPA, we tell them where to find it.

## Sourcing

- Portland breweries directly (Breakside, Upright, Hair of the Dog, Wayfinder)
- Distributor (Maletis Beverage) for Asahi and Eden
- Reverend Nat's directly
- 2 Towns through their distributor

We rotate kegs weekly; bottles by the case as needed.

## Storage

- Kegerator for 3 kegs (Breakside, Wayfinder, Asahi)
- Bottle inventory in a small cellar fridge
- Roughly 2 weeks of inventory at any time

## What I think about beer and cider

I drink beer occasionally. Mateo doesn't really drink beer. I bought into the program because Beto (sous chef) is a beer person and pushed for a serious list.

Beto curated this list. I trust him.

He drinks Breakside Pilsner most nights of his life. The fact that it's on our menu makes him a small amount happier every shift.

## Personal note

A great beer program is rarer in fine dining than it should be. The default approach is "we have a generic IPA and a Pilsner."

Beto wanted to do better. I am glad we did.

The Hair of the Dog Adam at the cheese course is the move I am most proud of in the beer program. Pairs better than any wine I have tried with our specific cheese plate.

Sometimes the right pairing is not wine.
