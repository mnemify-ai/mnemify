# Cocktail Program

**Status:** Drafted; testing in soft opening
**Date:** 2026-05-19

The cocktail offering at Lark. Small, specific, optional.

## The premise

Cocktails are not the center of the program. They are an alternative for the diner who doesn't want wine, plus an aperitif option before the meal.

We will not be a "cocktail bar with food." We are a tasting menu restaurant that pours cocktails.

## The list

7 cocktails. Rotating roughly quarterly.

### Current list (May 2026 — opening)

1. **Cedar Negroni** — Cedar-infused gin, Cocchi Americano, Aperol. $16
2. **Yuzu Tonic** — gin, yuzu shrub, Fever-Tree tonic. $14
3. **Sansho Old Fashioned** — bourbon, sansho-pepper-infused demerara, orange. $16
4. **Sake Smash** — Junmai sake, shiso, lime, cucumber, dry sparkling. $14
5. **Pacific Highball** — Japanese whisky (Toki), citrus, soda. $15
6. **Plum and Smoke** — mezcal, ume plum, lapsang souchong tea. $17
7. **The Nightcap** — Madeira, walnut, orange peel. $14

Plus a "house spirit-free" option: **Roasted Rice and Citrus** — toasted rice tea, yuzu, soda. $9

## Why this list

Each cocktail has a relationship to the menu:
- Cedar (from the bread course's smoked seaweed)
- Yuzu (used in the soup)
- Sansho (used in the cold trout course)
- Shiso (in the pre-dessert)
- Ume plum (in the lamb course)
- Walnut (in the cheese course)

The cocktails extend the flavor world of the menu rather than competing with it.

## Build philosophy

- Stirred or shaken simply
- No more than 5 ingredients per cocktail
- No syrups we wouldn't make ourselves
- No "modern" gimmicks (no foams, no spheres, no rotary evaporator unless we have time and reason)

Our bar is intentionally simple. One bartender per service (the FOH covers it when needed).

## Glassware

- Standard rocks for old fashioned and similar
- Coupe for the Negroni and stirred drinks
- Highball for the soda-driven drinks
- Sake cups for the Sake Smash (specifically; we use a wide-mouth ceramic)

We use 6 glass shapes total for the cocktail program. Simple.

## Garnishes

- One twist or one piece of garnish per drink
- No "garden bouquets"
- The garnish is functional (releases oil over the drink) or it's not there

## Ice

- Large rocks for stirred drinks (we order from Cube Custom Ice; $1.80 per piece)
- Crushed for the smash
- No ice for the Highball (chilled glass; Japanese style)

## Non-alcoholic

We have a real non-alcoholic option (the Roasted Rice and Citrus). We are working on 2 more.

The non-alcoholic options will appear on the cocktail menu, not segregated to a "mocktail" sub-menu. The pricing is around half the alcoholic cocktail price.

## What we don't have

- A speed rail of basics for casual drinks (Old Fashioned without the sansho; we will make it on request)
- A massive whiskey selection (we have 4 references)
- A martini list (we make martinis on request; not on the menu)
- An espresso martini (Mateo's hard no)
- Pina coladas
- Mules

We make almost anything on request within reason. The menu lists what we have crafted with intention.

## Cocktail cost

- Average pour cost: 18% of cocktail revenue
- Average sale: $15
- COGS: ~$2.70

This is good for cocktails. The discipline: small list, no waste, simple infusions made in-house.

## Pairings (informally)

Cocktails are not paired to courses in our pairing program. But Mateo will suggest:
- Plum and Smoke before the lamb course
- Yuzu Tonic before the cold courses
- Sansho Old Fashioned for diners who want a single drink to anchor

## Who can order

The cocktail menu is available before and during the meal. Most diners take the wine pairing; some take a cocktail before and switch to wine.

## What I think of cocktails

I am a wine and sake drinker mostly. The cocktail program is more Mateo's pull than mine.

But I respect that cocktails are how many diners enter a fine dining meal. The opening cocktail is the first thing they taste at our place. It should be good.

## Personal note

My cocktail mentor (Lou Vasconcellos, a bartender in NYC) taught me: a good cocktail respects the spirit and respects the drinker. It doesn't perform. It doesn't show off.

Lou would walk into Lark, look at the menu, and probably approve. The Cedar Negroni is for him.
