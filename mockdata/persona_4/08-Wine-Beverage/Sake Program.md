# Sake Program

**Status:** Built; refining
**Date:** 2026-05-17
**Author:** Mateo + me

The sake offering at Lark. The most distinctive part of our beverage program.

## The premise

We pour real sake at temperature, in cups that fit the sake, in pours that respect the sake.

Most American restaurants treat sake as exotic decoration. We treat it as wine — specific producers, specific styles, paired to food.

## The list

12 sakes at any given time:

### Junmai (rice + water + koji)
- Kuroushi Junmai (Wakayama)
- Hidakami Tsubakimichi Junmai (Miyagi)
- Wakatake Onikoroshi Junmai (Shizuoka)

### Junmai Ginjo (more polished)
- Tedorigawa Yamahai (Ishikawa)
- Kamoshibitokuheiji (Aichi)
- Hakkaisan Junmai Ginjo (Niigata)

### Junmai Daiginjo (most polished)
- Tatsuriki Komeno Sasayaki (Hyogo)
- Dassai 39 (Yamaguchi)
- Shichida Junmai Daiginjo (Saga)

### Specialty
- Kikuhime Junmai Kimoto (Ishikawa) — aged, structured
- Hirota Yume Akebono Junmai Genshu (Akita) — undiluted
- Akashi-Tai Plum (Hyogo) — for the dessert course pairing or for non-sake drinkers

## Service

Each sake has a service temperature:
- Kuroushi: chilled (45°F)
- Tedorigawa Yamahai: room temp (60°F)
- Hidakami: slightly chilled (50°F)
- Etc.

We chill in a separate sake fridge. Mateo monitors temps before pour.

## Glassware

We do not use ochoko (the tiny ceramic cups) except for the most casual sake.

For the program:
- Tasting cups: small handblown glass cups (purchased from Hakkomi in Kyoto, $48 each)
- Larger pour glasses: Zalto universal glasses (same as wine)
- Specialty: hot sake cups (only for the one hot pour we do)

## Pours

- Standard pour: 2.5 oz (sake pours are smaller than wine; the abv averages 15% so the actual ethanol is comparable to 3 oz wine)
- Tasting flight: three 1.5 oz pours
- Hot pour: 2.5 oz heated to 110°F

## Pairings

Sake appears in our pairing program at:
- Course 1 (opening bite): Tedorigawa Yamahai
- Course 4 (soup with dashi): Hakkaisan
- Course 11 (pre-dessert): Akashi-Tai Plum (alternative to Sauternes)

That's 3 sake pours in the 12-pour pairing. The other 9 are wines.

## Sourcing

We buy through Mutual Trading Co. (our Japanese importer) and JFC International. Mateo has visited 3 producers in Japan (Tedorigawa in 2023; Tatsuriki and Hakkaisan in 2024).

We do not buy any sake we have not personally vetted.

## What we don't have

- Cheap sake (no nigori for "fun")
- Cold sake to mix with shochu
- "Wine sake" (sake blended with wine concept; gimmick)
- Sake bombs (will refuse to serve)
- Junmai Daiginjo as a base pour (it's expensive; we save it for tasting flights or by-the-glass)

## The flight option

We offer a 3-pour sake-only flight ($35) as an add-on or a substitute for the standard wine pairing.

The flight rotates monthly. Current flight (May):
- Kuroushi Junmai (chilled, dry)
- Tedorigawa Yamahai (room temp, rich)
- Tatsuriki Sasayaki (chilled, delicate)

The flight serves the diner who wants to explore sake without committing to a sake-only meal.

## Education and explanation

When a diner orders sake, the server (briefed by Mateo) explains briefly:
- What category the sake is
- Why we paired it with this course
- What to look for in the taste

We do not lecture. The explanation is 30 seconds, not 3 minutes.

If a diner is interested, Mateo will come to the table for a longer conversation. He has done this 14 times in private dinners. It is one of the most loved things he does.

## Cost

Sake cost is 28% of sake revenue. Slightly better than wine because we pour smaller portions and the absolute prices are tighter.

## Personal note

I grew up drinking sake at family meals (Japanese mother, social Japanese household). Most Americans drink sake wrong. They drink it cold and they drink it fast.

The sake program at Lark is a quiet teaching tool. The diner who has 3 sakes paired to food may understand sake in a different way after the meal than before.

That is the point of any beverage program. Not to impress. To teach.
