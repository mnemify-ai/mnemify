# Baird Family Orchards

**Relationship:** Stone fruit, especially peaches for the dessert course
**Located:** Dayton, OR (Willamette Valley)
**Contact:** Mike Baird
**First met:** 2025-09, recommended by Rosa at Gathering Together
**Delivery cadence:** Twice weekly during peach season; otherwise monthly check-ins
**Status:** Critical for opening dessert course

## Background

Mike Baird is the third generation running the orchard. His grandfather planted the first peach trees in 1947. They now have about 80 acres planted across peaches, apricots, plums, cherries, pears, and apples.

They are known regionally for peaches. Specifically: their late-season Suncrest variety, which is one of the most flavorful peaches I have ever eaten.

## What we order

For opening menu:

- **Suncrest peaches** for the dessert course. Need to order 2-3 cases per week during peak (mid-July to mid-August).
- **Other peaches as supplemental** (Cresthaven, O'Henry varieties earlier in season).
- **Pluots and apricots** for occasional special courses.

For fall menu (planning ahead):

- **Honeycrisp and Pink Lady apples** for the autumn dessert direction Lila is sketching.
- **Quince** for the geoduck vinegar (I've been making my own quince vinegar — they sell me seconds quinces for cheap because they're cosmetic but flavor-perfect).

## Pricing

- Peaches in peak season: $3.50/lb
- Apricots: $4/lb (small production)
- Quince seconds: $1.50/lb (a gift, basically)
- Apples in fall: $1.80/lb

## How we work

Mike texts me every Sunday during peach season with what's ripening and at what stage. I pick up Tuesday and Friday mornings at his farmstand, an hour drive from Portland.

The drive is part of the relationship. I get out to the orchard, walk a few rows with Mike, taste from trees, see what's coming.

## What worries me

Peaches are weather-dependent. A late frost can knock out a whole year's crop. Mike lost 60% of his 2023 peach crop to a frost. Lark's dessert course assumes peaches; if peaches fail, the dessert pivots.

Backup for peaches: pluots and apricots from Baird (lower volume but reliable), or stone fruit from California (much less interesting, last resort).

## What Mike told me on my first visit

We were standing under a peach tree. He picked one, handed it to me, and said: "If a peach is going to be great, it has to be picked at the moment it's ready. Not the day before, not the day after. Right then. Most farms can't do that because they're picking for shelf life. We pick for flavor."

This is exactly the supplier I want for stone fruit. The "picked at peak" promise is real.

## Personal note

Mike's wife Sherry made me lunch on my first orchard visit — a peach salad with their own peaches, prosciutto, and burrata. It was simple and almost perfect. I told her about wanting to write a Lark dessert that captured what her salad did. The shio koji peach we ended up developing is, in some way, an answer to her dish.

## What I want long-term

Baird stays our stone fruit supplier. We extend the relationship to apples in fall, quince and pears in late fall. The dessert course at Lark has Baird fruit as its baseline, year after year, season after season.

When their kids are old enough to take over (currently teenagers), we continue with them.
