# Ron — Skamania Springs Trout

**Relationship:** Trout farmer, primary supplier for trout course
**Located:** Columbia River Gorge, near Skamania
**First met:** 2025-09, on a trip up the Gorge with Mateo
**Delivery cadence:** Weekly, sometimes twice weekly
**Status:** Critical to the trout course

## Background

Ron has been raising rainbow trout in the Gorge for 31 years. His farm is small — about 8 ponds, all cold-spring fed. He doesn't use any commercial feed brand; he makes his own mix and won't fully tell me what's in it. He says it includes "ingredients you'd recognize and a few you wouldn't ask twice about."

He's 64. His daughter Mia helps on weekends but doesn't want to take over.

## What he brings

Rainbow trout, between 8 and 14 ounces dressed weight. Delivered whole on ice, cleaned but not filleted. We get 6-8 fish per delivery.

The fish are extraordinary. Cold-water fish that taste like the water they live in — mineral, clean, slightly green. Nothing like supermarket trout.

## How we work together

Ron texts me Sunday night with what he expects to send Monday. If something's wrong with a pond, he tells me. If a fish is undersized, he tells me. He's honest in a way that's almost startling for a fish supplier.

I pay him in cash. He doesn't take cards. He has a checking account but he prefers cash. Mateo handles delivering the money on the weekly run because Ron likes Mateo.

## The price

$12.50/lb delivered. This is on the high end for trout but reasonable for the quality. A typical fish gets me 5-6 portions of trout course, depending on yield. So one fish runs me roughly $20-25 in food cost, plus dry-cure and smoke time.

## What worries me

Ron is 64. He works the farm himself. If he gets sick or hurt, I have no backup at his quality level.

Also: his cold springs run cold through July. By August the water warms up to 60°F+ and the fish quality drops. This is the seasonality I built the trout course around — the course runs from opening (mid-July) through about late August, then we rotate to a different cold appetizer for fall.

## Backup plans

- **Lummi Island Wild** for Coho salmon (different course, but covers if trout is unavailable)
- **Pacific Pearl Sturgeon** for smoked sturgeon
- A relationship I'm building with **Coffin Butte** in northern Oregon — they raise trout too, but their water is slightly warmer and the fish are less distinctive. Backup, not primary.

## What Ron told me on my first visit

We sat on a bench by his largest pond on a September afternoon. I asked him how he learned to raise trout. He said, "I didn't. I learned to listen to water."

I wrote that down in the notebook in my pocket. I have not stopped thinking about it.

## Personal note

Ron and I have a deal. He saves me the best fish each week. In exchange, every time he comes to Portland (rare, but it happens), I cook him a multi-course meal at the restaurant on the house. He came once in March. I made him the trout course made from his own fish. He cried a little. I cried a little.

This is, I think, the right kind of business relationship. Mutual respect. Acknowledgment of the work each of us does. No false equivalences.

## What I want from him long-term

Ron raises trout for 5 more years, then transitions to something less physically demanding. Mia has her own life and isn't taking over.

Realistic outcome: when Ron stops, I lose this trout. I will not pretend the trout course is sustainable forever. While we have it, we honor it. When we lose it, we evolve.
