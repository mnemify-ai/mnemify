# Bee Local Honey — Tim and Maggie

**Relationship:** Honey for cheese course and dessert
**Located:** Hood River, OR
**Contacts:** Tim and Maggie Sweeney
**First met:** 2025-11 at a Hood River farmer's market
**Delivery cadence:** Monthly
**Status:** Important for cheese course; the right honey makes the dish

## Background

Tim and Maggie keep about 60 hives in the Hood River area. They've been at it 12 years. The honey is single-source — they don't blend with other apiaries' honey. Each season produces a slightly different flavor profile based on what was blooming.

The honey we use is their spring/summer harvest, which has notes of cherry blossom (early), then stone fruit and meadow flowers later.

## What we order

- **Spring/summer honey** for the cheese course (the honeycomb chunk and the drizzle)
- **Buckwheat honey** (their fall harvest, more robust) for occasional staff meal and home use

Total monthly spend: $200-300.

## Pricing

- Spring/summer honey: $28/lb in jar; $42/comb for whole honeycomb (the comb chunk on the cheese plate is from the comb honey)
- Buckwheat: $24/lb

Honey is expensive when it's this good. Worth it for the cheese course.

## How we work

Tim or Maggie deliver monthly when they're in Portland for the farmers market. I order in advance.

For the honeycomb specifically, they set aside a section of their comb harvest for me — about 4 lbs per month. This gives us roughly 80 portions of the cheese course (50g comb per portion).

## What worries me

Bee colonies are fragile. A bad winter or a pesticide drift can wipe out hives. Tim and Maggie lost 12 hives in 2023 to a neighboring farmer's spray. They were furious. The neighboring farmer paid for the bees but the season was set back.

If a similar event happens, our honey supply could be tight for months.

Backup plans: I have a relationship with two other Oregon honey producers (Old Blue Raw Honey, Glory Bee). Both have honey we could use, though neither matches Tim and Maggie's spring profile exactly.

## What Tim told me on my visit

He showed me a hive being worked. He said: "These bees are doing the same work bees have done for forty million years. The honey is just the way they thank us for not killing them."

Maggie laughed at him. I wrote it down.

## Personal note

Maggie sent me a small jar of a specialty honey for my birthday last year — a comb section from a hive that had been working a wild meadowfoam patch. It was extraordinary. They didn't have to do that. It means something that they did.

## What I want long-term

Tim and Maggie's operation stays healthy. We continue using their honey for as long as they're producing.

If they ever scale back or transition out, I'll find another honey source for the cheese course, but the relationship will be missed.
