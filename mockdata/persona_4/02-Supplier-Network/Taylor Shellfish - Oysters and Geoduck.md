# Taylor Shellfish — Oysters and Geoduck

**Relationship:** Primary shellfish supplier
**Located:** Shelton, WA
**First met:** 2025-10 through a colleague at Single Thread
**Delivery cadence:** 2x weekly
**Status:** Critical for opening bite (oyster option) and geoduck course

## Background

Taylor Shellfish is the largest shellfish producer on the West Coast. They've been around since the 1890s. They are not a tiny artisanal operation — they're a serious commercial farm — but they have a focus on sustainability and a reputation for quality that justifies my using them as primary.

Caroline is my contact. She handles chef accounts. She's been there 14 years and knows every chef in the region.

## What I order

- **Kumamoto oysters** for the opening bite (if Option A makes the final menu). Small, sweet, melon-finish.
- **Olympia oysters** for special pours and the half-shell display in the front room.
- **Geoduck** for the geoduck-and-chrysanthemum course (if it makes the menu).
- **Manila clams** for staff meal occasionally.

## Pricing

- Kumamoto oysters: $1.10/each wholesale
- Olympia oysters: $1.40/each (rare; small quantities)
- Geoduck: $32/lb whole; we pay per animal (averages around 3 lbs)

The geoduck is expensive but the cost per portion works out reasonably given the menu pricing. The yield is the concern, not the cost.

## How we work together

Caroline sends me a Monday morning availability email. I order by Tuesday. Wednesday/Friday delivery to the restaurant.

For special orders (specific oyster varieties, special-size geoducks), I email Caroline directly and we figure it out. She's responsive and clear.

## What worries me

- **Geoduck demand** is increasing nationally. Chinese restaurants in the US are willing to pay very high prices. Caroline has been clear with me that prices may rise.
- **Oyster supply** can be affected by algal blooms, water temperatures, harvest closures. Taylor is large enough to weather most disruptions but it does happen.

## Backup plans

- **Hama Hama Oyster Company** as backup for oysters. Smaller, more boutique, slightly different flavor profile but excellent.
- **Blue Pool Shellfish** for Kumamoto specifically — they have a small grow operation and have offered to set aside production for me if Taylor can't deliver.

## Personal note

I am not as close with Taylor as I am with the smaller suppliers. This is a larger commercial relationship and they have hundreds of restaurant clients. Caroline is professional and helpful but we are not friends.

This is fine. Some supplier relationships are friendships, some are vendor relationships. Both are necessary.

## What Caroline told me when I asked about Kumamoto seasonality

"Kumamotos are good year-round but they're best from late November through March, when the water is coldest. In summer they get slightly larger and slightly less sweet."

This influenced my menu design. The opener that uses Kumamoto is intended for fall/winter rotation. For opening summer menu we're going with the fluke + green strawberry option, which doesn't depend on oysters.

If we end up wanting oysters as the opening bite in summer, the Olympia is a better choice — smaller, more concentrated flavor, less seasonality variation.

## What I want long-term

Taylor stays our primary shellfish supplier. We expand the relationship as we add courses or special pours that need shellfish. When we eventually do a once-quarterly "Coast Day" special menu (a long-term plan), we work with Taylor to feature their specific oyster varieties.

## Open issue

The cost of geoduck is going to push our price for the geoduck course high. If we can't justify it at $14 food cost per plate, we drop the dish. I am still working out whether to commit to it for opening.

Caroline knows this is undecided. She has not asked me to commit.
