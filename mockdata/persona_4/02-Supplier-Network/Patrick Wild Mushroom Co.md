# Patrick — Wild Mushroom Co

**Relationship:** Mushroom forager, our primary mushroom supplier
**First met:** 2024 at a James Beard event in Portland; reconnected 2026-02
**Phone:** [redacted]
**Delivery cadence:** 2x weekly; he texts me what he has Sunday and Wednesday mornings
**Status:** Critical to the mushroom course

## Background

Patrick is in his late fifties. Used to be a high school biology teacher in McMinnville. Started foraging seriously in his forties when his wife got sick — he says the woods kept him sane. Wife passed in 2018. He went full-time foraging in 2020.

He works the Coast Range and the Cascades. He has routes he won't tell anyone. He picks by hand, no shortcuts, no commercial harvest. What he brings is what he picked that day.

## What he brings

Depends entirely on the season and the rains.

- **Spring:** Morels (April-May, sometimes into June if cool), oyster mushrooms, early porcini
- **Summer:** Chanterelles (peak in August, but sometimes a July push), lobster mushrooms, hedgehogs
- **Fall:** Matsutake (October-November), porcini, more chanterelles, black trumpets
- **Winter:** Limited — winter oysters, occasional hedgehogs from low-elevation woods

In a good week he brings 12-20 pounds of usable mushroom across 2-4 species. In a bad week he might bring 3-5 pounds of one species, or nothing.

## The economics

Patrick charges fair prices. He's not cheap and he's not gouging. Chanterelles in season are $18-22/lb wholesale to me. Matsutake go for $80-140/lb depending on grade — and he grades honestly, which I appreciate.

I pay him within a week, always. He delivers to the back of the restaurant in his Subaru, in plastic crates lined with paper.

## How we work together

I do not tell him what to bring. He tells me what he has. I build the menu around it.

This is a different relationship than most chef-supplier relationships. Most suppliers want a standing order. Patrick can't deliver a standing order — the forest delivers what it delivers. The menu has to flex to match.

This is why the mushroom course on our menu is written as "tonight's foraged mushrooms" rather than as a specific species. The flexibility is built in.

## What worries me

If Patrick gets injured. If Patrick's truck breaks down at the wrong moment. If a bad year of rain produces a poor mushroom season.

I have backup plans (Mycoterra Farm cultivated; dried stock as ace-in-the-hole) but the course loses its specific magic without Patrick.

## What I'm doing about it

- Cultivating Patrick's apprentice Selene. She's been with him 18 months. He says she'll be ready in 2-3 years to run her own routes.
- Building a small dried-mushroom inventory for emergencies. Patrick himself sells me his lower-grade pieces to dry — porcini especially.
- Not putting all my mushroom needs on him. The bread course gets a different mushroom (from Mycoterra). Family meal mushrooms are from Anthony's. Patrick is for the mushroom course only.

## Personal note

Patrick brought me a small jar of preserved chanterelles last week, "for your home, not the restaurant." His own recipe — chanterelles in olive oil with garlic and thyme. I ate two with toast on Sunday morning before Mateo woke up. They were extraordinary.

Patrick is one of those suppliers who is also, in some way, my friend. I don't know what to do with that exactly. I want the restaurant to be a business but the work depends on relationships like this.

I will not let the business chew up the friendship. I'll pay him on time, pay him fairly, and not ask him to do things the forest doesn't want him to do.

## What I want from him long-term

Five years from now, ideally, Patrick is still foraging and Selene has her own routes. We have a network of two foragers serving us. Each can cover for the other when needed. The mushroom course becomes a permanent feature of the restaurant, sustainable for both us and them.

This is the version where everyone wins.
