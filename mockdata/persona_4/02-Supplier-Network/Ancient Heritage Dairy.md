# Ancient Heritage Dairy

**Relationship:** Sheep's milk cheese for cheese course
**Located:** Madras, OR (high desert, central Oregon)
**Contacts:** Anna and David
**First met:** 2025-12 at a Portland cheese event
**Delivery cadence:** Weekly
**Status:** Critical for cheese course

## Background

Ancient Heritage is small. Anna and David run it together with help from one farmhand. They have a flock of around 40 East Friesian sheep and make a handful of cheeses, all aged on site. They sell mostly to Portland-area restaurants and a few specialty cheese shops.

David came from a software career; Anna came from agriculture school. They moved to Madras in 2014 because the high desert was the cheapest land they could afford with good water access.

## What we get

Primarily their washed-rind sheep's milk wheel — they don't have a name for it publicly, they just call it "the wheel." It's funky in a controlled way, with a creamy paste and an assertive rind. Tastes like the high desert.

We take 6 lbs per week, which gives us about 100 portions of cheese course at a 25g pour weight.

## Pricing

$32/lb. Not cheap. Worth it.

## How we work together

Anna texts me Mondays. She tells me what's ready that week. I confirm the order. Friday delivery (she drives in from Madras Friday mornings).

For special occasions (private events, special menus), I can request more with two weeks notice.

## What worries me

Their flock is small. If something happens to a few of the milking ewes, the cheese supply tightens. Anna has been clear that they could lose 10-15% of production with a single bad season.

Backup plans: I have relationships with Briar Rose Creamery (cow's milk; very different but workable) and Rivers Edge Chèvre (goat). Both are excellent alternatives if I needed them.

## What David told me on my farm visit

He drove me out to where the sheep graze. Said: "The flavor is the land. We're just shepherds and cheesemakers in between."

This is the kind of philosophy I respond to. The cheese is good because the land is the right land for sheep, and Anna and David are good at letting that come through.

## Personal note

Anna sent me a small wheel of an experimental cheese she's been working on — bloomy rind, more delicate, almost like a Brie made with sheep's milk. She said it was for me to think about, not for production. She didn't expect me to use it.

I want to use it. It's beautiful. We are talking about doing a "cheese flight" private event sometime in the fall featuring it.

## What I want long-term

Anna and David's operation stays small. The cheese stays excellent. Lark stays one of their primary restaurant customers. They never feel pressured to scale up beyond what makes the cheese good.

This is the kind of supplier I want to invest in — by being a reliable customer, paying on time, talking them up to other chefs, never asking them to compromise.
