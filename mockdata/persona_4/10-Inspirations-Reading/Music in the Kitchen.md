# Music in the Kitchen

**Status:** Active policy
**Date:** 2026-04-08

What we play in the kitchen during prep and service.

## The premise

The kitchen has music during prep. The kitchen has no music during service.

Both of these are intentional. The music during prep changes the energy of the work. The silence during service focuses it.

## Prep music

Owner-curated playlists. I built a playlist for each day of the week. Beto built three. Lila built a few. We rotate.

What I look for in prep music:
- Energy without being aggressive
- Variety so the kitchen doesn't get tired of a sound
- Not too lyric-forward (it competes with conversation)
- Quality that respects the people listening

Genres I rotate:
- Jazz (Coltrane, Wayne Shorter, Mary Halvorson)
- Brazilian music (Tom Jobim, Elis Regina, Marisa Monte)
- Hip-hop instrumentals (Madlib, J Dilla, Knxwledge)
- Japanese ambient and city pop (Haruomi Hosono, Mariya Takeuchi)
- Some folk (Joni Mitchell, Joanna Newsom)
- Electronic but mellow (Floating Points, Caribou)

What I avoid:
- Top 40 anything
- Songs we will all be sick of in 3 months
- Lyrics about violence or anger (the kitchen is not the place)

## What we don't do

- Music chosen by individual cooks during prep (one of us picks for the room)
- Music loud enough to require shouting
- Music during service

## Service silence

When the doors open, the music stops.

The kitchen needs to hear:
- The pass calling tickets
- The fryer timer
- The walk-in door opening
- The plates being placed on the pass
- The cook beside you breathing differently
- The vague hum of the dining room

These are the sounds of service. They are information. Music covers them.

The dining room has a quiet playlist (sub-conversation level) curated by Mateo and Caro. The kitchen does not hear it.

## The headphone debate

Some kitchens let cooks wear single earbuds. We do not.

The cook in the kitchen needs to hear what's happening around them. A single earbud cuts that in half. The risk is too high.

This is a hard rule. New hires sometimes push back. The rule stays.

## Family meal music

Family meal at 5pm has music. The cook on family meal duty picks. It tells you something about them.

Theo picks J Dilla. Inez picks classical. Jasper picks dad rock. Lila picks Brazilian. Beto picks early hip-hop. I pick Japanese ambient. Mateo picks French chanson (we tolerate it).

The music at family meal is the most diverse moment in the kitchen. It is one of my favorite times of the day.

## Service music for the dining room

Mateo built the dining room playlist. He has the better ear for what fits a tasting menu.

Genres:
- Old Brazilian (lots of Tom Jobim)
- Erik Satie and similar piano
- Bill Evans, Keith Jarrett solo recordings
- Quiet jazz vocal (Helen Merrill, Mark Murphy)
- Some shamisen and koto recordings (Japanese acoustic)
- Sufjan Stevens at low volume

What we avoid:
- Songs people know well enough to sing along to
- Anything that pulls attention from conversation
- Volume that competes with dining room ambient

The playlist is roughly 9 hours long. Plays from 5:30pm to 10:30pm without repeating. I cycle the playlist seasonally.

## A specific song

There's a Tom Jobim recording ("Águas de Março," sung with Elis Regina) that I almost cried to once at a kitchen prep. I have not put it on the dining room playlist because it would be too much.

It plays at prep about once a week. The cook nearest the speakers often pauses to listen.

## Personal note

Music in the kitchen is one of the small things I think a lot about. The music tells the team what kind of kitchen they are in. A kitchen with thoughtful music is a thoughtful kitchen.

The cook who notices the music notices everything.
