# Quotes I Keep

**Status:** Personal collection
**Date:** 2026-04-25

Quotes about food, work, and life that I have written down over the years. They live on a small notebook on my desk.

## On cooking

"Cooking is not about being the best or having a Michelin star — it's about cooking with your soul." — Massimo Bottura

"To eat is a necessity, but to eat intelligently is an art." — La Rochefoucauld

"Cooking with kids is not just about ingredients, recipes, and cooking. It's about harnessing imagination, empowerment, and creativity." — Guy Fieri (I keep this one despite Guy Fieri because it's true)

"If you can read this thank a teacher. If you can taste this thank a cook." — anonymous; I saw it on a t-shirt at a culinary conference

"The discovery of a new dish does more for the happiness of mankind than the discovery of a new star." — Brillat-Savarin

## On discipline

"What you do every day matters more than what you do once in a while." — Gretchen Rubin

"The price of discipline is always less than the pain of regret." — anonymous

"In cooking, as in all the arts, simplicity is the sign of perfection." — Curnonsky

"The simplest method is most often the best." — my mother

## On the work

"Don't waste your time looking back. You're not going that way." — Ragnar Lothbrok (I know, but the quote is good)

"Show up. Pay attention. Tell the truth. Don't be attached to the outcome." — Angeles Arrien

"You can't be what you can't see." — Marian Wright Edelman (in my context: this is why representation in kitchens matters)

"The cook is the only worker whose product is destroyed in the very act of consumption." — Auguste Escoffier

## On hospitality

"To know what you prefer instead of humbly saying Amen to what the world tells you you ought to prefer, is to have kept your soul alive." — Robert Louis Stevenson

"You can't make people happy. But you can make a place where they can be happy if they want to be." — Danny Meyer (paraphrased; I forget exactly)

"True hospitality consists of giving the best of yourself to your guests." — Eleanor Roosevelt

"The first thing a stranger should be offered is water. Then food. Then a question about who they are." — paraphrase of various traditions

## On failure

"You miss 100% of the shots you don't take." — Wayne Gretzky (yes, the hockey quote; works)

"I have not failed. I've just found 10,000 ways that won't work." — Thomas Edison

"The opposite of love is not hate. It's indifference." — Elie Wiesel (applied to food: a diner who is bored is worse than a diner who hated the meal)

"Fall down seven times, get up eight." — Japanese proverb (taught to me by my mother in Japanese first)

## On craft

"Hold an image of the life you want, and that image will become fact." — Norman Vincent Peale (I keep this even though I disagree with Peale on many things)

"Practice is the answer to most questions." — anonymous

"You can't depend on your eyes when your imagination is out of focus." — Mark Twain

"Style is the answer to everything." — Charles Bukowski (I argue with this one; for cooking, technique is the answer, style is the surface)

## On time

"The two most powerful warriors are patience and time." — Tolstoy

"It is during our darkest moments that we must focus to see the light." — Aristotle

"What you do speaks so loudly that I cannot hear what you say." — Emerson (relevant to running a kitchen)

## From my own thinking

"The work is the work. The performance of the work is something else. Be careful which you spend your time on."

"You cannot make a great restaurant with a tired team."

"Cook for the person who walked in tired. The other diners will benefit too."

"A small thing done well is bigger than a big thing done poorly."

"Care is the ingredient nobody can substitute."

## Quotes about my mother that I have written

She has said many things that I think about:
- "If you can't feed your staff well, you can't feed your guests well." (She paraphrased a Korean grandmother to me; I attributed it to Atomix in earlier notes but it was first hers.)
- "Do not lose yourself."
- "Bring patience to the dashi."
- "The smell tells you. Trust your nose."

I write these down so they are not lost.

## Personal note

I collect quotes the way some chefs collect knives. They are the words of people who thought carefully and said something true. I do not put them on the kitchen wall. I keep them in the notebook.

When I am uncertain, I open the notebook.

— Aiya
