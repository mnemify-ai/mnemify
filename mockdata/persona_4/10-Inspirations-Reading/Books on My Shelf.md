# Books on My Shelf

**Status:** Personal library, kitchen-relevant subset
**Date:** 2026-05-15

The books in the small bookshelf I keep at the restaurant. About 30 books I refer to often. Curated, not exhaustive.

## The technical books (15)

- **On Food and Cooking** by Harold McGee. The reference. I have the 2004 edition. Heavily annotated.
- **Modernist Cuisine at Home** by Nathan Myhrvold. I use this for technique reference, not modernist gimmicks.
- **The Science of Cooking** by Stuart Farrimond. Quick reference; good for new cooks.
- **The Flavor Bible** by Karen Page and Andrew Dornenburg. Used constantly.
- **Salt, Fat, Acid, Heat** by Samin Nosrat. Better than people give it credit for.
- **The Professional Chef** (CIA). Reference. Mostly old-school French.
- **The Japanese Grill** by Tadashi Ono and Harris Salat. Yakitori technique.
- **Washoku** by Elizabeth Andoh. The single best English-language book on Japanese home cooking.
- **The Folk Art of Japanese Country Cooking** by Gaku Homma. Underrated.
- **Japanese Soul Cooking** by Tadashi Ono and Harris Salat. Casual reference; well-written.
- **Plenty** by Yotam Ottolenghi. For vegetarian inspiration.
- **The Noma Guide to Fermentation** by René Redzepi and David Zilber. Heavily used for the pickle pantry.
- **Black Sea** by Caroline Eden. Travel writing about food. Read for the writing as much as the recipes.
- **Pacific** by Sasha Martin. Pacific Rim cooking; varied quality but good for ideas.
- **Larousse Gastronomique**. Reference. I look up classical preparations.

## The memoirs (8)

- **Kitchen Confidential** by Anthony Bourdain. Honest about restaurant work in a way few books are. Read every 3 years.
- **My Life in France** by Julia Child. Read once a year. The most generous food memoir I know.
- **Heat** by Bill Buford. Stage culture, intense and specific.
- **Toast** by Nigel Slater. British, quiet, sad, beautiful.
- **The Apprentice** by Jacques Pepin. Technique memoir.
- **Yes, Chef** by Marcus Samuelsson. Important for me. A chef who looked like a version of me telling his story.
- **Blood, Bones & Butter** by Gabrielle Hamilton. Honest about being a woman in this work.
- **The Devil in the Kitchen** by Marco Pierre White. Reminder of the bad model.

## The philosophy and craft (7)

- **The Earth Knows My Name** by Patricia Klindienst. Garden writing.
- **The Last Whole Earth Catalog** (Stewart Brand). Inspirational.
- **A Pattern Language** by Christopher Alexander. Architecture; informs how I think about the space.
- **The Making of a Cook** by Madeleine Kamman. Old. Strong on the philosophy of cooking.
- **Tassajara Bread Book** by Edward Espe Brown. Bread book that is also a Zen book.
- **Letters to a Young Poet** by Rilke. Not about food. About the work.
- **The Wisdom of Insecurity** by Alan Watts. For balance.

## What I do not keep on the shelf

- Most cookbooks. Most are recipe collections without the underlying thinking. They are noise.
- Most celebrity chef books. The good ones are above; the average ones are extractive.
- The Internet. I do not bookmark food blogs at the restaurant.

## How I read

I do not read cover-to-cover usually. I keep books at the kitchen and open them at random when I am thinking. McGee falls open at certain pages I have read 30 times. Page references I trust:
- McGee on protein denaturation in fish (p. 220-225)
- McGee on emulsions (p. 626-634)
- Washoku on dashi (p. 28-36)
- Noma on lacto-fermentation (p. 35-86)

## Books I refer to once and never again

Many cookbooks. Most are a single useful technique surrounded by a lot of filler. I extract the useful pages, photocopy them into a binder, and donate the book.

The binder of photocopied pages is 4 inches thick. It is one of the most useful things in the kitchen.

## Personal note

I came up in kitchens where the chefs did not read. They cooked from instinct and apprenticeship. The instinct was good. The reading would have made it better.

I read because I am not as good as my mentors. The reading closes the gap.

I also read because I love it. It is the rest I take from the kitchen that returns me to the kitchen better.

— Aiya
