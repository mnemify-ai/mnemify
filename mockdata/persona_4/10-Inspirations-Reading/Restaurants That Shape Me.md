# Restaurants That Shape Me

**Status:** Personal canon
**Date:** 2026-03-30

The restaurants I have eaten at that have shaped how I think. Not all the great restaurants. The specific ones that did something to me.

## The formative meals

### Saison (San Francisco, 2016)
A 22-course tasting menu in their original Mission location. I was 28. I had saved for months to afford it. Lasted 4 hours.

What shaped me: the discipline of the kitchen at full intensity. The plates arrived at the right second. The choreography was perfect. The food was opinionated.

What I disagreed with: too long. 22 courses was 6 too many. By course 14 my palate was tired and the kitchen was still pushing.

The lesson: the kitchen has to know when to stop.

### Single Thread (Healdsburg, 2018, before I worked there)
A 10-course tasting menu in their first year. I was 30. I cried at the dessert course.

What shaped me: the integration of the farm into the meal. The chef Kyle Connaughton named every ingredient by farmer. The wine pairing was Pacific NW heavy. The room was warm.

What I learned: a restaurant can be exceptional and warm at the same time. The two are not opposed.

The fact that I worked there for 2 years after was because of this meal.

### Atomix (NYC, 2020, before I worked there)
A 10-course Korean tasting. The cards. The discipline. The space.

What shaped me: see "Atomix Influence." Worked there for 4 years.

### Coi (San Francisco, 2017)
Under Daniel Patterson. A long, slow, ingredient-driven meal.

What shaped me: the quiet of the room. The fact that the chef was on the line and not performing.

The lesson: the chef can be in the kitchen, not in the dining room. The diner does not need to meet the chef.

### Alma (Los Angeles, 2014, when it was open)
Ari Taymor's small restaurant. A meal that I would describe as "imperfect and brave."

What shaped me: a chef cooking specifically for himself in a way that was not yet ready for everyone. The food was inconsistent. The food was also one of the most original meals I had had.

The lesson: bravery has a cost. The cost of being yourself in your food is that not everyone will love it.

### Ai Sushi (small place in NYC East Village, 2018)
A 10-seat omakase in a tiny basement. Chef-owner from Tokyo. He was 70.

What shaped me: the simplicity. The fish was perfect. The rice was perfect. There were 14 pieces. No flourishes. No drama.

The lesson: if the ingredients are right, you do not need to dress them.

### A Casual Family-Run Trattoria in Marche, Italy (2019)
We were on vacation. Mateo wanted to eat there. The chef cooked her grandmother's pasta and showed us the technique after.

What shaped me: the directness of family cooking. The hospitality of a single person serving you food she has been making since she was 8.

The lesson: hospitality is older than fine dining. The 70-year-old grandmother in Marche knows things the 30-year-old chef in NYC does not.

### Tartine Manufactory (San Francisco, 2017)
A casual lunch. Lila (now my pastry chef) was working there. I went because of her.

What shaped me: the bread. The bread program at Tartine is unsurpassed. The bread was a small revolution for me.

The lesson: bread is its own course. We have a bread course at Lark because of this.

### A street stall in Bangkok (2015)
A khao soi from a stall I returned to 5 times in 4 days. Cooked by a woman who had been making it for 30 years.

What shaped me: spice. Depth. The fact that $2 of food cooked well is better than $200 of food cooked carelessly.

The lesson: price is unrelated to quality. The reverse, the relationship between price and quality, is also unrelated.

### Atomix in 2024 (my last year there)
The night I cooked the smoked trout course for the first time as a full station. It was a Friday. The dish came together. The chef came over after service and squeezed my shoulder.

What shaped me: the night I knew I was a chef who could cook professionally at this level. Not before. Not because I told myself so. Because the work was good.

The lesson: the moments of becoming are quiet and specific.

## Meals I will revisit

If I had a week off in 2027, I would eat at:
- Sushi Saito (Tokyo)
- a return to Single Thread
- Ondo (Lisbon)
- a small place in Hakodate my aunt mentioned in her notebooks

That's my list.

## Personal note

I have eaten at many famous restaurants. Most were forgettable. The ones above are the ones I think about while cooking at Lark.

The reading and the eating and the cooking are one practice. The meal at Atomix shapes the plate at Lark. The trattoria in Marche shapes the warmth at Lark. The khao soi in Bangkok shapes the spice work.

It is all one thing.

— Aiya
