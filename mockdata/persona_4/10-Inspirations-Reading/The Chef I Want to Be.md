# The Chef I Want to Be

**Status:** Reflective; private
**Date:** 2026-05-01

A note to myself about the chef I am trying to be. To return to when I forget.

## The chef I want to be

She is on the line every night. She cooks. Her food is the food the diner eats. She does not direct from a clipboard. The diner sees her hands in the kitchen if they sit at the counter.

She is not famous. She does not chase fame. She is in the photo only when the photo is necessary. She trusts that the food is what people will remember.

She is honest. With the team, with the diner, with the press. She does not soft-pedal hard truths. She does not perform false modesty. She knows what she does well and she knows where she is weak.

She is calm. The kitchen runs on her energy. If she is anxious, the kitchen is anxious. She has learned to project calm even when she is afraid.

She is generous. She teaches. The line cooks at her kitchen are better cooks when they leave than when they arrived. She gives them what she has, including the parts she paid for with hard years.

She is tired. The work is real. She does not pretend it isn't. Some nights she goes home and cries with her partner. Some nights she goes home and laughs. Both nights happen.

She is curious. She reads. She eats at other people's restaurants. She listens to her team. She learns. She is not above learning even at 50.

She is rooted. She has a place. She has chosen Portland and she belongs to it. She knows her suppliers' names and their kids' names. She buys her groceries from people who remember her face.

She is loyal. Her team is her team. She protects them. She defends them when they need defending. She fires them when she has to but she does it with dignity and a severance check.

She is professional. She runs a business that makes money. She pays the team well. She takes a fair share. She does not confuse the artistic part with the business part.

She is private. The work is public. The chef is not. Her personal life is hers. Her relationship is hers. Her struggles are hers. The work she shares.

She is committed. Not "passionate." Committed. Passion comes and goes. Commitment is the choice she makes every morning when she wakes up and goes to the kitchen.

## What she is not

She is not the chef on TV. She is not the chef writing books. She is not the chef at the chef conferences. She is not the chef collaborating with brands.

She might be those things if they help the restaurant. She is not those things first. She is the chef in the kitchen first.

## What she will become

She will probably get better. Most chefs do, up to a point. She might peak in her 40s and stabilize in her 50s. She will continue to learn from her team and from new chefs coming up. She will mentor.

She will probably get worse at some things. Her body will get worse. Her wrists, her knees, her back. She will need to delegate more physical work eventually. She is preparing for that.

She will probably have moments of doubt. Several. She will manage them.

She will close the restaurant one day. Either after a long run or after a shorter one. She will close with dignity. She will not bankrupt her investors. She will not abandon her team.

She will not regret the work. She is sure of that one thing.

## What I do today to be her

- I cook every day. Even on prep days, I cook.
- I read 10 pages of something good.
- I check the financials once a week.
- I have an honest conversation with one team member.
- I sleep enough.
- I do not look at social media for more than 10 minutes a day.
- I write occasional notes like this one to remind myself.

## What I want to remember

The chef I want to be exists. She is not a fantasy. She is a discipline. She is the result of the daily choices I make.

She is also Aiya, age 38, sitting at a desk in May 2026 writing this note 7 weeks before her restaurant opens.

She is not after. She is now.

— Aiya
