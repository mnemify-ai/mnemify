# Tampopo and Other Food Films

**Status:** Recommendations and reflections
**Date:** 2026-04-18

The films about food I love. Watched repeatedly. Influence the work.

## Tampopo (1985, dir. Juzo Itami)

The single best film about food I have ever seen. Watched 12 times.

What it does:
- Takes ramen seriously without being precious
- Honors the cook (Tampopo, the noodle shop owner) as a hero
- Treats food eating as both ridiculous and sacred
- Includes a famous scene of a couple sharing a raw egg yolk that I will not describe further but which is essential

What I love about it:
- The film treats food the way I want to treat food
- The protagonist learns to make perfect ramen by working with mentors who criticize her without cruelty
- The supporting vignettes are surreal and tender
- The film has comedy without being a comedy

What I have stolen from it:
- The principle that mastery is the result of repetition with feedback
- The principle that a great cook can be a small business owner, not a celebrity
- The image of a noodle bowl as a serious thing

## A Chef in Love (1996, dir. Nana Djordjadze)

Georgian-French film. About a chef in Georgia during the Soviet era. Lyrical.

What I love: the food is incidental to a love story but it is treated reverently.

What it taught me: a chef in a film can be specific to a place. The Georgian food in this film is not generic "Eastern European." It is specific to Georgia.

## The Taste of Things (2023, dir. Trần Anh Hùng)

Recently watched (twice). The opening sequence is the best food cooking sequence ever filmed.

What I love: the patience. The film is slow. The dishes take their time. The viewer is invited to sit with the act of cooking, not the result.

What it taught me: cooking can be cinematic without being performed. The film does not heighten. It observes.

## Jiro Dreams of Sushi (2011, dir. David Gelb)

The documentary about Jiro Ono.

What I love: the discipline. The lifetime of work. The relationship between Jiro and his sons.

What I have reservations about: the film glamorizes a working life that may be lonelier than it admits. Jiro's sons are also Jiro's employees for life. The dynamic is complicated.

The film is also a portrait of a specific kind of artistry that has no female voice. I am aware of this.

What I have taken: the principle that one perfect thing is the work of a lifetime. The piece of sushi at 80 is the result of decades.

What I have not taken: the singular obsession that excludes other parts of life. I am not building a restaurant that requires me to give up my partner, my mother, my reading, my walks.

## Big Night (1996, dir. Stanley Tucci, Campbell Scott)

About two Italian brothers running a failing restaurant in 1950s New Jersey.

What I love: the timpano scene. The honesty about the financial precariousness of a small restaurant. The brothers who love and resent each other.

What it taught me: a restaurant can be ruined by trying to be too much. The brothers' restaurant fails because they are not willing to compromise their vision. The film does not judge them for this. It honors them.

What I have asked myself: do I have the discipline to compromise where compromise is needed and the discipline to refuse where compromise would kill the restaurant? I think yes. I will find out.

## Like Water for Chocolate (1992, dir. Alfonso Arau)

Magical realism about food and emotion.

What I love: the principle that the cook's emotional state passes into the food. This is mystical. It is also (some chefs would say) literally true. I do not have a position on this. I notice my food is better when I am centered. The film says the same thing.

## My Octopus Teacher (2020, dir. Pippa Ehrlich, James Reed)

Not a food film exactly. About a man and an octopus.

What I love: the patience. The sustained attention to one being over a year.

What I have taken: the principle that knowing one thing deeply is more valuable than knowing many things shallowly.

This applies to my supplier relationships. I know Patrick the mushroom forager well. I know what he picks each week. I know what worries him. That kind of knowing is the work.

## Documentaries I have not yet watched

- The Search for General Tso
- For Grace
- Chef's Table (specific episodes)
- The Truffle Hunters

I will watch these eventually. The Tom Sellers episode of Chef's Table specifically.

## Films I would not recommend

- The food films that are romantic comedies in disguise (food as a metaphor for love, food as backdrop for plot)
- Any film where a chef yells at the food
- Most cooking shows (Bourdain's are the exception; he is also a different category)

## Personal note

I watch food films to remember why I do this. The films are not about technique. They are about the relationship between the cook and the work.

Watching them at home with Mateo on a Sunday is one of my favorite things. We pause to discuss. We disagree about which sushi place in Tokyo is best (his choice, mine choice). The films are food for me the way wine is food for him.

Tampopo is the one I would put in a time capsule if I had to pick one.

— Aiya
