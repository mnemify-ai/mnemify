# Contractor Notes — The Build

**Status:** Build nearly complete; punch list outstanding
**Date:** 2026-05-26

We started construction in October 2025. We are nearly done. The build has been hard.

## The contractors

- **GC: Bradbury Construction** (Portland, mid-sized commercial)
- **Mechanical: Westside Mechanical**
- **Electrical: Brian's Electric**
- **Plumbing: Reliable Plumbing**
- **Hood and suppression: Pacific Fire Systems**
- **Finishes: Bradbury subcontracted to several smaller specialty trades**

## Major milestones

- Demolition: completed November 2025
- Framing and rough-in: completed January 2026
- Major equipment delivery and install: February-March 2026
- Finishes (walls, floors, ceilings): March-April 2026
- HVAC commissioning: in progress (target this week)
- Punch list: in progress

## What's been hard

The HVAC is the hardest thing. We're trying to keep the kitchen exhaust quiet, the dining room temperature controlled, and the air pressure differential right (so kitchen smells don't pull into the dining room). All of this with a tight building envelope. Westside Mechanical has been struggling.

The hood specifically is louder than I want. We may need a baffle revision after opening.

## Cost overruns

The original budget for build-out was $385,000. Current cost is approximately $462,000. Overrun of $77,000 — about 20%.

Drivers of overrun:
- HVAC complexity ($28k over)
- Electrical capacity upgrade required by city ($15k unplanned)
- Custom millwork (the bar and the pass) ($18k over)
- Misc finishes and changes ($16k)

The $77k overrun is real but within Diane's contingency. We are not blowing through the contingency line.

## Personal observations

- **Bradbury is good but slow.** The GC has been honest about timing and quality is solid. But every milestone has slipped a week or two.
- **Westside has been frustrating.** Communication has been poor. Three times I've found out about delays through other subs rather than from Westside directly.
- **Brian's Electric is excellent.** Brian himself is on every job. We have not had a single issue.

## What I would do differently

- Get a second bid on HVAC. We went with Westside on a recommendation. The recommendation didn't pan out.
- Build a 25% contingency into the budget, not 15%. Overruns are normal.
- Visit the site daily during finish work. The decisions that matter for daily operations are the decisions made in the last 30 days of construction.

## Personal note

The build has been more emotionally taxing than I expected. The decisions are constant. The trade-offs are constant. The cost overruns are constant.

Three nights last week I came home and cried at the kitchen table. Mateo just sat with me. He understands.

This is supposed to be the easy part — the planning. The harder part — running the restaurant — is still ahead.

But also: when I walk into the kitchen and the layout is exactly what I imagined, when I see the pass with the right amount of light, when the cold line works the way I sketched it on paper a year ago — there's a real feeling of having made something. The build is the bones of what this restaurant becomes.

Almost done.
