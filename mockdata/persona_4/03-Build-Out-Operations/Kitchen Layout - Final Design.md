# Kitchen Layout — Final Design

**Status:** Locked. Built. Operating.
**Date:** 2026-05-26
**Designer:** Caro coordinated; Beto and I drove the layout decisions

## The constraint

We have approximately 320 square feet for the working kitchen. This is small. A 28-seat tasting menu restaurant typically wants 400+ square feet of kitchen. We don't have it. We had to make every inch work.

## The layout

The kitchen runs along the back wall and an L-shape into the right side of the building.

**Hot line** (back wall, 14 feet):
- 4-burner induction range
- 36-inch flat top (gas)
- Convection oven below
- Salamander above for finishing
- Plancha (small, dedicated to certain courses)

**Cold line / garde manger** (right wall, 8 feet):
- 6-foot refrigerated prep table (with cold wells for service)
- Plating surface above
- Drawer refrigeration below

**Pass** (parallel to hot line, 8 feet):
- Hot pass with overhead heat lamps
- Cold pass section adjacent
- Final plating happens here under my direct line of sight

**Dish station** (separate, accessed through door):
- Conveyor dishwasher
- Three-compartment sink
- Drying racks

**Walk-in cooler** (off main kitchen, 6x8):
- Shared by all stations
- Organized by station (hot prep, cold prep, dairy, produce, proteins)

**Dry storage** (small closet, off the back hallway, 6x4)

## Why this layout works

- Beto can see every station from the hot line
- I can see every station from the pass
- The cold line is positioned so that course prep flows naturally into the pass
- The dish station is acoustically isolated (mostly) by a partial wall

## What we gave up

- A dedicated pastry station. Lila works on the cold line during prep, then plates dessert at the pass. This is suboptimal but workable.
- A dedicated wet bar. Mateo has a small bar in the dining room but no wet station in the kitchen.
- Storage. We have less dry storage than is comfortable. We compensate with more frequent deliveries.

## Equipment choices

The big decisions:

- **Induction over gas for the range.** Heretical for some chefs. I love induction for precision and for the kitchen climate (lower ambient heat). The flat top is gas for control of certain searing and crisping needs.
- **No deep fryer.** We're not doing anything fried at scale. The shiso leaf gets fried in a small pan. That's it.
- **Two convection ovens, no traditional oven.** The convections handle 95% of what we need. For the rare time we want gentler heat (occasional brioche), we use the salamander on low.
- **Plancha for the kombu wafer and a few other specifics.** Was probably overkill but I wanted it.

## What I would change in retrospect

- The pass is 6 inches narrower than I wanted. We accommodated to give the cold line more space. Now I think the cold line could have absorbed less. Will live with it.
- The walk-in is too far from the hot line. Cooks lose time walking. In hindsight, we could have re-thought the back hallway to bring it closer.

## What's working better than expected

- The induction range. Beto was skeptical. He's converted.
- The acoustic separation of the dish station. Service noise level is lower than I expected.
- The pass under direct overhead light. Plating is visible. Detail catches my eye when something's off.

## Personal note

Designing this kitchen took eight months. I lived with floor plans for weeks. I'd go to bed thinking about the position of the walk-in door. I'd wake up worried about the height of the pass.

What I learned: the kitchen design is the cuisine. The decisions I made about layout shape what we can cook. A different kitchen would have given us a different menu.

I'm proud of this kitchen. It's small. It's mine.
