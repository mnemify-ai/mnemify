# Walk-In Cooler Organization

**Status:** Locked
**Date:** 2026-05-20

The walk-in is 6x8 feet. Smaller than I'd like. We have to use every inch well.

## Organization by zone

Walking in, the layout is:

**Left wall (entering):**
- Top shelf: Dry-cured items (in containers — trout cure, cured yolks, etc.)
- Middle shelf: Proteins on order for the next 3 days (lamb, fish, etc.)
- Bottom shelf: Reserved for tomorrow's prep

**Back wall:**
- Top shelf: Stocks, sauces, jus
- Middle shelf: Dairy (cream, butter, milk)
- Bottom shelf: Cheese (separated from other items; sealed properly)

**Right wall:**
- Top: Produce that needs cold (mushrooms, herbs)
- Middle: Heavy produce (root vegetables)
- Bottom: Beverages (specific wines that need refrigeration, etc.)

**Floor:**
- Reserved for crates of immediate-use produce
- Cleared and mopped daily

## Rules

- Everything labeled with date received and date prepared
- FIFO (first in, first out) strictly enforced
- Anything past prep date moves to staff meal or is composted
- Raw proteins never above ready-to-eat items
- Cheese in sealed containers always

## What I learned at Atomix

The walk-in is a temple. Disorganization in the walk-in is the visible symptom of a kitchen that doesn't respect the food.

I make Beto walk through the walk-in with me every Friday. We check organization, check rotation, identify anything off.

## Common failures

- Containers labeled illegibly
- Things stored where they don't belong because someone was in a hurry
- Items past prep date that didn't get cleared
- Spills not cleaned up

All of these are correctable through habit. The habit is built one week at a time.

## What we do daily

- 9 AM: Caro or Beto walks the walk-in for organization
- After lunch service: full reset of the walk-in
- Before family meal: walk-in temperature check, log

This is boring. It is also how restaurants stay clean and safe.
