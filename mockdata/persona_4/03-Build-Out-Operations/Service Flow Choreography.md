# Service Flow Choreography

**Status:** Working draft
**Date:** 2026-05-24

How a meal moves through the restaurant from start to finish.

## Guest arrival

- Greeted at the door by Caro or FOH lead
- Coat check if needed (small closet by the entrance)
- Walked to table, water poured, menu placed
- 90-second pause for settling
- Server returns for any allergy/preference check

## Beverage opener

- Server takes drink order (Mateo's pairing flight is the default; à la carte is available)
- First pour or water touch within 4 minutes of seating
- Mateo personally introduces the pairing if it's selected

## Food service

The meal moves in 9 courses (10 if pre-dessert is deployed). Timing follows the Menu Pacing Notes.

For each course:
- Course fired in kitchen
- Plates assembled at pass under my supervision
- Server runs the course
- Server states the name of the course and any key sourcing element (e.g., "Smoked trout from Skamania Springs")
- Server retreats to give diner the first taste alone
- Server returns 90 seconds later to clear if needed or check on flavor

## Pacing cues

The kitchen runs on the pass watching the dining room. We do not fire the next course until the previous one is fully consumed at all tables in service.

This is hard to operationalize at 28 covers. We use a paper system (a small whiteboard at the pass listing tables and their course status) until we get better at this.

## Tableside moments

Three moments where the server or Mateo or I am at the table:

1. The bread arrival (brief)
2. The lamb glaze pour (theatrical, by Beto in soft openings; eventually any server can do)
3. The cheese plate placement (slow, deliberate)

These moments are choreographed. They are not improvisational.

## Departure

- Guest finishes dessert
- Coffee/tea offered (most decline; some take espresso)
- Bill presented within 5 minutes of finishing
- Caro or I walk over to thank them personally for coming

## Total experience time

Target: 2 hours 15 minutes from seating to bill.

## What goes wrong

- A table runs slow. We slow the kitchen.
- A table needs to leave early. We compress courses where possible.
- A course is delayed in the kitchen. We touch the table to acknowledge.

The recovery from any of these is the FOH's job. Caro is training the team specifically on recovery scenarios.

## Personal note

The choreography is what makes a tasting menu feel like an experience rather than a series of plates. We have to get this right.
