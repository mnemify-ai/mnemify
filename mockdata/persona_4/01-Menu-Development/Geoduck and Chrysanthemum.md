# Geoduck and Chrysanthemum

**Status:** Concept. May not make the opening menu.
**Date:** 2026-05-18

The idea came from a conversation with my mother in March. She was telling me about a dish her grandmother used to make in Hokkaido, paper-thin slices of something — she couldn't remember if it was scallop or squid, the memory was hazy — dressed with chrysanthemum petals and a vinegar that her grandmother made herself from rice and persimmon. She said it tasted like *natsukashii*, which is the word in Japanese for the kind of nostalgia that hurts a little.

I have been chasing that dish for two months.

## What I've landed on

- Geoduck, sliced cross-section at about 1mm — the neck only, the body is too tough
- Quick blanch (3 seconds in salted water, ice bath immediately)
- Edible chrysanthemum petals (yellow only; the purple ones are too bitter for this)
- A vinegar I'm making from rice and quince — quince because persimmon is wrong for spring/summer, and quince has the same kind of perfumed acidity
- A small drift of grated frozen daikon at the edge of the plate
- Salt: a little of the Hokkaido sea salt my mother sends me twice a year. Yes, it's special. Yes, it costs more than it should. No, I don't care.

## What's stopping me

The geoduck supply is rough. I'm working with Caroline at Taylor Shellfish in Shelton and she can get me geoduck consistently, but the prep is labor-intensive and the yield is rough. We get about 200g of usable neck meat per geoduck, and a single 4-portion service is going to use roughly 60g of that. So each geoduck gives me about 12 portions, which sounds great until you account for trim loss and the fact that Beto and I are still learning the cut.

At the projected cost, this course is running $14 in food cost per plate, which on a $295 tasting menu is fine but tight.

## What my mother said about the test plate

I FaceTimed her on Sunday and walked the dish through. She watched me plate it and was quiet for a long time. Then she said, "It does not look like Obaachan's dish. But it looks like a dish Obaachan would have liked."

That's the best thing she could have said. I cried a little after I hung up. I will not put that on the menu description.

## What might kill it for opening

- Geoduck supply is too unstable for a tasting menu we're running 5 nights a week
- The vinegar I'm making from quince takes 8 weeks to develop properly. I started the first batch in March. The second batch I started two weeks ago. I have enough vinegar to run through July, maybe.
- It's a fragile dish. One mistake on the slice and the whole course is ruined. We're not yet at the level of consistency on the line where I can trust this in a 5-night-a-week service.

## What I want to do

Hold this for a special-occasion course. Run it once or twice during the soft opening to see how it lands. If it survives, give it a permanent slot at course 2 by end of August. If not, keep it in the back of my head for the autumn menu rotation.

## Note to myself

Do not over-narrate this dish to the diner. The server should say: "geoduck, quince vinegar, chrysanthemum." That's it. No story. The story is mine, not the diner's. The diner should taste it cold and then taste it.

The story will be on a small printed card we give them at the end of the meal, if at all. Maybe just for the regulars who come back.
