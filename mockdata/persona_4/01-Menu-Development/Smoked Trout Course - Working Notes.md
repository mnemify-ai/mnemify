# Smoked Trout Course — Working Notes

**Course:** Probably course 3 or 4, depending on rhythm
**Status:** Third iteration. Getting closer.
**Date:** 2026-05-22

The trout is from Ron at Skamania Springs — he keeps a small operation in the Gorge, raises rainbows on a mix of cold spring water and a feed he won't fully disclose (asked him three times, he just laughs). They taste like the water they live in, which is to say cold and mineral and slightly green, which is what I want.

## What's on the plate now

- Cold-smoked trout, dry-cured 36 hours, smoked over alder and a little cherry
- Crème fraîche made in-house, salt-cured in cheesecloth for two days so it's almost like fromage blanc
- A wafer of charred kombu, brushed with brown butter
- Pickled ramp bottoms (last of the season; switching to spring onion in two weeks)
- A single drop of trout roe per plate
- Sansho oil, three drops

## What's working

The kombu wafer is the unlock. I was fighting myself for two weeks on whether to include a starch element. Beto kept pushing for crispy rice and I was getting irritated because crispy rice on a tasting menu in Portland is, in 2026, on every other tasting menu in Portland. The kombu wafer gives me the crunch and the umami without the cliché. It also nods to the Japanese side without screaming it, which is the whole problem I'm trying to solve with this menu.

The sansho is non-negotiable. Three drops. Beto wanted to dial it back; I told him no. The numbness on the back of the tongue is part of the course.

## What's not working

The crème fraîche is too sweet relative to the trout. I've been making it from Larsen's cream, which is lovely but more lactic-sweet than I want. Going to try the Garden Path cream next week — Sara said she'd send me a half-gallon. If that doesn't work I'll thin it with a touch of buttermilk culture.

The portion is one bite too big. Right now it's two bites and the second bite is the diner thinking "I wonder if there's more sansho coming." Cut it down. One bite, big enough to register all five things, small enough that the diner wants the next course immediately.

## What Lila said

Lila tasted it on Friday during family meal prep. She said, "this is the most you the menu has tasted." I asked her what that meant. She said the trout-and-roe combination, the kombu, the Pacific Northwest water mineral thing — it lands as one thing, not seven. She's right. I have not been able to say it that cleanly.

## What I'm worried about

The trout supply chain in midsummer. Ron's cold spring stays cold through about late July; by August the water warms up and the fish start tasting muddier. I have a backup plan with Lummi Island Wild for Coho, but Coho is a completely different course. If Ron's trout drops off, this course is gone for the season.

Backup-backup plan: switch to a smoked sturgeon from Pacific Pearl, which is excellent but feels more steakhouse than I want for course 3. Saving it for a rainy day.
