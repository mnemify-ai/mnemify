# Dashi Variations Test Log

**Maintained by:** Aiya (with Beto)
**Started:** 2026-04-02
**Updated:** 2026-05-26

The base dashi is showing up in at least four courses on the opening menu. The trout course uses it in the kombu wafer. The lamb glaze starts from it. The chawanmushi (whether or not it makes the menu) is built on it. The mushroom course uses it.

So the dashi has to be *right*. Not "fine, professional, what you'd get at a competent Japanese restaurant." Right.

I've been testing variations. This is the log.

## Base recipe

- 1L cold filtered water
- 30g Rishiri kombu (from the importer my mother uses, comes via a small shop in Seattle)
- 25g hanakatsuo (shaved katsuobushi) — buying from Mutual Trading in LA, shipped weekly
- Cold-steep the kombu 8 hours minimum
- Heat to 60°C, hold 1 hour with kombu in
- Remove kombu, heat to 80°C, add katsuobushi, off heat
- Steep 4 minutes, strain through cloth, do not press the bonito

This is the gold standard. I've been comparing to this.

## Variations tested

### V1: Rishiri vs. Ma-konbu (April)

Tested Rishiri (my baseline) against Ma-konbu from the same importer. Rishiri has a cleaner finish; Ma-konbu has more body and a slight sweetness. For the lamb glaze, Ma-konbu actually performed better — the sweetness rounded the bone reduction. For everything else, Rishiri wins.

**Decision:** Use Rishiri as base. Make a separate small batch with Ma-konbu for the lamb glaze.

### V2: Cold-steep duration (April)

Tested 4h, 8h, 12h, 24h cold-steep on the kombu.

- 4h: under-extracted, thin
- 8h: my baseline
- 12h: slightly more umami, slightly more bitter
- 24h: too aggressive, almost vegetal-bitter

**Decision:** 8h is correct. Stop tinkering.

### V3: Bonito brands (May)

Tested three sources of hanakatsuo:

- Mutual Trading (LA, my current): consistent, smoky, balanced
- Yamakei (NYC importer): more aggressive, deeper smoke, less balanced
- A small-producer brand I found through a Japanese specialty shop in Vancouver BC: cleaner, sweeter, less smoky

The Vancouver one is interesting. For the chawanmushi it's actually better — lets the egg sing. But it's $48/pound vs Mutual Trading's $32, and the supply is uncertain.

**Decision:** Mutual Trading as workhorse. Order 2 pounds of Vancouver for the chawanmushi specifically if we put it on the menu.

### V4: Iriko (sardine) infusion (May)

Tested adding 5g of high-quality iriko (dried sardine) during the cold-steep phase to see if it deepens umami without going fishy.

Result: yes, but only at low concentration. 5g/L gives complexity. 10g/L pushes it into a more "Japanese restaurant" flavor that I do not want for our identity.

**Decision:** Use 5g iriko in the lamb glaze dashi only. Standard dashi stays bonito-only.

### V5: Local kombu sourcing (May, ongoing)

Hannah at OreganicTea mentioned a small kombu-style seaweed harvested off the Oregon coast that someone in Astoria is processing. The aesthetic appeal is enormous — local, sustainable, on-message for the restaurant. The flavor is, sadly, not Rishiri. It's grassier, less clean, with mineral notes that are interesting but not the dashi I want.

**Decision:** Not using it for the base dashi. Going to play with it for a separate broth, possibly the mushroom course infusion.

## What I've learned

The dashi base is the spine of the menu. Get it right and the rest of the menu has somewhere to stand. Get it wrong and every course is fighting upstream.

The discipline of testing one variable at a time is the only way. I've been doing this for two months. Beto thinks I'm being obsessive. I told him: when the lamb course works perfectly on opening night, this is why.

## What I want documented

When we open and we're running 5 nights a week, the line has to make this dashi at scale without my hand on every batch. The protocol has to be written down in the kitchen binder. Beto is writing it up with my edits. Goal: any line cook on prep can make a perfect dashi batch from the binder.

Cooking restaurants live or die on the consistency of their base preparations. I have lived through one restaurant where the dashi was good when the chef made it and mediocre when he didn't. We will not be that restaurant.
