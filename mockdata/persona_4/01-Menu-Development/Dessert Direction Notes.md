# Dessert Direction Notes

**Course:** Final course (course 9 or 10)
**Owner:** Lila, with my input
**Status:** Lila is driving, I'm consulting
**Date:** 2026-05-23

Lila is the pastry chef and the dessert is hers. I have opinions but I am trying to stay out of her way. The opening menu is going to have one dessert course, possibly two if Lila wants a small pre-dessert. We've agreed on a few principles.

## Principles we've landed on

1. **No chocolate.** Portland tasting menus are 80% closed with a chocolate something. We're not doing that. Lila agreed immediately.
2. **No sorbet alone.** A scoop of seasonal sorbet is not a dessert, it's a transition course. If we use sorbet it's a component.
3. **It should taste like the season.** July dessert is going to use stone fruit. By autumn we'll be on apples and quince. By winter, citrus.
4. **One genuinely surprising element per dessert.** Could be a savory ingredient, could be a texture, could be a presentation. The diner should have one moment where they think "huh."
5. **It should be a real ending.** Not a fade-out. The last bite of the meal is the bite the diner walks out remembering. Lila has internalized this and I trust her on it.

## What Lila is working on for opening

A composed plate built around stone fruit (likely peaches from Baird Family Orchards). The current draft:

- Roasted peach half, brushed with shio koji (which she's been making for two months, deeply umami-sweet now)
- A scoop of buttermilk ice cream, churned that day
- A piece of buckwheat shortbread
- A drizzle of olive oil — yes, savory olive oil on dessert; this is the "huh" element
- Tarragon leaves, very small ones, scattered

I tasted version 3 yesterday. The shio koji on the peach is genius. It deepens the peach without sweetening it; the umami background lets the buttermilk's tang stand out. The olive oil is a question mark — half the diners are going to love it, half are going to be confused. Lila is committed and I'm letting her run with it.

## The pre-dessert question

I've been pushing Lila to add a small pre-dessert — a single bite that transitions from the savory courses. She's resisting because she doesn't want to make the meal feel longer than it needs to. We compromised: she'll prep an optional pre-dessert that we deploy on slow-pacing tables only. Diners who are eating quickly will skip directly to the dessert course.

Her pre-dessert concept: a single spoonful of yuzu granita with a tiny disc of olive oil cake. Quick, palate-cleansing, technically dessert but doesn't commit the diner to a heavy ending.

I like this. We're trying it Wednesday.

## What I'm not allowed to do

Lila told me, explicitly: "Stop suggesting Japanese flavors for the dessert." She said this with affection and also with edge. She's right — every time I sketch a dessert idea I default to yuzu/matcha/black sesame and we already have Japanese influences in 6 of the 9 courses. The dessert needs to be its own thing.

She's letting me have the shio koji on the peach because that's structural (the koji works on the peach's sugar) rather than flavor-cosplay. I appreciate her drawing the line.

## Where dessert goes in autumn

We're already sketching the autumn version (September rotation). Lila is thinking: apple, brown butter, miso, walnut. The miso is for me, she says, but actually she just likes it now. Glad we corrupted her.

For winter: citrus + cardamom + a savory hazelnut element. We'll see.

## What pairing looks like

Mateo and I had a long fight about whether to do a dessert wine pour or move to a digestif. We settled on: the menu pairing ends with the cheese (course 8), and dessert is unpaired by default. Tables who want dessert wine can order it à la carte. This keeps the price down on the pairing and lets us focus on a clean ending.

Lila approves. She says wine on dessert is "almost always wrong unless it's specifically right."

## Lila's instinct on the dessert

She said yesterday: "I want them to take the last bite and put the fork down and just sit there for a second." That's the right instinct. That's what I want every course to do, but especially this one. If they pick up the phone or start the conversation about what they're doing tomorrow, the dessert didn't land.
