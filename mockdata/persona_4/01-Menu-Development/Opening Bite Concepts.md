# Opening Bite Concepts

**Course position:** The very first thing. Sets the entire meal.
**Status:** Locked in by next Friday or we open without a finalized opener
**Date:** 2026-05-19

The opening bite is the most important bite of the night. It tells the diner what kind of restaurant they're in. It also tells the kitchen whether the night is going to go smoothly or whether we're already chasing.

I'm working through three options. Pick one by next Friday.

## Option A: Oyster + cucumber granita

- One Kumamoto oyster on the half shell
- A spoonful of cucumber granita — cucumber juice, lime, a touch of green chile, frozen and shaved
- Sansho on top, three grains
- Served on a small bed of crushed ice with a single pressed nasturtium leaf

**Pro:** Crisp, bracing, sets up the meal with cold acidity. Lets the diner know we're going to be technical without being cold.

**Con:** Half the room has had an oyster with mignonette in the last six months. Even with the granita twist, it might feel familiar.

## Option B: Chawanmushi with uni and dashi gel

- A 30g portion of chawanmushi (egg custard) in a tiny ceramic
- 5g of Hokkaido uni on top
- A wobble of dashi gel beside it
- Yuzu zest, microplaned, the smallest amount

**Pro:** This is the dish I'm proudest of, technique-wise. The custard is impossibly silky if you get it right. The uni-dashi-yuzu is the trio I've been chasing for years.

**Con:** This is course three of a tasting menu, not the opener. It's too big a flavor statement to start with. Diner is hungry and not yet receptive to a meditation. Better to save for later in the meal.

## Option C: Cured fluke + green strawberry

- One slice of cured fluke, cured 4 hours in salt and kombu
- A halved green (unripe) strawberry — they have an almost cucumber-like crunch, faintly tart
- A dot of avocado oil
- Maldon flake, two grains

**Pro:** Surprising. Green strawberries are a real thing in early summer and almost no one in Portland is using them. Light, brings the diner in without overwhelming.

**Con:** Fragile course. The fluke has to be cut at service, the strawberry has to be sourced fresh that morning. Lila pointed out that green strawberries from our supplier are only consistent for about 5 weeks of the year. We open in mid-July; green strawberries probably gone by mid-August.

## What I'm leaning

Option C through August, then transition to Option A in fall when oysters are at their best. Option B never makes it to the opener — it's a course 3 dish.

The transition becomes part of the story: the menu is seasonal in a way that's not just "we use seasonal ingredients" but actually changes the architecture of the meal. Returning diners notice.

## What's stopping me from committing

The fluke. I have a line on it through Northwest Wild Caught but it's not consistent. Some weeks they have plenty, some weeks they have none and it's halibut. Halibut works for this course but the texture is different — chewier, less delicate. I might need to alternate based on what's coming in that morning.

This is the kind of operational ambiguity that drives Beto crazy. He wants the menu locked. He says: "How does the line prep when we don't know what's coming until 9 AM?" The answer is: we don't pre-prep. We adjust morning of. The line has to be trained to flex.

Which is going to be a training challenge.

## Decision deadline

Friday May 30. I'll do a full tasting with the team on Thursday and we'll vote. Beto, Lila, Mateo, Caro, me. Five votes. Whatever wins, wins.

If we tie 2-2 with one abstention I get to break the tie. So I'm voting last.

## What I'm reminding myself

The opener should taste like the restaurant. Not like the chef. The diner doesn't care that I'm Aiya Tanaka-Brown and I worked at Atomix and I have a particular thing for Japanese vinegars. They care that this bite was the right way to start their meal.

Make it the right way to start.
