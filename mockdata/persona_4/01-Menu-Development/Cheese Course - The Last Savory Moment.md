# Cheese Course — The Last Savory Moment

**Course:** Course 8 — between lamb and dessert
**Status:** Mostly locked
**Date:** 2026-05-20

The cheese course is a transition. It pulls the diner from the warmth of the lamb to the lightness of the dessert. Most tasting menus do a cheese plate that tries to teach the diner about cheese, which is wrong. The diner does not want a lesson. The diner wants a moment.

## The plate

- One cheese only
- A 25g portion (small)
- Served at room temperature, sat out 45 minutes before service
- A small honeycomb chunk on the side
- Three slices of black radish, cut on a mandoline, soaked in cold water for crispness
- A scattering of toasted spelt grains
- A drizzle of brown butter, cool but liquid

## The cheese

I have been going back and forth between three:

- **A Pacific Northwest sheep's milk** from Ancient Heritage Dairy near Madras. Funky, washed-rind, deeply local. Sometimes hard to source consistently. My instinct says this is the right cheese for opening.
- **A young Comté** from Marcel Petite. Reliable. Beautiful. But not distinctive — every cheese course in America uses Comté.
- **A blue from Rogue Creamery** — Rogue River Blue when in season, Caveman Blue otherwise. Bold. Risk: blue cheese is a polarizing way to close the savory part of the meal.

Going with the Ancient Heritage. Diane raised an eyebrow when I told her — she thinks the supply will hurt us. I'm betting it won't. Anna and David at Ancient Heritage have promised me 6 pounds a week minimum, which gives me about 100 covers worth of cheese course per week, slightly above what I need.

## Why one cheese instead of a plate

A plate of three cheeses is a tasting flight. It asks the diner to compare. By course 8 the diner is not in a comparison mood. They want a single, perfect moment of cheese.

One excellent cheese, served correctly, is more luxurious than three good cheeses arranged on slate.

## The honey and the radish

The honey is from a beekeeper in Hood River. Lighter than the standard buckwheat honey, with stone fruit notes. The honeycomb on the plate gives a textural element (the wax) and a small sweetness that meets the cheese.

The black radish is what nobody expects. Watery, slightly peppery, crisp, almost neutral. It cuts the richness of the cheese. The first time I tested it with a cheese course, three years ago at a Brooklyn pop-up, a diner said "I have never tasted black radish before in my life and I'm going to start growing it." I have not forgotten that.

## The brown butter

This is the element I am proudest of. Brown butter, cooled but still liquid, drizzled in a small line across the plate. The toasted nuttiness of the brown butter takes the cheese in a savory direction that delays the dessert transition.

Risk: brown butter at room temperature can solidify on the plate. Need to time the drizzle precisely. Beto has a small heated squeeze bottle just for this.

## What I do not put on the plate

- Bread or crackers. The diner has had bread earlier. They don't need it again.
- Quince paste. Too sweet, redirects the diner toward dessert too early.
- Fig. Same problem.
- Walnuts or other large nuts. Throws off the texture balance.

The spelt grains are the carb. The honeycomb is the sweet. The radish is the cut. Nothing else.

## Pairing

Mateo wants a small pour (60ml) of a Tokaji Aszú 5 puttonyos. I told him no — too sweet, transitions too sharply into dessert. We landed on a 60ml pour of a Madeira (a 10-year Rainwater) which is balanced enough to bridge the cheese into the dessert.

Actually that's also the last wine pour. The menu pairing ends here. Dessert is unpaired. (Lila and Mateo agree on this.)

## What I want the diner to feel

Settled. Grounded. The lamb was the climax. The cheese is the deep breath after. The dessert is the small surprising ending. The cheese course is the moment when they exhale.

If we get the cheese right — temperature correct, portion correct, honeycomb fresh, radish crisp — the diner is in exactly the right state of mind for what Lila is going to bring them next.

## Service note

The server should not narrate the cheese. The plate goes down, the server says "Ancient Heritage Dairy, near Madras." That's it. The diner gets to be alone with the cheese for a moment. Excessive narration on this course will kill the breath I'm trying to create.
