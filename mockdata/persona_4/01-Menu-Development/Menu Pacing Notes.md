# Menu Pacing Notes

**Maintained by:** Aiya, with Caro and Beto
**Status:** Working draft after soft opening 2
**Date:** 2026-05-24

The pacing of the meal is as important as the food. A 9-course tasting menu that takes 3.5 hours is too long. A 9-course tasting menu that takes 1.5 hours is too short. The right answer is approximately 2 hours 20 minutes for a standard table of 2.

After the second soft opening we have data. We need to tighten.

## Current pacing data (from soft opening 2)

| Course | Target time from seating | Actual avg | Variance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening bite | 0:08 | 0:11 | +3 min |
| Bread moment | 0:15 | 0:14 | -1 min |
| Course 3 (trout) | 0:24 | 0:22 | -2 min |
| Course 4 (salad) | 0:38 | 0:35 | -3 min |
| Course 5 (mushroom) | 0:52 | 0:48 | -4 min |
| Course 6 (halibut/sturgeon) | 1:08 | 1:14 | +6 min |
| Course 7 (lamb) | 1:30 | 1:42 | +12 min |
| Course 8 (cheese) | 1:50 | 2:05 | +15 min |
| Course 9 (dessert) | 2:10 | 2:28 | +18 min |

So we're losing time in the back half. The front half is moving fast (maybe too fast) and the back half is dragging.

## Why the back half drags

Three reasons:

1. **The lamb is slow off the line.** Beto's been doing the lamb himself and he's plating each lamb course individually. He has to learn to plate two at a time once we're at full service capacity.
2. **The cheese course is slow because we're warming each portion individually.** Need a better warming protocol — pull from walk-in 45 min before, sit out at pass.
3. **Conversation pace at tables 3 and 7 was unusually slow.** Those tables added about 8 minutes to the back half. This is unavoidable — some tables are slower talkers. The pacing has to accommodate.

## Why the front half is fast

The opener and trout courses are mostly assembly. Once the components are prepped, plating is fast. The diner is also still in the "getting oriented" phase and isn't lingering between bites.

This is actually fine. A fast front half lets the diner settle. The middle and back can slow down.

## What I want to change

- **Lamb plating speed.** Beto trains to plate 4 at a time by next Friday. We test this in soft opening 3.
- **Cheese course pull timing.** Caro updates the FOH protocol: cheese pulled at "course 6 fired." This gives 30 minutes for the cheese to come to temperature.
- **Dessert pacing.** Lila and I look at whether the dessert plating can be simplified. The composed plate is taking too long to assemble at the pass.

## What pacing teaches me about the kitchen

The pacing data is the truest read of how the kitchen is performing. When a course is consistently late by 5+ minutes, something structural is wrong. When a course is fast and consistent, the team has it dialed.

I'm going to keep this pacing log for every service after we open. Caro maintains it. Each Sunday I review.

This is one of the most boring parts of running a restaurant. It is also one of the most consequential.

## What Caro pointed out about service rhythm

She said: "The diner doesn't know that course 7 is supposed to come at 1:30. The diner knows that course 7 is coming too late if they finish their conversation and look around impatiently."

She's right. The pacing is good when no diner is looking around. The pacing is bad when even one diner is looking around.

Going to instrument this. Caro is going to log "looking-around moments" per service. If any diner looks around impatiently more than once per service, we have a pacing problem.

## On the timer culture

Beto wants a timer at the pass for each course. Visible. Counting up from when the course was fired.

I'm against it. The timer trains the kitchen to chase the clock instead of the dish. We watch the dish, we watch the diner, we plate when both are ready.

Compromise: a discreet stopwatch at the pass. Beto can use it. The cooks don't see it.

## The bigger truth

The pacing of a tasting menu is choreography. We are writing a dance and the diner is the dance partner. If we step too fast we leave them behind. If we step too slow they get bored.

The right pacing is when the diner doesn't notice the pacing at all.

That's the goal.
