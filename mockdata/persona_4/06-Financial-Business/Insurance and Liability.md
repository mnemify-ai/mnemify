# Insurance and Liability

**Status:** Active; bound May 2026
**Date:** 2026-05-04
**Broker:** Wendy Ortiz (Hospitality Insurance Group)

What we have, why we have it, and what it costs.

## Policies in place

### General liability
- $2M per occurrence / $4M aggregate
- Annual premium: $4,800
- Standard for our size

### Property
- Coverage: $500K business personal property + $100K improvements
- Annual premium: $3,200
- Includes equipment, inventory, build-out improvements

### Liquor liability
- $1M per occurrence
- Annual premium: $2,800
- Required for our liquor license

### Workers compensation
- Coverage per Oregon state requirements
- Estimated annual premium: $4,500 (varies with payroll)
- Required by law

### Business interruption
- 12 months coverage
- Annual premium: $1,800
- Covers loss of revenue from a covered event (fire, etc.)

### Cyber liability
- $250K coverage
- Annual premium: $900
- For data breach, POS hack, customer data exposure

### Employment practices liability
- $1M coverage
- Annual premium: $2,400
- For wrongful termination, harassment claims, etc.

### Umbrella
- $5M
- Annual premium: $2,400

**Total annual insurance: ~$22,800**

## What's covered (key scenarios)

- Diner injured at the restaurant (general liability)
- Diner gets sick (general liability; food poisoning is covered)
- Drunk diner injures someone after leaving (liquor liability)
- Fire damages the kitchen (property, plus business interruption for lost revenue)
- Employee gets injured at work (workers comp)
- Sexual harassment claim (employment practices)
- POS gets hacked, diner credit card info exposed (cyber)
- Major lawsuit exceeding standard limits (umbrella)

## What's not covered

- Earthquakes (separate policy; I have not bought it)
- Floods (separate policy; we're not in a flood zone but a sewer backup could be ugly)
- Acts of war
- Some intentional acts
- Damage to the building itself (that's the landlord's responsibility)

## What I wish was cheaper

The liquor liability premium is high relative to our wine-pour exposure. We pour 12 small wines per diner; our drunk-driving risk is real but small. The premium is calibrated for places that get diners drunker.

I am considering a policy review at year 2 to see if we can negotiate.

## What I made sure to add

Employment practices liability. Most small restaurants don't carry this. I do because I have read about too many cases where a single harassment claim destroyed a small business.

The premium ($2,400) is real money. It is the cheapest insurance I bought.

## Claims process

If something happens:
1. Caro or I document immediately (photos, witness statements, written log)
2. We call Wendy (broker) within 24h
3. Wendy guides us to the carrier's claims process
4. We do not admit fault to the diner; we acknowledge concern and offer to make it right within the restaurant
5. Anything beyond a comp or replacement goes through Wendy

## What I do at the restaurant level

### Slip and fall prevention
- Wet floor signs visible and used
- Floors cleaned during low-traffic moments
- Staff trained to walk arms-free in busy moments

### Allergen handling
- Documented process (Allergy and Diet Protocol)
- Server confirms with diner
- Kitchen flags allergen dishes
- Severe allergies isolated in prep

### Drunk diner handling
- Trained servers to recognize over-service signs
- Trained to refuse service politely
- Offered to call a ride for any diner who seems too far gone
- Never argue with a drunk diner; involve management

### Worker safety
- Heat protocols (kitchen has cooling station)
- Sharp safety (knife protocol)
- Slip safety (kitchen floors are not slippery — surface choice matters)
- Burn first-aid kit accessible

## The lawsuit I haven't had yet but expect

A diner will eventually claim food poisoning. The math says this happens. Most are false (they got sick from something else; restaurant is the easy target). A few are real.

When it happens:
- Listen, do not argue
- Get medical records if they offer
- Get an internal review of that night's prep, holding temps, service
- Notify the broker
- If it's real, take responsibility and fix the process
- If it's not, defend it without being defensive

I have not had a food poisoning incident in 10 years of professional cooking. Beto has not either. Our walk-in is rigorous. Our temperature logging is automated. But the math says: someday.
