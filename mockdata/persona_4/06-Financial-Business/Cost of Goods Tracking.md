# Cost of Goods Tracking

**Status:** Active for soft opening
**Date:** 2026-05-24

How we track food and beverage cost. The most important operational metric.

## Targets

- Food cost: 28% of food revenue
- Wine cost: 32% of wine revenue
- Blended: ~30% of total revenue

These are aspirational. Year 1 target: 30% food, 35% wine. Will tighten in year 2 as we learn supply chain.

## What we're tracking

### Per-plate food cost
For each dish on the menu, I have calculated the ingredient cost down to the gram.

Example — Opening Bite (smoked trout, salt-cured yolk, nori cracker):
- Trout: 8g of $42/kg trout = $0.34
- Egg yolk: $0.18 (factor in 4 yolks per dozen)
- Nori cracker: $0.22 (nori, flour, oil)
- Salt, oil: $0.05
- Plating waste 10%: $0.08
- **Total: $0.87 per plate**

At $185 menu / 12 courses = $15.40 per course retail. $0.87 cost is 5.6%. Very low. Average across all courses.

### Per-course retail
Each course retails at the same blended cost. Different courses have very different costs. The lamb course is $9.50 ingredient cost (highest). The bread course is $0.40 ingredient cost (lowest). The blend hits 28% food cost on the menu.

### Wine pour cost
For each wine in the pairing program:
- Bottle cost
- Pours per bottle (4 for the 3oz pairing pour)
- Cost per pour

The full 12-pour pairing has a total wine cost of ~$45. We retail at $145. 31% wine cost. On target.

## Daily tracking sheet

Beto or I update daily:
- Covers served (by menu version: omnivore, vegetarian, vegan, special diet)
- Total food revenue
- Total wine revenue
- Actual ingredient spend that day (from invoices)
- Calculated food cost % (rough)

Weekly: deeper reconciliation with QuickBooks.

## Where waste happens

- Trim and yield: even a careful butcher loses 30% on lamb shoulder
- Mistakes: a course that gets sent back, replated, or comped
- Cooking error: a sauce that breaks, a fish that overcooks
- Storage failure: produce that wilts before service
- Family meal use of remaining trim (we use a LOT for family meal — this is intentional and recovers some yield)

We track waste informally. End of each week, Beto and I review what got thrown out and what could have been used.

## Where waste doesn't happen at Lark

- Theft (we don't tolerate it; the team knows)
- Over-portioning (the tasting menu format means portions are tight; less drift)
- Comp inflation (Caro reviews every comp; gratuitous comping doesn't happen)

## Specific COGS concerns

### Specialty ingredients
Some ingredients are expensive and have unpredictable supply:
- Geoduck (variable; $80/lb when good)
- Matsutake (in season; $50/lb)
- Specific cheeses (Adelle is $36/lb)
- Fresh wasabi (steady price; $250/lb)

These don't appear daily. They flex with the season.

### Wine inventory
Mateo manages wine inventory. He keeps about 6 weeks of pour inventory at all times. We invest about $35K in wine inventory at any time.

### Imported items (Mutual Trading)
Yuzu kosho, sansho, specific shoyu — imported monthly. Holding 2 months' supply at all times because of import lead time.

## What I look at weekly

- Food cost % (target 28-32%)
- Wine cost % (target 32-38%)
- Top 3 spend categories
- Any anomalous spike

## What I look at monthly

- Yearly trends
- Specific cost drift on key ingredients
- Whether prices from suppliers are creeping up
- Whether we need to renegotiate or find alternatives

## Personal note

I came up in kitchens where food cost was the chef's problem and the chef yelled about it. The yelling didn't actually fix the cost. The discipline did.

I learned to track cost as a habit, not an emergency. The chef who tracks cost daily catches problems before they get big. The chef who tracks once a quarter is always firefighting.

I am tracking daily. So is Beto.
