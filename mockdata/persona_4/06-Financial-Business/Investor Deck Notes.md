# Investor Deck Notes

**Status:** Pitched (Sept 2025); revisiting for year 2 expansion conversation
**Date original:** 2025-08-22
**Date revised:** 2026-04-30

Notes from the deck we used to raise Lark's seed capital from Diane and three small angels.

## The raise

- $850,000 total raise
- $600,000 from Diane (lead)
- $250,000 from three angels ($75K, $100K, $75K)
- Structure: SAFE notes converting at exit or refinance event
- Diane has a board seat; angels do not

## The pitch in one sentence

Lark is a 28-seat Pacific Northwest tasting menu restaurant built around a Japanese discipline and an unfussy heart, with a strong team and a viable financial model.

## What I emphasized

- The team (Beto, Caro, Mateo already committed)
- The build-out cost (modest because of the space)
- The unit economics (small space + high check)
- The five-year horizon (not a quick exit)

## What I deemphasized

- The 80% failure rate of restaurants (Diane raised it; I addressed it)
- My lack of prior ownership experience (I had to be honest)
- The competitive landscape (Portland has many tasting menus; I framed it as "the market is here")

## The key slides (in order)

1. The food (high-quality images of dishes from a private dinner)
2. The team
3. The space and build-out plan
4. The financials (year 1 projection at multiple occupancies)
5. The customer (who comes to Lark and why)
6. The 5-year plan (steady, no rapid expansion)
7. The ask

## Diane's questions during pitch

- "What happens if Beto leaves?" — succession plan; I have a sous chef relationship at Single Thread who would consider it.
- "What if Mateo and you break up?" — honest answer: harder for me personally; the wine program continues.
- "What happens if your mother needs care?" — I framed honestly; this is in my life and Diane respects it.
- "What's your line on the menu price?" — I said $185 is my line; she pushed for $195; we ended at $185.

The hardest question was the one about Mateo. I was direct. Diane appreciated it.

## What sealed it for Diane

She came to a private dinner I cooked in late August 2025. 12 people. Full tasting menu. She tasted, asked questions, watched the room.

After: "You are not a chef who pretends. The food is what you say it is. I'm in."

She wrote the lead check the next week.

## Why the angels came in

Angel 1 (Stefan, runs a wine import business): friend of Mateo. Did it on Mateo's wine program reputation.
Angel 2 (Lisa, lawyer, food lover): regular at my old pop-ups. Came in on the food.
Angel 3 (Sam, retired restaurant entrepreneur): Diane introduced. Came in because Diane was in.

All three are quiet investors. They want monthly updates and a great meal occasionally. They are not operationally involved.

## What I want to do in year 2

- Open a second small concept (NOT a Lark expansion; something simpler, possibly counter-only)
- Use Lark as the brand anchor
- Reinvest year 1 net + a small raise from existing investors

I have not discussed this with Diane yet. She might say not yet.

## What I would do differently in the original raise

- I would have asked for $1M instead of $850K
- I would have left more room for unexpected costs (the build-out is already $80K over)
- I would have negotiated equity terms harder (SAFE at lower cap)

These are lessons. Year 2 raise (if there is one) will be different.

## Personal note

The day Diane signed the lead check I went home and cried.

It wasn't that I'd raised money. It was that someone with her track record and discernment believed in the work. Money is money. Belief is the real currency.

I have to make this restaurant be worth her belief.
