# Year 1 Financial Plan

**Status:** Locked with Diane
**Date:** 2026-05-05
**Owner:** Diane Park (investor) and me

The financial plan for Lark's first 12 months of operation. Conservative.

## Revenue model

- 28 seats, 5 nights per week (Tue-Sat closed Sun-Mon)
- Two seatings most nights, one seating Tuesdays
- Avg covers per night: 50 (full house = 56)
- Year 1 occupancy assumption: 75%
- Avg spend per cover: $260 (tasting + median pairing + tax + tip)

### Annual revenue projection
- 50 covers × 5 nights × 50 weeks × 75% × $260
- = $1,219,000

Conservative because:
- First month at 50% occupancy (ramp)
- Closed 2 weeks for kitchen reset and team rest (planned)
- No assumption of weekend premium pricing (we are not doing it year 1)

## Cost structure

### Cost of goods (food and wine)
- Food cost target: 28% of food revenue
- Wine cost target: 32% of wine revenue
- Blended COGS: ~30% of total revenue
- Annual COGS: $365,000

### Labor
- Total labor cost target: 38% (high; deliberate)
- Includes my salary, Beto's salary, Caro's salary, Mateo, Lila, line cooks, FOH, host
- Annual labor: $463,000

### Rent and utilities
- Rent: $8,500/month = $102,000/year
- Utilities (gas, electric, water): ~$2,500/month = $30,000/year
- Total: $132,000

### Other operating
- Linens, china, glassware replenishment: $24,000
- Equipment maintenance: $12,000
- Insurance: $18,000
- Cleaning supplies: $9,000
- Marketing (minimal): $15,000
- Accounting and legal: $14,000
- Software (POS, reservations, etc.): $8,000
- Other: $20,000
- Total: $120,000

### Total expenses
$1,080,000

### Net (year 1)
$139,000

### After Diane's preferred return (12%)
$104,000 to operations / reinvestment

This is positive year 1. Most restaurants are not.

## Why we project positive

- High average check ($260) — tasting menu economics
- Small space (28 seats) — manageable labor
- Strong pre-opening reservations (sold out through August)
- Diane's negotiated rent ($8,500/mo for the space; below market)
- Conservative occupancy (75%; we could hit 85%)

## Risk factors

- Slower ramp than projected
- Higher COGS due to supplier issues (sturgeon situation is example)
- Service complaint/online review storm in first weeks
- Staff turnover (one departure costs ~$8K)
- Lease costs unexpected (we have not had a winter yet; heat bills unknown)

## What we'll watch monthly

- Cover count and occupancy
- Average check
- Food cost as % of food revenue
- Wine cost as % of wine revenue
- Labor cost as % of revenue
- Cash balance and runway

Diane and I meet first Tuesday of each month to review.

## What success looks like (year 1)

- 12 months operating without missing payroll
- 75-80% occupancy by month 3
- Food cost holding at 28%
- A team that's intact (no major turnover in year 1)
- A clear plan for year 2 by month 9

## What worries me

- The shoulder months (October, January) when reservations may slow
- The cost of the small-space tasting menu format if labor cost can't be controlled
- The first significant negative review or social media incident

Diane's perspective on the worry: "Every restaurant has a near-death moment in year 1. You will too. Don't let it knock you off course."

## Personal note

I have never built a financial plan for a restaurant of this scale. Diane is teaching me. She is patient. She is also clear that I need to learn to run the numbers myself within 6 months.

The number that matters: $139,000 net in year 1. That is the buffer that lets us reinvest and survive the bad months. We are aiming for that number with discipline.
