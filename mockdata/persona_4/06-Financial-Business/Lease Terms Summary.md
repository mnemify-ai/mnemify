# Lease Terms Summary

**Status:** Signed
**Date signed:** 2025-10-12
**Term:** 7 years with two 5-year renewal options

The lease for Lark's space.

## The space

- 1,850 square feet
- Single-story, corner unit
- Inner SE Portland, off a quiet street
- Built in the 1920s; substantial brick exterior
- Was previously a bakery (closed 2024)

## The rent

- Year 1: $8,500/month base ($102K/year)
- Years 2-3: $8,800/month
- Years 4-5: $9,200/month
- Years 6-7: $9,600/month
- Renewal: market rate at year 8 with cap at +15% of year 7 rent

Triple-net: I pay property tax, insurance, and common area maintenance. Total at year 1: ~$2,800/month.

All-in monthly: ~$11,300.

## Why we got a good deal

Diane negotiated. She knew the landlord (Frank Mathieson) from her own restaurants. Frank wanted a tenant who would stay and make the building a destination. He gave us:

- Below-market base rent
- 4 months free during build-out
- $30K landlord contribution to build-out (HVAC and electrical)
- Right of first refusal on the unit next door (currently a small antique shop with 4 years left)

In exchange:
- 7-year initial term (longer than I wanted; Diane was right that this is a moat)
- Personal guarantee from me for 12 months of rent in default
- Diane co-guaranteed for an additional 12 months

The personal guarantee is the risk. If Lark fails in year 1, I am personally on the hook for ~$135K of rent.

## What I read in the lease (line by line)

- Use restriction: full-service restaurant, no fast food, no franchise
- Operating hours: no restriction (we open dinner only)
- Signage: limited to subtle storefront; no neon (matches my aesthetic anyway)
- Modifications: any structural changes require landlord approval (we got pre-approval on our build-out plan)
- Termination clause: 90 days written notice from either party if material breach; otherwise must complete term
- Subletting: prohibited without landlord consent

Diane's lawyer reviewed. He noted:
- The personal guarantee is normal for first-time operators but is a real risk
- The right of first refusal on the next-door space is genuinely valuable
- The triple-net terms are standard

## Negotiating points I'd remember for next time

- Always negotiate free build-out months (we got 4; I would push for 6)
- Always negotiate the landlord contribution (we got $30K; some landlords do more for the right tenant)
- Always negotiate the right of first refusal on adjacent space
- Cap the personal guarantee timeline (we limited ours to 12 months; this was key)

## What I worry about

- The personal guarantee
- The 7-year commitment is long if the concept doesn't work
- The renewal language ("market rate") could be brutal in year 8

## What I'm grateful for

- Frank is a good landlord. He has been responsive during build-out (electrical issue Tuesday; he was on site Wednesday morning).
- Diane's relationship made this possible.
- The space is the right shape. Tasting menus need a specific geometry. This works.

## The space's history

- 1923: Built as a bakery (Hellberg Brothers, Norwegian immigrants)
- 1942-1968: Bakery, then deli
- 1968-1994: Closed for years; intermittent use
- 1994-2018: Coffee shop (locally famous one called Brew Box)
- 2018-2024: Bakery again (a respected Portland bakery that lost its lease elsewhere)
- 2024-2025: Vacant
- 2026: Lark

I like the lineage. Food was made here for 100 years. Now ours.

## Personal note

I had nightmares about signing this lease. The personal guarantee is real. If Lark fails, I am paying for empty space for a year.

I signed anyway. The thing about being 38 and having spent every year of your career working toward this — there is no path to a different life that doesn't go through this signature.

I keep the lease in a binder on my desk. Sometimes I open it just to remind myself it's real.
