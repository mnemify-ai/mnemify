# Year 2 Planning Notes

**Status:** Early-stage thinking; not committed
**Date:** 2026-05-15

Notes for year 2 planning. Most of this is speculative. Real planning happens at month 9 of year 1.

## What year 2 looks like if year 1 is steady

If Lark hits the year 1 projections, year 2 is:
- Revenue: $1.4M (occupancy improves to 85%)
- Net: $200K (operating leverage as fixed costs stay flat)
- Team stable
- Menu evolved through 4 seasons
- Reputation building

## Strategic options for year 2

### Option A: Hold steady (recommended by Diane)
- Run Lark
- Optimize operations
- Build reputation
- Don't expand
- Invest in team development
- Modest staff raises

This is the conservative path. Diane is firm here.

### Option B: A second concept
- Smaller, simpler — counter only
- Could be a 12-seat counter at lunch or early dinner
- Pacific Northwest sushi-focused (different from Lark's identity but related)
- I have ideas for this; have not committed
- Would require ~$300K raise
- Earliest: late year 2 if Lark is steady

### Option C: Expanded Lark
- Add the next-door space (right of first refusal)
- Make Lark larger (40-50 seats)
- Add private dining
- Higher cost; same identity

I'm against this. Lark works because it's small.

### Option D: Cookbook / external presence
- Cookbook deal (no investment, all upside)
- Pop-ups at other restaurants in other cities
- Build name beyond Portland
- This is low-risk, low-investment

This may be the year 2 move depending on press traction.

## My current thinking (not final)

Year 2 priorities:
1. Operations excellence at Lark (the core)
2. Begin exploration of the Option B counter concept (low-burn, low-pressure)
3. Pursue cookbook conversations (passive)
4. Stay open to opportunities I can't predict

I want to grow but not greedily. The five-year horizon is more important than year 2 specifically.

## What Diane will say

I know what Diane will say at month 9 (because she said versions of it already):
- "Focus on Lark"
- "Don't expand until Lark is boring"
- "Boring means: you can take 2 weeks off and the food doesn't slip"
- "If Lark is boring, then talk about the second thing"

I will listen. She's been right so far.

## What Mateo will say

Mateo wants to invest in the wine program first. Add a sommelier hour. Build wine education events. Sell wine retail.

Some of this is feasible. We will discuss in October.

## What Beto will say

Beto wants menu freedom. He wants to push the menu further into Japanese territory than I have. He wants to do a tasting menu that runs alongside the main menu, more experimental.

I love this idea. It is also probably year 3, not year 2.

## What Caro will say

Caro wants more recognition for her work. She earned it. I am working on what that looks like — possibly equity in the business, possibly a meaningful raise.

Plan to discuss in November when the dust settles.

## What I worry about for year 2

- Restaurant openings often peak in year 1 and decline in year 2 if the chef-owner gets distracted
- Burnout is real; I will be tired
- Press attention fades; we have to keep earning it
- Cost inflation (suppliers, rent steps up in year 4 but watch costs continuously)
- Staff retention through the second year is critical

## What I am hopeful about for year 2

- The team will be tighter
- The menu will be deeper
- The customer base will know us
- The kitchen will have rhythm
- I will be a better operator

## The big questions

- Do we expand? (probably no in year 2)
- Do we add lunch? (no; it would dilute identity)
- Do we change the format? (no)
- Do we publish? (maybe; cookbook conversations to explore)
- Do we grow the team? (yes; one more line cook by month 6, possibly a captain promotion)

## Personal note

Year 2 is when most chefs make the mistakes that hurt them long-term. The mistakes are usually about ambition outpacing operations.

I want to remember: the goal is not to build an empire. The goal is to make a restaurant that lasts and feeds people well.

Lark is enough for year 2.
