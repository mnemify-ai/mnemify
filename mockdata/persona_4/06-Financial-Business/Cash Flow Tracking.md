Every Monday morning, I update the tracking sheet. Diane and I discuss every other Monday.

## What I look at on the sheet

### Inflows (post-opening)
- Reservations deposit revenue (we take $50 per cover as deposit; refundable up to 24h before)
- Cover charges (collected on night of)
- Wine pairings (collected on night of)
- Beverage other (cocktails, additional wine)
- Tips (collected and held; distributed weekly)

### Outflows
- Payroll (every 2 weeks)
- COGS (food, wine, beverages — invoiced weekly)
- Rent + triple net (monthly)
- Utilities (monthly)
- Insurance (quarterly)
- Other operating (variable)
- Investor distributions (year 2+)

### Cash position
Open and close cash for each week.

### Anomalies log
Anything unusual (equipment break, big special order, single big-spend table).

## Tools

The accountant (Sarah Min, recommended by Diane) reconciles monthly.

## What I've already learned

- Forecasting is hard. My April forecast was off by 15%.
- Suppliers' invoicing cycles vary wildly. Some weekly, some monthly. The lumpiness matters.
- Equipment surprises are real. The dishwasher repair last week cost $1,200 — not in budget.
- Build-out costs always exceed estimate. Always.

## What I'm learning to do better

- Reading the bank balance is not the same as reading cash flow. Bank balance is a snapshot. Cash flow is a movement.
- "Available cash" is what's in the account minus what I know I owe in the next 14 days. That's the number that matters.
- Diane says: "If you don't know your true cash position within $1,000 by Tuesday morning, you're not managing cash."

I am working on this. I am not there yet.

## The discipline

Every Monday, 7am, I sit with coffee and update the sheet. No exceptions.

I do not delegate this to the accountant or to Caro. The chef-owner has to know the cash position personally.

## Personal note

I am a chef. I am not naturally a cash-flow person. I had to be taught.

Diane gave me a book called "Profit First" that changed how I think about cash. The core idea: pay yourself first, set aside taxes first, then operate on what's left.

I'm not fully running this system yet, but I am closer than I was 6 months ago. The principle: cash is not just money. Cash is the ability to keep the doors open. Treat it with care.
