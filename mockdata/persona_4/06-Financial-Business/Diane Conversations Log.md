# Diane Conversations Log

**Status:** Ongoing
**Date started:** 2025-09-01

Notes from substantive conversations with Diane Park (lead investor, Portland restaurateur, partner in this project). I keep this log because she gives me a lot of high-quality input and I forget specifics if I don't write them down.

## 2025-09-15: Why she invested

Diane: "I've watched a lot of chefs try to open restaurants. The ones who succeed have three things. First, they can cook. That's easier than it sounds. Second, they can run a team. That's harder. Third, they understand that running a business is its own discipline. That last one is where most fail. I think you have the first two and you're teachable on the third."

What I'm taking from this: don't pretend I know the business side. Ask. Learn.

## 2025-11-04: On the menu

After a private dinner where she came to taste version 2:

"The menu is too long. You're not trusting the diner to be satisfied. You're trying to give them everything."

Cut from 14 to 12 courses. She was right.

## 2026-01-22: On hiring

"Pay above market. Don't ever skimp on people. The cost of a bad hire and the cost of high turnover are both higher than paying $2 more an hour."

Set the line cook wage scale immediately after this conversation.

## 2026-02-18: On me

"You have a quality I see in the best chefs and don't see in the average ones. You actually care if it's good, not whether people think it's good. That's rarer than you realize."

I held onto this when I was stressed about the build-out.

## 2026-03-09: On Beto

After Beto's trial day, before we offered him the role:

"He's the right person. I can tell from how he moves. Pay him well. Trust him with the kitchen when you're not there. Watch for whether he can grow with you over five years, not just two."

She was right.

## 2026-03-25: On Mateo and me

She brought this up. She'd been thinking.

"Working with your partner in life is the hardest thing you can do in a small business. Most of these end badly. Either the business breaks the relationship or the relationship breaks the business. Sometimes both. I want you to think about what happens when there's a conflict between you and Mateo and the business is in the middle."

I told her: we have rules. We don't argue about business at home. We don't argue about home in the business. We make explicit decisions about who has authority over what.

She said: "Good. Hold to those rules. Especially the first one."

## 2026-04-08: On the build-out overrun

We were $80K over the original build-out budget. I was upset.

Diane: "Eighty thousand. Yes, it's real. Did you know I was 24% over on my third restaurant? It happens. The question is whether the overrun reflects bad planning or unexpected reality. If it's unexpected reality, that's life. If it's bad planning, learn for next time."

In our case, it was unexpected reality (HVAC complications, permit costs, supplier delays). She accepted it. She didn't yell. She did make me walk through what I'd do differently next time.

## 2026-04-20: On expansion

I floated the idea of a second concept in year 2.

Diane: "Not in year 2. Not until Lark is steady and you have a clear succession plan in place at Lark. Earliest year 3. And only if the concept is genuinely different, not a Lark spin-off."

I respected the answer. She's right. I'm getting ahead of myself.

## 2026-05-12: On opening date

I was anxious about the soft opening schedule.

Diane: "Don't slip the opening. Open July 12 unless something catastrophic is broken. You will not be ready in the way you imagine. No restaurant is ever ready for opening night. You just open and you adjust. The diner who comes night one will get a real meal but not the final form of Lark. They know this. They're coming because they want to be there at the start."

This was important to hear. I had been pushing for July 19 or July 26. I'm holding to July 12.

## 2026-05-21: On after-opening burnout

This was unsolicited.

Diane: "I want you to plan for what happens at month 4. You will be exhausted. You will have given everything to the opening. Around month 4 most chef-owners hit a wall. The food slips. The team feels it. Plan for a week off in November. Beto can run the kitchen. You take a real break. Not a working break."

I'm planning Thanksgiving week off. Going to visit my mother in Seattle.

## What I've learned from Diane

- The business and the food are different disciplines; both have to be good
- People are the most important investment; don't skimp
- Plan for the bad days, not just the good
- Don't expand until the core is steady
- Working with your partner is hard; have rules
- The diner is forgiving in the opening weeks
- Take care of yourself or the food slips

## Personal note

Diane is the best mentor I have ever had. I am lucky to have her. I want her to be proud of what we build.

She would never describe herself as a mentor — she'd say she's an investor who wants returns. Both are true.
