# Family Meal Philosophy

**Status:** Locked
**Date:** 2026-05-19

Family meal is the meal the staff eats together before service. It is the most important meal of the day at a restaurant.

## Why family meal matters

- It's the only time the entire team sits together
- It sets the energy for service
- It's the moment to address the day's issues
- It's where we taste each other's cooking
- It's where culture builds

Cheap or careless family meal signals that the staff isn't worth caring for. I am not going to be that kind of chef.

## What family meal looks like at Lark

Every day, two hours before service. The whole team (kitchen and FOH) eats together for 30-45 minutes.

The meal is real food. Not staff scraps. Not leftover prep. A real cooked meal.

Each cook (rotating) is responsible for family meal one day per week. They cook for the team. They serve it. We eat together.

## Budget

I have allocated $80/day for family meal ingredients. This is generous. Most restaurants spend less. I think it's worth it.

Total: $480/week, $25,000/year. Real money. The investment is in team morale, retention, and culture.

## What we eat

Whatever the cook on family meal duty wants to make, within reason. Some recent meals during build-out:

- Slow-cooked pork shoulder with rice and pickles (Inez)
- Lamb shank stew with farro (Jasper)
- A massive Thai green curry (Theo)
- Carbonara made with our own bacon (Beto)
- Buttermilk fried chicken (Lila — yes, the pastry chef makes fried chicken on family meal days)
- Tonkatsu with cabbage (me)

The variety is the point. Family meal is when each cook shows their own background and personal cooking.

## Rules

- Family meal is sacred. No phones at the table.
- We eat together, all of us.
- The cook who made the meal serves it.
- We complement the food and we comment on the food honestly.
- We talk about the night ahead at the end of the meal.

## What I want from family meal

The team to feel cared for. The team to learn about each other through food. The team to taste cooking from different traditions.

If family meal is great, service is great. If family meal is rushed and bad, service is harder.

## Personal note

I learned this discipline at every restaurant I worked at, but most explicitly at Atomix. The chef there used to say: "If you can't feed your staff well, you can't feed your guests well."

I believe him.
