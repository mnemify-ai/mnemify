# Sous Chef Job Description — Final

**Status:** Filled (Beto)
**Date posted:** 2025-08-14
**Date filled:** 2025-09-30

## The role

Sous Chef at Lark, a 28-seat tasting menu restaurant opening in Portland in July 2026. Reports directly to me. Runs the kitchen when I am not on the line.

## What you'll do

- Co-own the menu execution
- Run service when I'm not present
- Train and develop the line cooks
- Manage prep schedules
- Maintain kitchen standards (cleanliness, organization, technique)
- Coordinate with FOH on pacing
- Contribute creatively to menu development

## What you'll have

- A strong working partnership with me
- Real ownership over kitchen operations
- Time and resources to grow as a chef
- A team and a kitchen designed to support precision work
- Investor backing for the long haul

## What I'm looking for

- 5+ years in tasting menu or fine dining kitchens
- A demonstrated ability to maintain consistency under pressure
- Strong technique foundation; specifically, experience with French and Japanese methods
- A management style that's firm without being cruel
- Comfort with my level of detail (which is high)
- Honest communication

## What I'm not looking for

- A future executive chef who will leave in 18 months. I want someone who wants this role for several years.
- A reflexively authoritarian person. The kitchen runs on respect, not fear.
- Someone who needs validation. I will give honest feedback, including criticism. I do not give praise that isn't earned.

## Compensation

- Salary: $72,000-82,000 based on experience
- Health insurance (after 90 days)
- 14 days PTO
- Profit sharing after year 1
- Meals during shift
- Education budget ($1,500/year for stages, books, conferences)

## How we hire

- Phone screen with me
- In-person interview (90 min)
- Trial day in the kitchen (paid)
- Reference check
- Offer

## What sealed it for Beto

His trial day. He came in on a Saturday for prep. He worked the trout cure with me. He didn't ask a single question for the first hour; he watched and matched. When he did ask questions, they were the right questions. At the end of the day he cleaned his station better than I would have.

I offered him the job that night.

He had three other offers — two more prestigious on paper. He took ours.

I asked him why. He said: "Because I want to learn from someone who still cooks. The other places I'd be running the kitchen alone."

That answer was exactly right.
