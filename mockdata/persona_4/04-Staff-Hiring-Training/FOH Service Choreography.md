# FOH Service Choreography

**Status:** Drafting with Caro
**Date:** 2026-05-15
**Owner:** Caro

How service moves on the floor. Caro's domain; I review.

## The shape of a service

5:00pm — Staff arrives. Family meal at 5:00-5:30.
5:30-6:00 — Final reset, pre-service brief.
6:00 — Doors open. First seating.
6:00-7:30 — First seating, 14 covers.
7:45 — Second seating, 14 covers.
9:30 — Second seating finishes.
10:00 — Reset, close.
10:30-11:00 — Out.

12-13 hour days for FOH. This is honest about the work.

## The brief at 5:30

Caro runs a 15-minute pre-service brief every day:

1. Tonight's menu (any changes from yesterday, any 86s)
2. Tonight's guests (VIPs, allergies, special occasions, regulars)
3. Tonight's wines (Mateo's pairings, pours)
4. Tonight's choreography (anything different)
5. Yesterday's issues (what happened, what to do better)

This brief is short. It is not for chitchat. We get tight, then we open the door.

## The opening moment

When the first guest walks in:

- Caro greets at the door
- Lia (host) takes coat
- Caro walks them to their seat
- Server (Sienna at counter, Kai at communal, Erin/Theo at tables) presents at table
- Water poured within 90 seconds of seating
- Menu presented (we use a single card)

## The pacing of courses

12 courses over ~2 hours = roughly one course every 10 minutes, with variance for the longer/heavier courses.

Caro and Beto coordinate via headset. When the kitchen calls "table 4 up," Caro confirms within 5 seconds and dispatches a server. Server arrives at table within 30 seconds with the plate.

Plates leave the pass at the right temperature; they reach the table at the right temperature. The 30-second rule is hard. The pass is 12 feet from the closest table. We've designed for this.

## The diner-care moments

Caro has flagged seven moments of acute diner care per meal:

1. The greeting at the door
2. The water and bread arrival
3. The opening bite presentation
4. The course four pause (small bathroom break window)
5. The wine arrival ritual (Mateo)
6. The course nine ("are you enjoying?")
7. The exit (Caro again, by name)

Each moment has a specific approach. Caro is training the team in each.

## Counter service

The counter is its own world. Sienna runs it. The diners at the counter are watching the kitchen. They will ask questions.

We brief Sienna on every course every day so she can answer:
- What's in it
- Where it comes from
- How it's made
- Why we're serving it

She's allowed to send a question to the kitchen if she doesn't know. We don't bluff.

## Communal table

Kai runs the communal. The challenge: 8 strangers eating together. Energy varies enormously.

Kai's job is reading the table. Some nights it's lively and he stays out of the way. Some nights it's awkward and he warms them up. We are not forcing fake conviviality. We are reading the room.

## Issue handling

When something goes wrong:
- Server doesn't apologize broadly. Server identifies the issue.
- Server alerts Caro within 30 seconds.
- Caro decides: fix at the table, replace, comp, bring to chef.
- If the issue is the kitchen's fault, kitchen knows in real time.

We do not blame on the floor. We fix on the floor, then debrief after service.

## Phone policy

No phones on the floor during service. Caro's phone in the office. Servers' phones in lockers.

Exception: medical, family emergency. Tell Caro at the start of service.
