# Kitchen Training Protocol

**Status:** Active for new hires
**Date:** 2026-05-24
**Owner:** Beto

How a new cook learns the kitchen at Lark.

## Week 1: Observation and basics

- Tour the kitchen, walk-in, prep areas
- Shadow each station for 2 hours
- Learn knife skills and basic prep
- No service work
- End of week: knife skills assessment with Beto

## Week 2: Prep mastery

- Assigned to morning prep with rotating coaching
- Each morning prep task documented and tested
- Sauces in the Sauce Bible studied and made
- Three target preparations to master this week (varies by hire)

## Week 3: Cold line

- Work cold line during prep
- Learn the cold courses (opener, trout, salad, cheese)
- Plate course by course under Beto's eye
- First plating drills with Plating Specs

## Week 4: Hot line

- Work hot line during prep
- Learn the hot courses (mushroom, halibut/sturgeon, lamb)
- Sear, glaze, sauce
- Plating drills continue

## Week 5: Service shadowing

- Stand next to the assigned station during service
- Watch, don't touch
- Take notes
- Debrief after service with Beto

## Week 6+: Service light

- Take small parts of service (one course, one component)
- Build to full station coverage over 2-4 weeks
- Pacing depends on individual progress

## Plating drills

The hardest part of training. Every cook plates every course 5 times against the spec. We document errors. Cook practices until zero errors.

This is boring. It is the difference between good and great.

## What every cook must know by end of training

- All sauces in the Sauce Bible (make from scratch, no notes)
- All plating specs (plate from memory)
- Walk-in organization
- Sanitation protocols
- Pacing expectations
- Escalation paths (when to ask for help)

## What we test for at end of training

- Make a perfect dashi from scratch
- Plate any course from memory to spec
- Identify any mushroom species we use
- Describe the source for any ingredient

Pass: full kitchen status, raise to mid-rate of pay.
Fail: extended training period or, in rare cases, parting ways.

## Onboarding philosophy

I learned from my chef at Single Thread that training is the most important investment a kitchen makes. The cook you train well today is the cook who runs your kitchen in five years.

I am committed to long training timelines. Beto agrees. The line cooks who come through Lark should be employable as sous chefs anywhere in the country within 2-3 years.

If that means they leave us, fine. We trained them well. The replacement comes through the same protocol.
