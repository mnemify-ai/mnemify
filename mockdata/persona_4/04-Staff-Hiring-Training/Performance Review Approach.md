# Performance Review Approach

**Status:** Drafted; first reviews after 90 days post-opening
**Date:** 2026-05-27

How I want to think about performance reviews at Lark.

## What I don't want

The corporate annual review with a 1-to-5 scale and a HR form. That's how you make a kitchen feel like a Best Buy.

I've sat in those reviews. They are useless. The manager has already decided. The form is theater.

## What I do want

Honest, frequent, specific feedback.

- Daily: tiny corrections in the moment ("that's two seconds late on the trout")
- Weekly: 5-minute one-on-one with Beto (kitchen) or Caro (FOH)
- Monthly: 30-minute conversation with me or Beto/Caro
- Quarterly: deeper conversation about growth, goals, dissatisfactions

The annual review doesn't really exist. The conversation is continuous.

## What goes in a monthly conversation

Three questions, every time:

1. What's working for you?
2. What's frustrating you?
3. What do you want to be better at next month?

That's it. I don't want a scorecard. I want to know if the person is happy and growing.

## Difficult feedback

When something is wrong, I say it. Not in front of others (unless it's a quick correction). Not via email. In person, calmly, specifically.

Example I gave Jasper last week: "Your fish is good. Your reaction to feedback is making the kitchen tense. When I tell you the skin is overcooked, I don't want to see you tighten up. I want you to say 'got it' and adjust. Can you work on that?"

He took it well. He's already different.

## Praise

I do not give cheap praise. When I say "that's good," I mean it.

This is sometimes hard for younger cooks who are used to validation. I think they get used to my style within a month. They learn that when I do compliment them, it's real.

## Raises

After 90 days: review of starting wage. Possible bump.
After 1 year: profit sharing kicks in plus review of base wage.
After 2 years: significant raise if performance is strong.

No annual cost-of-living raises that mean nothing. Real raises tied to growth.

## Firing

I hope to fire no one in year 1. Realistically I will fire someone.

My approach:
- One private warning with clear expectations
- 2-week period to demonstrate change
- Second conversation: are we on track?
- If not on track: parting ways with severance (2 weeks pay)

I do not use PIPs. I don't believe in them. Either the person can do the job or they can't. If they can't, we end it cleanly and they go find something that fits them.

## Personal note

The chef at Atomix never raised his voice. He gave the hardest feedback I've ever received. It was always calm, specific, and aimed at making me better, not at humiliating me. I never forgot the difference between his style and the screaming chefs I'd worked for before.

That's the model.
