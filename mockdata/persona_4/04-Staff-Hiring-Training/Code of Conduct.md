# Code of Conduct

**Status:** Final
**Date:** 2026-05-08
**Distribution:** All hires

The rules of being on the team at Lark.

## The simple version

Treat your coworkers the way you want to be treated. Don't lie. Don't cheat. Don't steal. Don't be cruel.

That covers 90% of it.

## The longer version

### Respect

- No yelling. The kitchen is loud enough without screaming.
- No name-calling. "Hey chef" is fine. "Hey dipshit" is not.
- No mocking accents, names, food cultures, identities.
- No comments about appearance.

The kitchen has a long history of cruelty and machismo. We are leaving that behind.

### Sex and relationships

- No sexual conduct on premises. Period.
- If two staff members enter a romantic relationship, they tell Caro or me. We don't punish it; we just need to know to manage scheduling and conflict.
- No relationships between managers and the people they manage. Full stop.
- Harassment is a fireable offense. One report, investigation, action.

### Substances

- Drinking on duty is forbidden. Wine tasting with Mateo for staff education is the only exception.
- Coming to work impaired (alcohol, weed, anything) is grounds for being sent home unpaid.
- We don't drug test. I respect what people do on their own time. I just need them sharp at work.
- If someone is struggling with addiction, come to me. We will figure out support. We won't fire someone for being honest about a problem. We will fire someone for showing up impaired and lying about it.

### Honesty

- Don't fudge prep numbers
- Don't say a sauce is fresh if you reheated the morning's batch
- Don't blame another cook for your mistake
- Don't tell the table you're out of something we're not out of (servers, this is for you)

I find dishonesty corrosive. It's the fastest way to end up not working here.

### Money

- No taking ingredients home without permission. Family meal leftovers are fair game (label what you take).
- No comping food or drink to your friends without explicit clearance from me or Caro.
- No cash transactions with diners. All tips go through the system.

### Phones

- Phones in lockers during service.
- Phones during prep is fine within reason.
- No social media posts of dishes or kitchen during service.
- After service is over, you can post whatever you want.

### Conflict

If you have a conflict with a coworker:
1. Try to resolve it directly first
2. If that doesn't work, bring it to Beto or Caro
3. If it's serious, bring it to me

I will not tolerate festering grudges in the kitchen. If you're mad at someone for two weeks and didn't say anything, that's on you.

### Discrimination and harassment

Zero tolerance for harassment or discrimination based on race, gender, sexuality, religion, national origin, body type, age, immigration status, or anything else. One investigated incident = consequences. Two = termination.

Caro and I will both be available to receive reports. If the issue involves Caro or me, please bring it to Diane (our investor; she's agreed to this role).

### Reporting and protection

No retaliation for reporting. Ever. If anything resembling retaliation happens, the retaliator goes, not the reporter.

## What happens when someone breaks the rules

Depends on the rule.

- Minor (someone says something rude): private conversation, expectation of better
- Moderate (repeated rudeness, sloppiness, dishonesty in small ways): formal warning
- Major (theft, harassment, showing up impaired): immediate consequences up to and including firing

I will use judgment. The judgment is not "what serves the business." It is "what serves the team and the culture."

## Personal note

I worked in kitchens where the senior staff did cocaine in the walk-in, where the chef harassed servers, where a sous chef kept the line cooks in line with humiliation.

I will not run one of those kitchens.

If you've worked in one, I'm sorry. You don't have to bring those habits here. We are building something different.
