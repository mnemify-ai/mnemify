# Onboarding — Day One

**Status:** Active
**Date:** 2026-05-22

What a new hire's first day at Lark looks like. Designed to be welcoming, clear, and unambiguous about what we are.

## Morning

8:30am — Arrive. Caro greets at the door. (Not me. I want the first face to be hospitality, not the chef.)

8:45-9:30 — Sit-down with me. Coffee. We talk about:
- Why you took the job
- What you want out of the next year
- What I expect from you
- What you can expect from me
- Anything you need to tell me about your life that I should know

This is not a job interview. The interview is over. This is the start of a working relationship and I want it to start with honesty.

## Mid-morning

9:30-11:00 — Tour with Beto (kitchen) or Caro (FOH).

Kitchen tour covers:
- Every station, walk-in, dry storage, dish, prep
- Where everything lives (we have a map taped inside the walk-in)
- The Sauce Bible, the Plating Specs, the daily prep list
- Bathrooms, lockers, staff entrance

FOH tour covers:
- The floor, the bar, the service stations
- POS system
- Wine cellar with Mateo
- Linen, glassware, china

## Lunch

11:00-12:00 — Family meal. New hire eats with the team. This is intentional. The first meal at Lark is family meal, not staff food. Everyone meets them.

## Afternoon

12:00-3:00 — Prep work. Hands-on. New cook works alongside a senior cook on basic prep. Not testing them; just letting them touch the kitchen.

For FOH: shadowing Caro during reservation prep and reset.

3:00-4:00 — Paperwork. Tax forms, direct deposit, handbook, code of conduct.

4:00 — Debrief with me or Beto/Caro. How was the day? Questions? Anything that surprised you?

## What we give them

- A locker with their name
- A clean chef's coat (with their name embroidered, ready by day 3)
- A copy of the handbook
- A welcome card from the team (handmade by Lila — she insists on this)
- Cash $50 in a card for dinner tonight, somewhere they want to eat

The $50 is small but it matters. The night you start a job is exhausting. I want them to eat well and rest.

## What we don't do day one

- No service. They don't touch service for at least three weeks.
- No "throw them in the deep end."
- No hazing.
- No "let's see if they can hang."

We're not that kitchen.

## What I want them to feel by end of day one

- This place is serious
- This place is welcoming
- I am going to be challenged
- I am going to be supported
- I made the right choice taking the job

If a new hire goes home doubting any of these, we got onboarding wrong.

## Personal note

My first day at Atelier Crenn I was given a stack of paperwork, told my station, and ignored for the rest of the day. I went home and cried.

That memory shapes everything about how I onboard people. The job is hard enough. The first day shouldn't be.
