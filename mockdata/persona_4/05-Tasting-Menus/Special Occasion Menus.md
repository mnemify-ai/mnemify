# Special Occasion Menus

**Status:** Active
**Date:** 2026-05-21

How we handle anniversaries, birthdays, proposals, and other special moments.

## How we find out

- Reservation form has an optional field for "special occasion"
- ~30% of diners use it (industry average)
- Caro flags all special occasion diners during pre-service brief

## What we do (always)

The kitchen sends one small extra: a tiny dessert plate with "Congratulations" or a printed card from us.

The team learns the diners' names and uses them.

The server makes one small reference during the meal to the occasion ("we're so glad you're celebrating with us tonight").

Not over the top. Just acknowledged.

## What we do for specific occasions

### Anniversaries
- Small printed card with anniversary year
- Optional: glass of sparkling wine (champagne or pet-nat) on the house at end of meal
- If milestone (10, 20, 25 years): the chef comes to the table to congratulate

### Birthdays
- Birthday cake plate at dessert (modified version of our dessert with a candle)
- We do not sing happy birthday. Aiya does not want this. We acknowledge warmly without making a scene.

### Proposals
This is the highest-stakes special occasion. If the diner indicates a proposal is happening:
- Caro discusses logistics with the diner who reserved (usually the proposer)
- Often: we time the dessert course to be a quiet moment
- Sometimes: we provide a ring on a plate (the proposer's ring, presented by us)
- We coordinate any other surprise (photos, flowers, etc.) the proposer wants
- We try not to make the rest of the room about the proposal (other diners are still having their evening)

Caro is the lead. She has done dozens of these.

### Marriage proposals that get declined
This has happened to us. (Caro's prior restaurant, twice.) Our protocol:
- The server is briefed not to mention the proposal again
- The check is delivered as fast as possible if the table wants to leave
- The proposal extras (cake, etc.) are quietly absorbed (not billed)
- No staff makes eye contact with the table in a way that draws attention

This is rare but it has to be handled with grace.

### First dates
We don't advertise as a date spot but we are one. When the reservation indicates a first date:
- We do not say anything to the table about it
- We give the server context so they read the table well
- We seat them at a quiet table (not the counter, not the communal)

### Memorial / mourning meals
A diner sometimes books because the deceased loved this kind of food. We are honored when this happens.
- We don't acknowledge unless they bring it up
- If they share, we respond warmly
- A single white flower at the table is our gesture if we know in advance

## What we don't do

- Recorded "happy birthday" tunes
- Big crowd-clapping moments
- "Look at this couple!" embarrassment
- Sparklers (fire hazard plus tacky)
- Chocolate sauce writing on plates
- Group photos with the chef forced on the diner

We do not do the things every chain restaurant does. Special doesn't have to be performed.

## What I want every special occasion diner to feel

"They saw us. They cared. They didn't make us feel like a spectacle."

## Personal note

The best special occasion moment I have ever witnessed was at a small French restaurant in Brooklyn. An elderly couple celebrating 50 years. The waiter brought them one small plate of madeleines at the end of the meal. He set it down and quietly said "fifty years. Thank you for being here." That was it.

I cried.

That's the model.
