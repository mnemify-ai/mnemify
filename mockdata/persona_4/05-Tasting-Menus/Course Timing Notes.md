# Course Timing Notes

**Status:** Working sheet
**Date:** 2026-05-23

The minute-by-minute timing of a meal at Lark.

## Target total time

2 hours 15 minutes from seating to dessert.

This is the median. Tables vary. Some are 2 hours, some are 2 hours 30 minutes. We aim for the median by managing pace.

## What drives timing variability

- Diner pace (some are slow eaters; some race)
- Wine pour pace (Mateo adjusts wine pace to food pace)
- Conversation (we don't rush conversations; we read the table)
- Bathroom breaks (one per diner is expected; we slot them in)

## What I won't do

- Rush a table because we want their seat back
- Hold a course too long because we are slow in the kitchen
- Stack courses on top of each other to "be fancy"

## Pacing for two seatings per night

First seating: 6:00pm. Second seating: 8:30pm.

This requires first seating to be cleared and table reset by 8:25 — 2h25m turn time including reset.

If a first seating runs long, second seating slips. We have a 15-minute window built in.

If a first seating runs really long (2h45m+), we may delay second seating by 10 minutes and email them at 7:30pm.

## The two seating tension

The first seating diners are paying for the same meal as the second seating diners. They should not feel rushed.

The second seating diners booked at 8:30 and should not be made to wait.

This is the operational hardest thing about two-seating tasting menus. Some restaurants solve it by having one seating per night. Some solve it by having a hard time limit. We are doing two seatings and we are not going to rush.

The way we make it work: the kitchen has to pace cleanly. The FOH has to read the room. The two of these together = on-time turnover.

## What I'm watching during soft opening

- How often is the first seating > 2h30m?
- Is the second seating ever pushed back?
- What's the variance from the target schedule?

If variance is consistently > 15 minutes, the menu pacing needs work. If variance is < 15 minutes, we're hitting it.

## Pacing slack courses

Some courses are "pacing slack" — they can stretch to allow a slower table without disruption:
- Course 3 (bread)
- Course 5 (salad)
- Course 10 (cheese)

These are courses where the diner often pauses to chat. We use them as flexible buffers.

## Pacing tight courses

Some courses have to come on schedule or they don't work:
- Course 4 (soup, has to be hot)
- Course 7 (mushroom, has to be hot)
- Course 8 (halibut, has to be hot)
- Course 11 (pre-dessert, has to be cold)
- Course 12 (dessert, has to be tightly executed)

These are kitchen-driven and the FOH adjusts to them.
