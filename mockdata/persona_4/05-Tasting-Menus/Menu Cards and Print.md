# Menu Cards and Print

**Status:** In production
**Date:** 2026-05-20

The physical menu the diner sees at the table. Small thing, big design care.

## The format

A single 5x8 card. Heavy cream paper stock. Hand-printed on a small letterpress in Portland (Stumptown Printers — Jenna).

Each course is listed by its main ingredients, not the dish name:

```
LARK
12 — JUL 2026

Smoked trout, salt-cured yolk, nori
Geoduck, chrysanthemum, finger lime
Bread, butter, smoked seaweed
Hot dashi, matsutake, yuzu kosho
Spring greens, sea bean, white miso
Trout, sansho cream, pickled fennel
Hen-of-the-woods, brown butter, charred leek
Halibut, celery root, brown butter dashi
Lamb shoulder, fermented black garlic, savory plum
Adelle, Bee Local honey, bee pollen
Yuzu, shiso, white sesame
Hokkaido milk, brown butter cake, miso caramel
```

No prices on the menu (price is on the website and reservation confirmation).

No descriptions of preparation. The server explains.

## Why this format

- The diner doesn't need to read; they need to eat
- The ingredient list is more interesting than a fussy dish name
- A short menu card respects the diner's time at the table
- Letterpress signals craft without being precious

## The font

Custom hand-set type by Jenna. She's using:
- Goudy Old Style for the body (warm, slightly handmade feeling)
- Hand-drawn caps for "LARK" (Jenna is designing these specifically)
- Small caps for course headings

I told Jenna: warm, not stylized. Lark, not LARK. The restaurant feels like a hand-set type book, not a logo.

## Cost and quantity

- Print run: 500 cards for opening month
- Cost: $850 per print run (small)
- Refresh: monthly during menu turn periods, otherwise every 2 months

This is generous spend for a menu card. I think it's worth it. The first thing on the table after water sets a tone.

## The "today" sheet

For dietary substitutions and 86s, we have a small accompanying card the server can show.

The today sheet is laser-printed in the office. Updated daily.

## What the menu doesn't include

- Wine pairings (separate document)
- Pricing
- Allergen warnings (in the reservation confirm)
- Sourcing notes (the server tells these)

## The fonts I considered

- Goudy (chosen)
- Garamond (too elegant; not warm enough)
- Caslon (close second; slightly too formal)
- Bembo (too classical)
- Sabon (too modern)

Goudy feels Pacific Northwest to me. It's a Goudy designed in the early 1900s, recovered, slightly imperfect. It looks like it could have been used by a great regional restaurant 80 years ago. That's the feeling I want.

## What the back of the card has

A small printed line:

"Thank you for being here. — A & the team"

That's it.

## Personal note

The first time I designed a menu I was at the second restaurant of my career. The chef let me work on the menu for our seasonal change. He told me: "the menu is the only thing that's on every table. Take it seriously."

I am taking it seriously. The card is small. The font matters. The paper matters. The signature on the back matters.

Diners hold this card. It is the only thing they take away other than the memory of the meal. I want them to keep it.
