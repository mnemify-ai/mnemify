# Allergy and Diet Protocol

**Status:** Active
**Date:** 2026-05-13

How we handle allergies, dietary restrictions, and special requests at Lark.

## The principle

Every diner gets a great meal. If you can't eat something, we adjust seamlessly. We don't make you feel like a problem.

This is hospitality 101. It is harder than people think.

## How we capture

At reservation:
- Open text field for "dietary restrictions, allergies, preferences"
- We follow up by email 48 hours before the reservation if anything is unclear
- Caro reviews all reservations daily

At seating:
- Server confirms what was noted in reservation
- Server asks "anything else we should know?"

This redundancy catches the 30% of people who don't include their allergy in the reservation.

## How we adjust

For each course, Beto and I have pre-designed swap-out versions for:

- Vegetarian (no meat or fish; eggs and dairy ok)
- Vegan (no animal products)
- Pescatarian (fish ok, no other meat)
- Gluten-free (no wheat, rye, barley)
- Dairy-free
- Shellfish allergy
- Tree nut allergy
- Soy-free (harder than expected due to dashi)
- No alliums (onion/garlic family)
- Halal
- Kosher (limited; we can't fully do kosher because of kitchen setup, and we are honest about this)
- Sesame allergy (new common one)

Each swap is designed to be the same arc, same impact, same energy as the original course. Not a sad substitution.

## The hard ones

### Dashi-free
Dashi is in 4 of 12 courses. Vegetarian dashi (kombu only, no bonito) works for most courses. For the soup course, we use a mushroom broth that is fully different but excellent.

### Sesame
Sesame is everywhere. Tahini in two dressings, sesame oil in others. We have sesame-free versions of every course but it requires advance notice (24h).

### Multiple allergies stacked
Someone who is gluten-free + dairy-free + vegan is a real challenge. We can do it. We need at least 48h notice.

### Severe allergy
For severe allergies (anaphylaxis-level), we clean the prep area before starting the meal's prep and isolate utensils. We tell the kitchen so the cook plating that diner's course handles it last to minimize cross-contact.

## Who's not the right diner for us

Carnivore diets (meat only) — we can't really do it.
Strict raw food — we are mostly cooked.
Picky eating beyond medical need — we will do our best but the menu is the menu.

I tell people this honestly. Better to send them somewhere they'll love than to disappoint them at our table.

## Communication

When we adjust, we tell the diner what we're doing.

Sample wording: "For your course tonight, instead of the dashi we're using a mushroom and kelp broth — same warm bowl, slightly different umami profile."

This frames the substitution as care, not a sad version.

## Disasters to avoid

I have been at restaurants where:
- The server forgot to flag a nut allergy and the kitchen plated the pistachio garnish
- The kitchen forgot dairy and put butter on the bread for a vegan
- A "vegetarian" version still had fish stock

These are catastrophic. They breach trust. We design against them with multiple checkpoints.

## Personal note

My mother's friend has a shellfish allergy and once had an allergic reaction at a high-end restaurant in Tokyo. The server had said "no shellfish" but the broth had bonito (close enough to trigger her).

She was OK. The restaurant was mortified. That story sits with me.

We will not be that restaurant.
