# 86 List Protocol

**Status:** Drafted
**Date:** 2026-05-19

How we handle when we run out of a thing during service.

## The principle

86s happen. The question is how gracefully we handle them.

## What we 86 most often (predicted)

- Geoduck (supply variable; tide-dependent)
- Matsutake (when in season; demand exceeds supply most weekends)
- Specific cheeses (Adelle especially; small wheels)
- Heritage tomatoes during their short window
- Wild salmon (only in season; supply variable)

## What we do when we 86 a course mid-service

1. The kitchen calls "86 [item]" to Beto.
2. Beto confirms and immediately tells Caro.
3. Caro stops servers from announcing the original course to new tables.
4. The replacement course is now in effect. Servers are briefed.
5. For tables already mid-meal: we serve the original menu they were on (unless we're out for them too).

This means we may have two versions of one course running during a service. Beto manages this.

## The replacement course

For every potential 86, we have a "shadow course" — a course we can swap in quickly.

For example:
- 86 geoduck → cured scallop with the same vinegar and lime
- 86 matsutake → porcini in similar preparation
- 86 specific cheese → another cheese from Ancient Heritage or a guest cheese

The shadow course is designed in advance. The kitchen has prepped enough for the swap. It is not improvised in the moment.

## How we tell the diner

The server announces the swap at presentation: "Tonight's [course name] is our [replacement] instead of [original]. We're running with the freshest version of the dish."

We do not apologize unless the diner specifically asks why. The framing is the swap was a choice for freshness, not a failure.

## What we won't do

- Run out and tell the table "sorry, you can't have that course"
- Skip a course entirely (the menu is 12 courses, every diner gets 12 courses)
- Charge less for a swap
- Make the swap feel like settling

## What if we 86 a course AND the shadow?

This shouldn't happen with good planning. If it does:
- Beto and I make a judgment call in the kitchen
- We may improvise (in the worst case)
- We tell the diner honestly
- We may comp the wine pairing for that course

This has happened to me twice in my career. Both times I survived it by being honest with the diner and making something interesting on the fly.

## Out of supply for the whole service

If we know mid-day that we'll be short on something:
- Caro is informed by 3pm
- Caro emails any guest reservations who specifically asked about that course
- The menu sheet reflects the swap
- Wine pairings adjust

## Out of supply for the whole night

If we are completely out of an ingredient for the whole night:
- We pivot to the shadow course for all seatings
- No announcement on the floor; the menu just is what it is
- Internal note for the next day's ordering

## 86 list maintenance

End of every service:
- Beto logs everything we ran out of and roughly when
- Trends inform ordering for next week
- Repeated 86s on the same ingredient = we increase the par

## Personal note

A great chef once said to me: "Tell me what you ran out of last week and I'll tell you whether your kitchen is well-run."

The right answer is: a few things ran out close to closing, but not in the middle of service. That means the kitchen is matching pars to actual demand and not over-ordering.

If we 86 things in the first hour of service, we ordered wrong. If we never 86 anything, we over-ordered.

We aim for the middle.
