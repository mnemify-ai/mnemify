# Seasonal Menu Plan — Fall 2026

**Status:** Draft for September transition
**Date:** 2026-05-20

The opening menu (July 12) is summer. We move to fall ~September 15. Here's the rough sketch.

## What changes

- Soup: matsutake (summer) → porcini and dashi (early fall)
- Salad: spring greens (summer) → endive, walnut, persimmon (fall)
- Cold course: trout (summer) → cured tuna with apple and yuzu (fall)
- Mushroom course: hen-of-the-woods (summer) → matsutake hot pot direction (fall)
- Halibut: halibut (summer) → black cod with kabocha (fall)
- Lamb: lamb shoulder (summer) → duck confit (fall)
- Cheese: same direction, fall cheeses
- Dessert: Hokkaido milk cake (summer) → pumpkin and miso caramel (fall)

The opening bite stays. The bread stays. The pre-dessert evolves toward bitter (citrus → quince).

## Why these changes

Fall is the move from light to heavy. Earlier in the menu we stay quiet (cured fish replaces lighter trout) and we get richer later (duck confit replaces lamb shoulder is roughly a wash but duck is more autumn).

The matsutake season is the most important thing. Matsutake show up late August in the Cascades. We pivot the mushroom course around them. Patrick (forager) is going to be busy in September.

## Supply chain shift

- Patrick (mushrooms): matsutake become primary
- Anderson Ranches (lamb): we go to less lamb, more goat (their fall slaughter)
- Northwest Wild Caught: shifts from halibut to black cod
- Baird Orchards: brings in late apples and quince
- Hannah OregOnico: kabocha and chestnut focus

## The timing

September 15 transition is theoretical. In practice we transition over a 10-day window — we 86 one summer course at a time and slot the fall version in.

Beto has flagged this is operationally complex. The kitchen has to know which version is on for each table. We will manage with a clear daily menu sheet.

## What I won't change

- The 12-course structure
- The pacing (~2h15m)
- The cold-warm-rich-sweet arc
- The dashi as a recurring through-line (different dashi for fall)
- The opening bite (it's our signature)

## What I'll workshop

- Whether the duck course wants a bird (squab? quail?) instead
- Whether the dessert wants persimmon or pumpkin (probably one each year, alternating)
- Whether the cheese course should be one cheese or three

## Goals for the fall menu

- The menu should feel like fall the way the summer menu feels like summer
- The signature courses (opening bite, mushroom, dessert) carry the identity
- The seasonal courses (salad, cold course, fish, meat) feel inevitable for the season

## Press timing

Plan to invite local press in late September for the fall menu opening. By then we will have been open 10 weeks. Service will be tight. Story is "first season transition for the new tasting menu spot."

This is more compelling than "we just opened, please come see."

## Personal note

The first menu turn at a new place is when you learn what the restaurant actually is. The opening menu is who you say you are. The fall menu is who you are once you've been in the space for a season.

I'm excited and slightly afraid of the fall menu. We will know more by then.
