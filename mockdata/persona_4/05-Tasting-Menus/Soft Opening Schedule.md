# Soft Opening Schedule

**Status:** Locked
**Date:** 2026-05-26
**Opening date:** July 12, 2026

The four-week run-up to opening, where we cook the menu for real people but not yet for paying customers.

## Why we soft open

- The kitchen has to actually cook the menu, not workshop it
- The FOH has to actually serve, not rehearse
- The diners (friends, family, investors, industry) get the meal and give feedback
- We find the cracks before paying customers do

This is non-negotiable. Many places skip it. I think those places open badly.

## The schedule

### Week 1 (June 15-19) — Kitchen-only run-throughs
- Mon-Fri: full menu cooked for the team only
- 6 staff at 7pm each night
- No service choreography; just cooking and tasting
- Beto and I focused on prep and execution
- 5 cooks at full pace
- Cost: ~$2,000 in ingredients

### Week 2 (June 22-26) — Friends and family
- Mon-Wed-Fri: 14 covers per night (one seating)
- People we know personally; they understand it's testing
- Full FOH service
- Full kitchen service
- Wine pairings included
- Feedback solicited but not pushed
- Cost: zero charge; ~$3,000 in ingredients

### Week 3 (June 29-July 3) — Industry friends
- Tue-Thu-Sat: 14 covers per night (one seating)
- Other Portland chefs, sommeliers, restaurant managers
- Honest peer feedback
- Trying full pacing (two seatings) on Saturday only
- Cost: zero charge; ~$3,500 in ingredients

### Week 4 (July 6-10) — Press preview and final run
- Tue-Wed: 14 covers per night, mixed (press + early supporters)
- Thu: 28 covers (full house, both seatings)
- Fri: 28 covers (full house, both seatings)
- Sat: closed for staff dinner, rest, and cleaning
- Cost: zero charge; ~$5,000 in ingredients

### Opening: Sunday July 12
- Full opening, 28 covers, two seatings
- Paying customers
- Reservations sold out as of last week

## Cost summary

Soft opening costs: ~$14,000 in ingredients + labor (built into salaries).

This is a real cost. Diane and I agreed it's worth it. Better than opening cold.

## Feedback collection

Each diner gets a short feedback card with three questions:
1. What stood out?
2. What was disappointing?
3. What would you tell a friend about this meal?

We do not chase reviews. We do not solicit anything public during soft opening.

I will read every card. Beto and Caro will read every card. We will debrief each night.

## What I'm looking for during soft opening

- Pacing — is it 2h15m or is it 3h?
- Course balance — does anything feel like an outlier?
- Service consistency — is the team moving cleanly?
- Diner reaction — what's quiet, what's loud
- Plating — can we keep up at full house
- Kitchen pacing — do we 86 anything

## What I expect to fix

- Plating speed on the trout course (the most complex)
- Pairing decisions for courses 6 and 7
- Service handoff between kitchen and FOH at the heaviest moment (course 9 lamb)
- The dessert sequence (always the hardest part to time)

## Personal note

The four weeks of soft opening are the most important four weeks of the year. They are also the most fun.

The pressure of the real opening is still ahead. The pressure of trying to be a fully formed restaurant is not yet on us. We can cook, taste, adjust, try again.

It's the closest I'll come to graduate school in restaurant work. I'm looking forward to it.
