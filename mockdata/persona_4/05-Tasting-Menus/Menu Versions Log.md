# Menu Versions Log

**Status:** Living document
**Date started:** 2025-12-01

A history of every menu version. I track this because the path matters.

## v1 — December 2025

First draft. 14 courses. Too long. The menu read as "every thing I could think of."

Key courses: smoked trout, geoduck, raw oyster, mushroom soup, halibut, sturgeon, lamb, three desserts.

What I learned: cut. Always cut.

## v2 — January 2026

12 courses. Cut raw oyster (redundant with geoduck) and one dessert. Added soup course (mushroom dashi). Added a salad.

Beto pushed back on the salad: "we're not a salad restaurant." I kept it. Salad as palate reset is important.

## v3 — February 2026

Major rework after a 2-day workshop with Beto. Took the menu from "thing I like" to "story we're telling."

Changes:
- Reordered to a clearer arc (cold → warm → rich → sweet)
- Added the cheese course
- Cut a second mushroom course (had two; redundant)
- Changed dessert direction (was overly complex with three components; simplified)

This was the version we first cooked through as a complete menu, just the two of us. Took 4 hours.

## v4 — March 2026

Refinement, not restructure. Cleaned up plating, sauce work, technique consistency.

Killed three plates that were "trying too hard." Replaced with simpler, better-executed versions.

This is the version I cooked for Diane and a small group (8 people) at her place in mid-March. Their feedback was very positive but they noted the menu felt long. Diane: "I love every course. But I would have left full at course 9."

## v5 — April 2026

Tried to address the length feedback by cutting bread course. Felt wrong.

Restored bread course in same version. 11 courses for two weeks of testing; bread missing the whole time felt like a hole. Added back.

Lesson: don't cut a course because of one piece of feedback. Wait for a pattern.

## v6 — Early May 2026

The sturgeon experiment. Wanted to bring in something distinctive (sturgeon is rarely served on tasting menus despite being native to the Columbia). Worked with sturgeon for 3 weeks.

The fish itself was great. The supply chain was unreliable. Two of three deliveries had quality issues.

Pulled sturgeon. Returned to halibut.

## v7 — Late May 2026 (current)

Halibut restored. Soup moved earlier. Cheese moved later.

This is the working menu for opening.

## What I've learned across 7 versions

- The first instinct is too much. Cut.
- A menu has an arc. The arc is more important than any single dish.
- One brilliant dish does not save a menu with weak supporting dishes.
- Soup is grounding. Bread is grounding. Cheese is grounding. Use grounding courses well.
- A signature has to feel inevitable, not striking. Striking dishes feel like a chef trying. Inevitable dishes feel like a chef found.
- Dessert needs more time than I think it does.

## What I haven't learned yet

How this menu actually plays under service pressure. Cooking it once for 8 people in a relaxed setting is different from cooking it for 28 people on Friday night.

That's what soft opening is for.

## Personal note

I keep all the discarded versions in a folder. When I forget why I made a choice, I look back and see the path.

The menu is the most rewritten document at Lark. It should be.
