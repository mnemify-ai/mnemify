# Vegetarian Tasting Menu

**Status:** Designed; testing in soft opening
**Date:** 2026-05-25

The vegetarian version of the opening menu. Not an afterthought. A full parallel menu.

## The principle

A vegetarian diner at Lark should get a meal as ambitious as the omnivore menu. Same number of courses, same arc, same care.

This is hard work. Vegetarian fine dining is genuinely difficult to do well. Most restaurants treat it as a series of replacements. We are designing it as its own menu that shares structure with the omnivore version.

## The 12 courses

1. **Opening Bite** — Salt-cured egg yolk, nori cracker, kombu jam (no trout)
2. **Geoduck Position** — Compressed melon, chrysanthemum vinegar, finger lime (substitute)
3. **Bread** — Same as omnivore
4. **Soup** — Hot vegetarian dashi (kombu only), matsutake, yuzu kosho
5. **Salad** — Same as omnivore
6. **Cold Course** — Tomato consommé with smoked tomato, cucumber, sansho (substitute for cold trout)
7. **Mushroom** — Same as omnivore
8. **Halibut Position** — Celery root with vegetarian brown butter dashi, fermented black bean (substitute)
9. **Lamb Position** — Charred eggplant with fermented black garlic, savory plum (substitute)
10. **Cheese** — Same as omnivore (depending on diet — dairy allowed)
11. **Pre-Dessert** — Same as omnivore
12. **Dessert** — Same as omnivore

The four substitutions (positions 1, 2, 6, 8, 9) are designed to land in the same place as the original course.

## The harder ones

### Course 1 — Opening bite
Trout is hard to replace. The salt-cured yolk + kombu jam combo gives umami + salty + creamy. Lacks the smoky depth of the trout. Not 100% there yet.

### Course 6 — Cold trout
Cold tomato consommé works. Tomato is a real thing in season. Out of tomato season we have to figure out a different cold course (cured beet? cured radish?).

### Course 9 — Lamb
This is the hardest one. Charred eggplant with the same sauce work approaches lamb but doesn't fully match. Beto is working on whether a smoked eggplant gives us the savor depth we need.

## Vegan version

The vegetarian version uses some dairy and eggs. The vegan version eliminates them:
- Salt-cured yolk replaced with miso-cured tofu
- Cheese course replaced with savory final small bite (mushroom paté)
- Butter in courses 7 and 8 replaced with olive oil + mushroom emulsion

The vegan menu requires 48h notice. The work is substantial.

## What I will not do

- Serve a "salad sampler" as a vegetarian tasting menu
- Tell the diner "you can have the omnivore minus the meat courses"
- Reduce the price for vegetarian (the work is the same; often more)
- Make the vegetarian diner feel like an afterthought

## What I love about this menu

The vegetarian menu pushes me harder than the omnivore menu. I can't lean on fat and meat. I have to build flavor from ingredients I love but rely on less:

- Charred eggplant
- Mushroom intensity
- Fermented black garlic
- Smoked tomato
- Salt-cured yolk
- Kombu jam (this one is genuinely revelatory; I owe it to a chef friend in Tokyo)

## What I want from the vegetarian table

If a vegetarian diner walks out and says "that was as good as any tasting menu I've had," I've succeeded.

I will know. Vegetarian fine dining diners know whether the menu was designed or assembled. They can taste the difference.

## Personal note

My mother was vegetarian for about 10 years (her health) before her doctor told her to add fish back. I cooked for her in that period and learned that vegetarian fine dining is a real discipline.

A meal that respects a vegetarian's diet is also a meal that respects their dignity. I am committed to the second part as much as the first.
