# Tasting Menu Pricing

**Status:** Locked for opening
**Date:** 2026-05-12

What we charge and why.

## The number

$185 per person for the 12-course tasting menu.

That's it. There is no a la carte. There is no different price for the counter vs. tables. One menu, one price.

## Why $185

We modeled three price points:
- $145 (lower, fast-casual fine dining territory)
- $185 (standard high-end tasting menu in Portland)
- $245 (Michelin-aspirant territory)

At $145, we don't cover our costs even at 90% occupancy. Off the table.

At $245, we're competing with Le Pigeon's tasting menu and a few destination spots. The food has to absolutely justify it. We can argue the food is there, but the perception in Portland is that $245 is too much for a debut. We'd struggle to fill.

At $185, the math works at 80% occupancy. The price feels honest for what we're serving. The diner who pays $185 should walk out feeling they got value.

Diane and I went back and forth. She wanted $195. I wanted $175. We landed at $185.

## Wine pairing options

- 12-pour pairing: $145
- 6-pour pairing: $85
- 3-pour pairing: $55
- BYOB allowed (corkage $35)

The 12-pour is Mateo's full vision. The 6-pour is a curated subset. The 3-pour is for diners who want pairings but at a lower commitment.

A full 12-pour pairing + tasting menu is $330 per person. With tax and tip, around $400. That's the high end of what a Lark diner spends.

The lighter option (3-pour) brings the meal to $240 all-in. That's the entry point.

## What's included

- Bread service
- Tea or coffee with dessert
- Sparkling water for the table (the tap water at Lark is good but we want to be hospitable)
- Petits fours at the end

## What's not included

- Pairings (extra)
- Glassed-by-glass wines (separate from pairings)
- Specialty cocktails (we will have a short cocktail list)
- Tax (we will be transparent on the bill)
- Tip (suggested but not required)

## Suggested tip

We do not auto-charge a service fee for parties of fewer than 6. We suggest 20% on the bill.

Some restaurants in Portland have moved to a no-tip model with a 20% service charge baked in. We considered this. We did not adopt it because:
- Our staff prefers the pooled tip model
- The legal exposure on "service charge" vs. "tip" is real
- The diner education burden is high

We will revisit after a year.

## What this means for revenue

At 28 covers, 5 nights a week, 80% occupancy, average spend of $260 (tasting + median pairing + tax/tip):
- Weekly revenue: ~$29,000
- Monthly revenue: ~$125,000
- Annual revenue: ~$1.5M

This is the conservative model. We could outperform.

## The diner

$185 is a special occasion price for most Portland diners. We are the place you go for an anniversary, a celebration, an out-of-town guest, a first date that wants to impress.

Our challenge is making the meal feel like the right kind of special. Not "ostentatious special." Warm, specific, surprising special.

## Personal note

The first time I charged real money for a meal I'd cooked (a private dinner in NYC, $200 a head), I felt sick the whole night. I was sure I was overcharging.

Then the diners thanked me and asked when they could come back.

I learned then: you charge what the meal is worth. The discomfort is your problem, not the diner's. Solve the discomfort.

$185 at Lark will be worth it. I will make sure of it.
