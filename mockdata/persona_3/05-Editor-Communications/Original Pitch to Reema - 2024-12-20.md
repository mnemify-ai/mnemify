# Original Pitch to Reema — 2024-12-20

**From:** Carlos Morales
**To:** Reema Patel, Deputy Editor, *The Drift*
**Date:** 2024-12-20
**Subject:** Pitch: Mesa Verde / water rights / private equity

---

Reema,

Sending this from Mesilla. I've spent the last six weeks here on what I had thought was going to be a short follow-on piece about labor conditions at the fruit-packing operations along the river. The labor piece is real and we should still do it, but I am writing now to pitch something different that has surfaced from the work and that I believe is much bigger.

I'll keep this short. We can talk on the phone next week.

**The story:** Between 2014 and 2019, a Connecticut-based private equity firm — through a chain of Delaware LLCs and the legal services of a Phoenix law firm — acquired water-rights certificates representing roughly 47,400 acre-feet of annual surface-water entitlement in the Mesa Verde Irrigation District in southern New Mexico. The certificates were acquired from 47 individual ranching families. The total purchase price was approximately $14.3 million. The current value of the consolidated portfolio is, depending on assumptions, between $200 million and $400 million.

The acquisitions were structured in a way that, in my preliminary reading, evaded the public comment and downstream effect review requirements that should have applied. The state water board ratified the consolidations in 2022, in a vote that was preceded by the resignation of one board member who had concluded — based on her review of an internal memo — that the ratification was procedurally indefensible.

That former board member has agreed to speak with me on deep background. I have spent four sessions with her over the past six weeks. She is, to my reading, an extraordinary source.

**Why the story is bigger than Mesa Verde:** The pattern is not unique to this district. It is, in the assessment of an academic hydrologist whose work I've started reading, a general feature of how the financialization of western water has progressed over the past two decades. The Mesa Verde case is unusually well-documented (because of the source I just mentioned) but it appears to be representative rather than exceptional.

**What I am asking for:**

- Time. This is, in my preliminary judgment, a six-to-nine month piece. I would publish nothing before fall 2026 at the earliest. I would not file daily updates. I would not file anything on this until I had reported it through.
- A travel budget. I will need to be in New Mexico, Arizona, possibly Connecticut, and possibly Washington for various periods. Rough estimate: $30,000 over the lifetime of the piece, but I'll firm this up after I've laid out the reporting plan in more detail.
- Patience with my fact-checker. The piece will require extraordinary fact-checking. I would prefer Kayla, if her workload allows.
- An understanding that I will, in the course of this piece, need to make several editorial decisions about what to include and what not to include. I am thinking specifically of a tribal water-rights dimension to the story that I do not yet have permission to write about. I will keep you informed.

**What I am not asking for:**

- A commitment to publish until we both believe the piece is ready. If the piece does not come together, I want the freedom to walk away. I think it will come together, but the work is too consequential to commit to in advance.

I owe you the labor piece by mid-January. That is on track. This pitch is for the next thing, and I want to start the work in January if you are willing.

Talk this week.

— Carlos

---

**Reema's response, received 2024-12-22:**

Carlos —

Approved in principle. I have the same instinct you do, which is that this is the piece you should be working on for the next year. Let's talk Friday. Bring your reading list and a draft reporting plan if you have one.

Three things up front:
1. I want Kayla on this from the start. She will be told it is happening and asked to begin reading along.
2. David needs to know. I will brief him before our Friday call. He will have questions. Expect them to be sharp.
3. Do not, under any circumstances, contact Andrew Lacher or anyone at Verdant Holdings or its law firm until after we have spoken on Friday. We need to discuss timing and approach. The early contact with potential subjects of this kind of piece is the contact that most often gets the rest of the piece killed.

Take care of yourself. Eat something other than tortillas. Talk soon.

— R

**CM notes:**

This was the moment the story began as a thing the magazine had committed to. Reema's response is, looking back at it eighteen months later, a model of how a senior editor commits to a piece without overcommitting. She approved "in principle." She constrained my behavior immediately on the question of contact with subjects (which was the right call; I would have made an error). She told me she would brief David and that I should expect sharp questions. She told me to eat.

I have kept this email because it is the document of the moment when the piece was, on the magazine's books, a piece. The reporting that has happened since then has been done with the magazine's institutional backing. That backing has mattered. I have been able to do things — file FOIA requests using the magazine's resources, retain outside counsel for advice on specific source protection questions, travel without justifying every trip in advance — that I could not have done as a freelance reporter.

This is, to me, the central argument for institutional journalism in 2026. The institutions that still exist, and that still take long-form investigative work seriously, are the institutions that enable work like this to be done.

There are, I will admit, days when I doubt whether the magazine will be around in three years. There are days when I doubt whether the readers we are writing for are still reading work of this length. There are days when I wonder whether the work, when it appears, will have the impact I am willing to spend a year and a half of my life to produce.

These doubts are not productive. I have set them aside, mostly. The work is the work.
