Reema,

The piece is at roughly 38,000 words across eight chapters. Chapter 1 is in third-pass shape and I think nearly done. Chapters 2, 4, 5, 6 are in second-pass shape and need additional work but are structurally where they want to be. Chapter 3 is broken structurally and I am rewriting it. Chapter 7 (the tribal question) is short and may end up shorter. Chapter 8 is in first-pass shape and is the chapter I am most uncertain about.

I want to share with you, while it is still possible to act on what I am about to say, the three problems I am wrestling with most directly.

**Problem 1: The Helen Fung material.** Helen has, in our most recent conversation, told me that she has more to say to me about her father, and that she will say it only after we have spoken once more. I am scheduled to see her in late June. The material she may give me in June could substantially change the structure of Chapter 5, which is currently the most interior chapter in the piece. If she gives me what I think she might give me, the chapter becomes the moral center of the entire piece. If she does not, the chapter remains good but is not the center.

I do not know which it will be. I am writing Chapter 5 currently in a way that allows for either possibility. This is not, as you know, my preferred way of working. But I think it is the right way for this material.

**Problem 2: The tribal nation.** I had the conversation we discussed at the end of April. The conversation went, as you anticipated, almost exactly as we predicted it would. I am confident in our editorial decision to withhold what I know about the impending federal action.

What I want to flag, however, is that the timing of the federal action is now potentially relevant to our own timing. If the action becomes public before we publish, the piece changes — not in its central claims but in its closing register. If the action remains under seal past our publication date, the piece will be read, eventually, against the action when the action does become public. I have written the closing chapter (Chapter 8) in a way that is, I hope, robust to either scenario.

The question I would like to discuss with you is whether we should publish in October as currently planned, or whether we should consider publishing in early September instead. September publication would slightly precede the window in which the federal action is most likely to occur. October publication would slightly follow it. Each has tradeoffs. I do not have a strong view yet. I would like yours.

**Problem 3: The Sandoval section.** This is the section I have been most afraid to write. I am, in the current draft, writing it in a way that depends heavily on what Hortensia decides she wants made public about her late husband. I am scheduled to speak with her in mid-July, at which point I will share with her the specific paragraphs in question and ask her to approve, modify, or refuse them.

I want to flag that there is a possibility she refuses. She may, in particular, refuse the paragraphs about Manuel's cognitive decline, which are, in some ways, the heart of the chapter. If she refuses, I have a fallback structure for the chapter that depends less on this material. The fallback is weaker. The chapter would still work but would not have the same moral weight.

I am writing this now so that you and David can begin to think about what the piece looks like under the fallback. I will tell you which version we are working with by July 20.
