# Memo to David Hsu — Legal Considerations Ahead of Publication

**From:** Carlos Morales
**To:** David Hsu, Editor-in-Chief, *The Drift*
**Cc:** Reema Patel
**Date:** 2026-05-18
**Subject:** Legal preview, Mesa Verde piece

---

David,

Reema has briefed you on the piece's progress. I am writing this memo to give you, ahead of the legal review process that will begin in September, a complete picture of the legal landscape the piece will navigate. I am writing it now, four months before legal review, so that we can begin engaging outside counsel on specific questions while there is still time to alter the piece in response.

**The principal exposures:**

**1. Verdant Holdings and Andrew Lacher.** The piece characterizes the firm's strategy as having been deliberately designed to evade public scrutiny, on the basis of documentary evidence, source testimony, and the corporate structure analysis. The characterization is supportable. The risk is a defamation action, brought either by the firm or by Mr. Lacher personally, alleging that the piece's characterization of the firm's intent is unsupported by evidence sufficient to meet the actual-malice standard.

My assessment: low to moderate risk of action filed; very low risk of action prevailing. The actual-malice standard requires showing that we published with knowledge of falsity or with reckless disregard for the truth. We have a documentary record that supports every factual claim, source testimony that is internally consistent and externally corroborated, and a paper trail of attempts to obtain comment from the firm. The piece will quote a former employee on Mr. Lacher's statements regarding seller understanding. That quote is the most consequential single piece of attribution in the piece. I am confident in it.

I am scheduling a separate conversation with our general counsel to walk through the Lacher quote in detail.

**2. Helen Fung.** The piece characterizes her professional work with respect and quotes her on the record. She has agreed to her quotations and has been given the opportunity to review wording. The risk of action by Helen Fung is, in my assessment, essentially zero.

The downstream risk is the risk of action by her former firm, alleging that she has disclosed confidential client information in speaking to me. She has not disclosed confidential client information. She has discussed her own professional reflections and her own ethical thinking. The firm may nonetheless respond. We should be prepared.

**3. The general counsel of the State Water Resources Board.** The piece describes his drafting of the 2021 consolidation memo and his prior representation of Verdant Holdings. Both descriptions are accurate and supported by public records. He will be given an opportunity to respond before publication, through his attorney if he prefers. He may not respond. If he does not, the piece will note the offer of response and its non-acceptance.

Risk: a defamation action would face the same actual-malice burden as in the Verdant case, and would face additionally the fact that he is a public official whose conduct in his official capacity is being described. This is, in classic terms, exactly the kind of speech the First Amendment most strongly protects. Risk of action: low. Risk of action prevailing: very low.

**4. The members of the State Water Resources Board who voted for the consolidation in June 2022.** The piece names them as having voted, which is a matter of public record. The piece does not allege wrongdoing on their part beyond the wrongness of the vote itself. Some of them may object to being characterized as having voted on insufficient information. The characterization is supportable.

Risk: minimal.

**5. The unnamed political actors described in Chapter 2.** Esther's description of "categories of people" who exercised influence over the consolidation memo includes individuals in the Governor's Office. The piece will, on the basis of independent reporting beyond Esther's testimony, name two of these individuals. The naming will be supported by documentary evidence — specifically, communications I have obtained that establish their involvement.

This is the area I am most concerned about legally. The communications I have obtained are not authenticated in the way court-admissible documents would be authenticated. I have independent confirmation of their content from three sources. I believe the documents are genuine. I cannot, however, prove their provenance in a way that would survive aggressive challenge.

I am consulting with our general counsel on whether to:
(a) name the individuals as the piece currently does, with the documentary support I have;
(b) name them with stronger documentary support I am working to obtain through additional FOIA requests; or
(c) describe them by role rather than by name, which would weaken the piece but reduce legal exposure.

I have not yet decided. I would like your input before making the call.

**6. The Sandoval family material.** Mrs. Sandoval has consented on the record. Her daughter Yolanda has consented on the record. Her son Roberto has been informed. Her son David has not been consulted because of the family estrangement. The piece's characterization of Manuel Sandoval includes material about his cognitive decline, which Mrs. Sandoval has provided and which she has consented to be used.

Risk: a family member, most plausibly David Sandoval, may object after publication to the characterization of his late father. The objection would not, in my assessment, sustain a defamation action. It might, however, generate public criticism of the piece.

I am thinking about how to balance Mrs. Sandoval's clear consent to the material against the possibility of family objection after publication. My instinct is that Mrs. Sandoval is in the best position to make decisions about her late husband's representation, and that her informed consent is sufficient. I would welcome your view.

**7. The tribal nation material.** The piece does not contain material that the tribal nation has asked me to withhold. The chapter on the tribal question explicitly acknowledges that there is more to the story than the piece is telling, and explains why. The tribal water resources office is aware of this approach. They have communicated, through an intermediary, that they consider it acceptable.

Risk: essentially zero.

**Process suggestions:**

- General counsel review begins August 25, three days after fact-check completion
- I will be available for any number of consultations during the review period
- I will not contact Mr. Lacher, Verdant Holdings, or its law firm representatives directly until after general counsel review is complete and we have a draft of the right-to-respond letter that all of us are comfortable with
- I will, however, contact Helen Fung, Esther (if she has agreed to be named by then), and Mrs. Sandoval for final language approval throughout August

— Carlos

**David's response, received 2026-05-19:**

Carlos —

Thank you. Three things.

On the Governor's Office individuals: I want to err on the side of naming them, if your documentary support is what you describe. We should not let a stretched executive privilege assertion deter us from public-interest reporting. Let's discuss the specific documents next week.

On the Sandoval material: agree with your instinct. Mrs. Sandoval is the right person to decide.

On the Lacher quote: I want to be in the room when you review the source's account with general counsel. Schedule that meeting for the second week of August. I will clear time.

You are doing the work of your career. Take whatever time you need. Do not let the schedule force a shortcut.

— D

David's permission to take whatever time I need is the kind of editorial support that does not exist at most magazines anymore. I will not abuse it. The schedule will hold.
