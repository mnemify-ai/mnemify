# Pitch for Companion Visual Essay

**From:** Carlos Morales
**To:** Reema Patel, David Hsu, and Sasha Wei (Art Director, *The Drift*)
**Date:** 2026-05-08
**Subject:** Visual essay companion to Mesa Verde piece

---

All —

The piece is going to be roughly 55,000 to 60,000 words of text. The text is, I think, in good shape and earning its length. I want to propose a separate component that I have not raised before but that I have been thinking about for months: a companion visual essay.

**What I am proposing:**

A separate piece, roughly 1,800 to 2,400 words plus photographs and one or two simple maps, that runs alongside the main piece. The visual essay would be authored by a photographer working in collaboration with me, would be published in the same issue, and would be linked from the main piece.

**Why I think this is necessary:**

The main piece is doing a particular kind of work. It is documenting a financial and legal mechanism by which a public resource became a private asset. The work is, of necessity, technical and analytical in its core sections. The main piece will earn its emotional content through its specific scenes — the kitchen with Esther, the drive to Lake Talavera with Hortensia, the conversations with Helen.

What the main piece will not be able to do, even at 60,000 words, is render the place. The Mesa Verde watershed is a specific geography. The depression where Lake Talavera was is a specific place. The land that Verdant Holdings now owns the water rights to is a specific land. The piece will describe these places in sentences. Sentences are not, for this material, sufficient.

A companion visual essay would let the reader see what the sentences describe. Not in a documentary-photography way that glamorizes or romanticizes the West. In the way that the reader of a long piece about a specific place needs to be able to see that place, briefly, in order to inhabit the reading.

**Who I would propose to work with:**

I have been talking, informally, with [photographer]. Their work on water and land in the Southwest is, in my view, the best work currently being done in the area. They have indicated interest. They have not committed; they want to know whether we are serious before they invest time in proposing approach.

If we are not interested in working with them, I have a list of two other photographers whose work I think would be appropriate.

**What I am asking for:**

- Commitment to the companion essay format in principle
- A budget for the photographer's work, which I estimate at $18,000 to $25,000 plus travel
- A commitment to publish the visual essay alongside the main piece in the same issue
- Space in the magazine for the photographs in print, even though we both know most readers will read digital

**What I am not asking for:**

- A specific commitment to a specific photographer until you have indicated interest in the format
- Any change to the timing of the main piece
- Editorial control over the visual essay; the photographer should have authorial control over their work, with my consultation on factual accuracy

**Why I am raising this now:**

If we are going to do this, the photographer needs to be in the field before late summer, because the visual qualities of the landscape change substantially after the first storms. If we wait until July to decide, the visual essay will not happen.

I would welcome any of the three of you to push back on this if it does not fit the magazine's plans. I think it would substantially strengthen the package. I do not think the main piece needs it. I think the package would be better with it.

— Carlos

**Sasha's response, received 2026-05-09:**

Carlos —

I am in favor in principle. I have been waiting for you to propose this. The piece is the kind of piece that asks for a visual companion, and we have been thinking, at the art-direction level, about whether to propose one to you. You have saved us the trouble.

On the photographer: I have admired their work for years. If they are interested and available, I am extremely interested. Let me make the formal approach if you have not already pitched the project to them.

On budget: $25,000 is realistic. I will work with David to find the funds. We may be able to share costs with our grant-funded project on the Southwest.

On space: yes. We will find the print pages.

On timing: agreed. We need a decision this week.

— S

---

**Reema's response, received 2026-05-10:**

Carlos —

Reluctantly in favor. Reluctantly because every additional component in a piece this complex adds risk. In favor because Sasha is right that the package wants this, and because I trust your judgment on what the piece needs.

Conditions: the visual essay does not become a parallel investigation. The photographer is making a photographic essay, not co-reporting. The text in their essay is short, observational, and does not assert facts that the main piece has not already established. We do not have time or capacity for a second fact-check.

If those conditions are acceptable to the photographer, we are go.

— R

---

**David's response, received 2026-05-10:**

Approved. Sasha will lead on the photographer relationship; Reema on editorial integration; Carlos on factual consultation. Carlos retains the right to flag any factual issue in the photographer's accompanying text.

This is the right call. The package will be stronger.

— D

**CM notes:**

The institution is doing exactly what an institution should do. Sasha had been thinking about the visual companion before I proposed it. Reema is asking the right questions about scope. David is making the budget decision and then stepping out of the way.

I am writing this memo because I want to remember, when I am later having doubts about the value of institutional journalism, that this is what it looks like when it works. Four senior people, with overlapping expertise and aligned incentives, making a decision in two days that improves the published work.

The visual essay will, when it appears, be the part of the package I am proudest of having proposed. It is also, in some ways, the part that will be most criticized. Readers who object to the main piece will use the photographs to argue that the piece is editorializing. The objection will not be wholly wrong. The photographs will, by their existence, make a moral argument. The moral argument is one I am willing to make.

That is the difference between this stage of my career and the stage twenty years ago. Twenty years ago I would have written a piece that aspired to objectivity. Now I write pieces that aspire to accuracy. The pieces I write now have a position. The position is the position I have arrived at through the reporting. I do not pretend the position is not there.

If I am wrong about the position, the position will be corrected by readers and by subsequent reporting and by time. If I am right about the position, the piece will help.

This is the framework I have arrived at. It is the framework I will work within.
