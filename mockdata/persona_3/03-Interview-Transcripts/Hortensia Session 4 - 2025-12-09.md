# Interview Excerpt — Hortensia Sandoval, Session 4

**Date:** 2025-12-09
**Location:** Hortensia Sandoval's kitchen, Belen, New Mexico
**Duration:** 1 hour 50 minutes
**Recording:** No (per her preference; written notes only)
**Transcribed from notes by:** CM, same day
**This excerpt:** Approximately the first half of the session

---

[The session began with coffee. Hortensia was making tamales for her granddaughter's birthday party the following day. We sat at the kitchen table while she worked.]

HORTENSIA: I want to tell you something I have not told the other people who have asked me about this.

CM: All right.

HORTENSIA: Manuel did not want to sell. I want to be clear about that.

CM: I have understood that he did not want to sell.

HORTENSIA: But he did sell. So you have to ask, why did he sell. And I have not been honest about that with anybody.

CM: With me?

HORTENSIA: Not with you, not with Yolanda, not with anybody. Not even with myself, most days. But I will tell you now.

Manuel sold because he was afraid.

CM: Afraid of what?

HORTENSIA: Of what he was becoming. He was beginning to know that he was beginning to forget things. He had been forgetting names. He had been forgetting how to do small repairs in the shop. He had been forgetting where he had parked the truck, twice. He had not told me. He had not told the doctor. He had told nobody.

What he had decided, I think, without telling me, was that the family's affairs needed to be in order before he could no longer keep them in order. And the affairs included the land and the water rights. And when the man came to the door from Verdant Holdings, with a very fair price — I will say this, Carlos, the price was fair, by the standards Manuel knew — Manuel decided to act, because Manuel did not know how much time he had.

CM: He didn't think the water rights were worth more than the price he was offered?

HORTENSIA: He did not think to ask. He did not know to ask. He was a rancher, not an attorney. He had been told, by his father, that the land was the inheritance. He had not been told that the water rights were a separate thing that could be inherited separately. He did not know the difference.

The man who came to the door knew the difference. The man who came to the door knew what was being sold and what was being kept and what was being given away that the seller did not know was being given away. That is the difference between them. It is not a difference of intelligence. It is a difference of information.

CM: Have you forgiven Manuel?

HORTENSIA: I do not know if "forgiven" is the word. I have understood. The understanding came after the anger. The anger came after the grief. The grief is still here. They do not, in my experience, line up in a sequence. They sit in the kitchen with you while you are making tamales.

CM: Has the understanding changed what you are doing?

HORTENSIA: No. I will keep writing the letters. I will write them until I die, and then Yolanda will know that I wrote them. The letters are not for the people I send them to. The letters are for the record. I want there to be a record.

CM: I understand.

HORTENSIA: I do not think anyone will read the letters. I do not think the law will be changed. I do not think the water will come back to the lake. I write them because writing them is what I can do.

You are doing what you can do, Carlos. I am doing what I can do. Manuel did what he could do. We do what we can. The work continues after us.

[She returned to the tamales. I made notes for several more minutes without speaking. The conversation continued on practical matters — the upcoming party, her grandchildren, the weather. The substantive interview ended with her statement above.]

**CM notes after this session:**

I have, in two decades of work, rarely received a gift like this conversation.

The disclosure about Manuel's fear is, by Hortensia's own description, something she has not told anyone else. She told me at our fourth meeting. The first three meetings were her testing whether I deserved the disclosure.

I have to think carefully about how to use this in the piece.

The arguments for using it: it humanizes Manuel without infantilizing him. It explains the choice he made without excusing the choice the buyer made. It allows Hortensia to be the one who articulates the moral weight of the moment, rather than my voice as reporter doing it for her.

The arguments against using it: it is a private family matter. Manuel cannot consent. His other children — David in particular — have not been consulted. The use of it in print may damage relationships in the family that are not yet healed.

I will ask Hortensia, before publication, what she wants done with this material. If she has any hesitation, I will not use it. If she affirms that she wants it used, I will use it carefully, with the language she prefers.

The line *the work continues after us* is the line I will not forget. It is the line that, in some way, sits at the center of the entire piece. The work continues after Manuel. The work continues after Esther. The work continues, perhaps, after me. Hortensia gave it to me without knowing she was giving it.

I have to deserve it.
