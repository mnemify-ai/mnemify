# Interview Notes — Conversation with Tribal Water Resources Office

**Date:** 2026-04-30
**Location:** A phone call, my office, Mexico City
**Duration:** 47 minutes
**Recording:** No (per request)
**Notes by:** CM, same day
**Speakers:** The tribal nation's general counsel for water matters; their senior water resources director; me

---

**Format note:** I am not naming either of the tribal officials with whom I spoke. I am not, in these notes, even using initials, because either initial would be sufficient to identify the speaker in conjunction with their role. I am instead designating them as Counsel and Director throughout.

---

[The conversation began with introductions and a discussion of my prior reporting. Approximately the first twelve minutes of the call were spent on this. I am picking up after that point.]

COUNSEL: Carlos, I am going to be straightforward with you about why we agreed to this call, and what we are and are not going to discuss.

CM: Please.

COUNSEL: We agreed to this call because we have read your prior work and we have spoken to a person we trust who has spoken with you. We believe you will treat this conversation with the discretion it requires.

CM: Thank you.

COUNSEL: We are not going to discuss our current legal posture. We are not going to discuss what filings, if any, we are preparing. We are not going to discuss timing of any actions. If you ask us those questions, we will end the call.

CM: I will not ask those questions.

DIRECTOR: We will discuss, in general terms, the framework that our nation's water-rights claims operate within, and we will discuss why the matters you are investigating concern us. We will not discuss specific individuals in your story. We will not characterize the actions of Verdant Holdings or any other party.

CM: I understand.

COUNSEL: With those parameters, we will speak.

CM: I have a single question, then, which I will let you answer in your own way. The framework you mentioned — under which your nation's water-rights claims are senior to the rights administered by the irrigation districts, including Mesa Verde — has existed for over a century. The claims have not been routinely enforced. Why are they relevant to my story now?

DIRECTOR: I will answer that. The claims have always been relevant. They have not always been actionable. There is a difference.

The claims become actionable when three conditions are met. First, when the resource is sufficiently constrained that the failure to administer the senior claims has measurable consequences for the nation. Second, when the institutional capacity of the nation is sufficient to pursue the claims through the federal mechanisms available. Third, when there is a strategic case for action that does not unreasonably risk the broader interests of the nation.

I will tell you, in language I have chosen carefully, that the first two conditions are presently being evaluated. I will say nothing about the third.

CM: I understand.

DIRECTOR: Your investigation concerns us because it touches the second condition. The institutional capacity question is, in part, a question about resources, and about expertise, and about how the nation has been able to develop the legal infrastructure to assert its claims. It is also, however, a question about the public landscape — about the awareness of these matters in the broader political environment in which we operate.

If you write a careful piece, that piece will, in some marginal way, alter the public landscape. That alteration may affect our calculus. We are not telling you what to write, Carlos. We are telling you that your writing does not occur in a vacuum.

COUNSEL: We are also telling you, in the same breath, that we cannot directly assist you with information that would alter the timing of our own actions. The two are in tension. We are trusting you to manage the tension responsibly.

CM: I take this seriously.

COUNSEL: Good. We are taking this seriously as well. We are taking the unusual step of speaking with you because we trust your judgment. We hope that we have not misjudged.

CM: I will not betray that trust.

COUNSEL: Then we have an understanding.

DIRECTOR: There is one more thing I want to say. Off the record. Are you comfortable receiving this off the record?

CM: Yes.

DIRECTOR: [Brief statement, off the record, not included in these notes. CM]

CM: Thank you. I understand.

[The conversation continued for another fifteen minutes on practical matters about future communication. No further substantive material.]

---

**CM notes after this call:**

I will not write here what the Director said off the record. The notes I have made on it are in a separate, encrypted file with very limited access. The off-the-record statement does not appear in the piece in any form.

The on-the-record portion of the conversation — which is to say, the portion above that may be paraphrased in the piece — establishes two things:

First, that the tribal nation views the Mesa Verde matter as relevant to its own institutional concerns, and that the relevance is structural rather than incidental.

Second, that the nation's posture with respect to public reporting is sophisticated and deliberate. They are not avoiding the press out of distrust. They are managing the timing of public attention as one variable among several in a much larger strategic calculation.

The piece will reflect this without specifying what I have learned. The chapter on the tribal question — Chapter 7 in the current draft — already acknowledges that there is more here than I am saying, and explains why. This conversation will inform the framing but will not be cited.

I am, after this call, more confident than I was that the deliberate decision to withhold what I know about the impending federal action was the right decision. The nation has trusted me with that knowledge precisely because they expected me to honor it. The trust is the basis on which more substantive cooperation may eventually be possible — for this piece or, more likely, for future work.

This is the part of investigative reporting that does not appear in the published work. It is also, sometimes, the part that matters most.
