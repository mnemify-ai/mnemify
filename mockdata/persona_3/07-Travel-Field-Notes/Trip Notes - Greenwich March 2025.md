# Trip Notes — Greenwich, Connecticut

**Date:** March 14, 2025
**Purpose:** Attempt to make first contact with Andrew Lacher
**Logged from notebook same day, on the train back to New York**

---

I flew into JFK the night before and stayed at a hotel near Grand Central. In the morning I took the New Haven line out to Greenwich. The train was sparsely populated by 10:00 in the morning. The passengers were mostly older women in pearls and younger men in business casual with backpacks. I sat near the window and read the *Times* and watched the suburbs go by.

The address I had for Lacher's firm was on a quiet block off the main commercial avenue, in a four-story office building of dark stone, with a small brass plate by the entrance listing the building's tenants. The firm's name appeared on the plate without a logo, in the same typeface as the other tenants. Two financial services firms, an estate planning attorney, an investment management group whose name I recognized.

I went into the lobby. There was a reception desk. There was a small seating area with three armchairs and a copy of the *Financial Times*. There was a directory on the wall confirming the firm's location on the third floor.

I had a card in my hand. I had a letter in a sealed envelope. The letter was addressed to Mr. Lacher and was, in essence, a polite request that he speak with me. I had a copy of my outlet's masthead in case the receptionist did not know what *The Drift* was. I had prepared, on the train, three different opening sentences.

The receptionist was a woman in her late thirties named Vivian. She had been trained, I could see, to receive unexpected visitors. She listened to my opening sentence with the careful patience of a person who has been instructed to neither engage nor offend.

When I had finished, she said: "I will see if I can pass this along. May I take your card and the letter, and you can leave?"

I gave her the card and the letter. I asked if there was a possibility I could speak with anyone at the firm in person. She said: "There is no one available for unscheduled meetings today, no." I asked if I could schedule a meeting. She said: "I cannot make appointments on Mr. Lacher's calendar. I can pass along your information and someone will contact you if they wish to schedule something."

I said thank you. I left.

The whole interaction took, by my watch, four minutes and thirty-six seconds.

---

I walked back toward the train station. I stopped at a coffee shop and ordered a coffee and sat at a small table by the window and wrote down what I have just written above. The waitstaff did not pay me any attention. There were two other people in the coffee shop, both on laptops, both wearing AirPods.

A thing I have noticed, in my career, about places where significant capital is concentrated: the places do not look like much. There are no public markers of the wealth that the places generate. There are no signs. There are no statements of mission. There are office buildings with brass plates, and reception desks with women named Vivian, and coffee shops with waitstaff who do not pay attention to the journalists who come into town to attempt to interview the principals of the firms in the buildings.

This is, by design, what the surface of significant capital looks like in 2025. The capital itself is elsewhere — in the ledgers, in the structures, in the assets that the structures hold title to. The capital is not in the office. The office is where decisions are made about the capital.

I do not write this in the piece. The piece is about Mesa Verde. The reflection on the surface of capital is for me, in these notes, in this coffee shop, in this part of Greenwich that I will likely never see again.

---

On the train back to New York, I made the call to Reema that I had been preparing to make. I told her what had happened. I told her that I had not, in any meaningful sense, been received. I told her that I would write to Mr. Lacher again, by letter, and that I would not return to Greenwich without a confirmed appointment.

Reema said: "That was the meeting you needed to have. You went. You can say, truthfully, that you tried. The piece will say that you tried."

She was right. The trip was not, in the conventional sense, productive. The trip was a piece of the record. The piece will note that I made the trip. The piece will note that I was politely but firmly directed to leave my card and depart. The piece will note that I left it and I did.

The fact of my having gone matters, in some small way, to the credibility of the piece. The fact of having been rebuffed also matters. The polite rebuff is part of the texture of the story. The rebuff is what powerful subjects do when they are aware that someone is preparing to write about them. The rebuff has its own meaning.

---

**Note added six months later:**

I have, since this trip, called Mr. Lacher's office eleven more times. I have written him six more letters. He has not responded to any of them. I have spoken, in the intervening months, to a person who knows him professionally, who told me that Lacher is aware of my reporting and has been advised by counsel not to engage.

This is not surprising. This is what I expected. The piece will, in its right-to-respond letter, include the specific quotation attributed to him and the specific characterizations of the strategy he designed. If he chooses not to respond to those characterizations either, the piece will note the non-response and will publish.

The piece will not, however, characterize him as having "refused to comment." The phrase is inaccurate and is the kind of phrase that suggests an active engagement that has not occurred. I will, instead, write: "Mr. Lacher did not respond to repeated requests for comment, including a final letter sent through counsel six weeks prior to publication."

This is the accurate description. It is also a more damning description, because it implies what is true: that he was given every opportunity to provide his account of the matter and chose not to take any of them. The choice is his.

The piece will respect his choice. The piece will also be the piece I am writing.
