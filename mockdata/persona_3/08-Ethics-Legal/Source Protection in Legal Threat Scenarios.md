# Source Protection in Legal Threat Scenarios

**Author:** Carlos Morales
**Status:** Internal planning document
**Date:** 2026-05-15

## What this document is

A planning document for scenarios in which the piece, after publication, is met with legal action that includes attempts to identify my unnamed sources. The document is for me. It is also being shared with Reema, David, and the magazine's general counsel.

The piece relies on several unnamed sources. The most consequential is the source designated in the piece as "an individual who was present at Verdant Holdings during the relevant period" (Employee 2 in my files). The piece also relies on unnamed sources for the characterization of the strategy as deliberately designed to evade scrutiny (Employees 1 and 3), for the federal-agency context (the agency staffer), and for parts of the political dynamics described in Esther's chapter.

I have, in my career, never had a source's identity compromised. I have come close twice. The first time was an oversight on my part — I left a notebook on a desk and an unauthorized person photographed pages of it. The unauthorized person was, in that case, well-meaning, but the incident taught me that physical security is not optional. The second time was a subpoena, in 2018, which I resisted and which was eventually withdrawn after extended legal proceedings. I did not, in that case, reveal the source.

The Mesa Verde piece is more legally consequential than either of those prior incidents. I am writing this document because I want to think through, in advance, what I will do if the worst happens.

## Scenario one: A defamation suit that includes discovery of source identities

This is the most plausible scenario. Andrew Lacher's firm, or Mr. Lacher personally, could file a defamation suit in a friendly jurisdiction and use discovery to seek the identities of the unnamed sources cited in the piece.

My response: assert reporter's privilege in whatever jurisdiction the suit is filed. New York has a strong shield law. Connecticut, where Mr. Lacher is based, has a weaker shield law. Federal court, depending on the circuit, varies. The magazine's general counsel has already conducted preliminary analysis of the relevant jurisdictions. The piece's primary venue for distribution is New York-based and we have a strong argument for New York jurisdiction over discovery questions even if the underlying suit is filed elsewhere.

If reporter's privilege is overcome — which would require, in most jurisdictions, a showing that the source information is central to the claim, that it is unavailable from other sources, and that there is a compelling need — I will not voluntarily reveal the identities. I will accept whatever sanctions follow from non-compliance.

I have, in my prior subpoena situation in 2018, demonstrated to myself that I am willing to accept those sanctions. I have not been tested at the level of substantial fines or contempt incarceration. I am hopeful that I will not be tested at that level. I am prepared to be tested.

## Scenario two: A grand jury investigation that subpoenas source materials

Less likely but more procedurally severe. A federal or state grand jury investigation, potentially convened on the theory that the publication itself has revealed information that constitutes evidence of unrelated wrongdoing, could subpoena my notes, communications, and source contacts.

My response: same as above, with the additional complication that grand jury subpoenas have less procedural transparency than civil discovery. The magazine's general counsel has been briefed on this scenario and has prepared a response strategy.

The most important practical consideration here is that, as of the publication date, my source notes for unnamed sources will not be in any persistent file that can be subpoenaed. They are, currently, in encrypted files on devices that are not networked. The notes will, by publication date, be reduced further to the minimum necessary for any future fact-check requests. The remainder will be destroyed.

I am not trying to evade legal process. I am trying to ensure that legal process cannot retroactively compromise source commitments I made before the legal process existed.

## Scenario three: Civil suits filed by individual sources I have named

Lower probability but worth considering. A source I have named — Helen Fung, for instance, or one of the named state officials — could decide, after seeing the piece, that they object to how they have been characterized and could sue for defamation.

My response: this is, in my experience, rare. Named sources who have been given the opportunity to review their quotations and characterizations before publication are, in my experience, the least likely sources to sue. They have had the chance to alter the language and have consented to its publication. They have, in effect, helped author the description of themselves.

If a named source sued, the defense would rely on their participation in the editorial process. I keep contemporaneous records of every conversation in which a source has reviewed and approved language. Those records are the defense.

## Scenario four: Informal pressure on sources

This is the scenario I worry about most. After publication, parties unfriendly to the piece could apply informal pressure to my unnamed sources — through their former employers, through their professional networks, through legal threats that do not rise to formal action. The pressure could include attempts to identify them through process of elimination, through contacting people who might know them, through monitoring their public activity.

I cannot prevent this kind of pressure. I can prepare my sources for it.

I have, in conversations with each of the unnamed sources, discussed what they should do if they experience this kind of pressure. The conversations have included: (1) not responding to inquiries about whether they spoke with me; (2) not confirming or denying any specific characterization in the piece; (3) referring inquiries to their own attorney if they have one, or to the magazine's general counsel if they do not.

The sources have agreed to these protocols. Most of them have prior professional reasons for being skilled at not responding to inquiries. The risk is not that they will spontaneously reveal themselves but that the cumulative weight of indirect inquiries will, over time, identify them through narrowing.

If this happens, I have committed to writing about it. The first follow-up piece, if such pressure occurs, will be about the pressure itself. This is, in some sense, a defensive commitment — the threat of subsequent reporting on bad-faith identification efforts is a deterrent against the efforts in the first place.

## What I will not do

I will not, under any circumstances, reveal the identity of an unnamed source. I will not, under any circumstances, confirm or deny any identification proposed by another party. I will not, under any circumstances, allow my personal files relating to source identities to be inspected by any party not authorized by the magazine's legal counsel in writing.

These commitments are absolute. They are not subject to renegotiation. They are not subject to my own discomfort, my own legal exposure, or my own professional convenience.

## What I have done to prepare

I have, over the past six months, taken the following steps:

- Encrypted all source files and notes with strong encryption, on devices that are air-gapped from any network
- Established a protocol with the magazine's general counsel for response to any subpoena or formal demand
- Prepared each named and unnamed source for the kinds of inquiries they may receive after publication
- Reviewed my own communications, professional and personal, for any information that could be used to triangulate source identities, and have minimized such information
- Discussed with Beatriz, my wife, the practical implications for our family of the most severe scenarios. She has, I am grateful to say, indicated that she would support whatever response I would judge necessary.

I have not, except in the broadest terms, discussed any of this with Marina. She is nine. The work is hers eventually to understand. It is not hers now.

I expect to be tested. I expect to pass.
