# On the Tribal Question Deferral

**Author:** Carlos Morales
**Status:** Internal reasoning document
**Date:** 2026-05-04

## What this document is

A written record of the reasoning behind my decision, and the magazine's decision, to withhold from the published piece certain material I know about the tribal nation's likely federal action. The reasoning is being recorded because the decision is consequential, because it may be questioned later by readers or by colleagues, and because writing it down forces me to test it against itself.

## What I know that I am not publishing

I know that the tribal nation whose territory overlaps the Mesa Verde watershed is preparing a federal court filing that, if successful, would assert senior water-rights claims sufficient to invalidate or substantially modify the Verdant Holdings consolidations. I know, in approximate terms, the timing the nation is targeting. I know the legal theory the filing will advance. I know who, within the tribal water resources office, has been leading the work.

I learned this in two conversations: the formal call with the tribal nation's general counsel and senior water resources director on April 30, 2026, and a separate off-the-record statement made at the end of that call by the director. The off-the-record statement is the most specific piece of information I have, and it is the piece of information I am most committed to not disclosing.

## Why I agreed to withhold

The agreement was made before I had heard the substance. I committed, at the start of the call, that anything specifically tied to the timing or substance of the nation's legal posture would not appear in the piece. The commitment was a condition of the call taking place.

I made the commitment because I believed, and continue to believe, that the legal action the nation is pursuing will be more consequential than my piece can be. The action, if successful, may restore tribal control over a substantial portion of the watershed's water. My piece, in its most ambitious version, will inform a public conversation. The action will alter the underlying facts.

In a contest between informing a public conversation and altering the underlying facts, the underlying facts should win.

## What I considered

I considered, before making the commitment, the following counterarguments:

**That the public has a right to know.** This is the standard journalistic defense for publishing information that is in someone's interest to withhold. I respect the argument. I do not, in this case, find it dispositive. The public does have a right to know. The public's right to know is, in my judgment, fully satisfied by the substantial reporting the piece will publish about what has already happened in the Mesa Verde watershed. The piece does not need the additional fact of the tribal nation's plans to be a complete public account of the matter that has occurred.

**That withholding the information makes me a participant in the tribal nation's strategy.** This is a more serious concern. I have, by withholding, become aligned in a small way with the nation's interests. I am not pretending otherwise. I have concluded that the alignment is acceptable because the nation's interests, in this matter, are interests I would want to advance regardless of whether I had been asked to. The alignment is real but it is not a corruption of my reporting.

**That the decision sets a precedent for similar situations.** This is a real concern. If I withhold for the tribal nation in this case, am I obligated to withhold for similar reasons in future cases? My answer: no, not automatically. Each case is evaluated on its specific facts. The fact that this nation has, through legitimate channels and over a sustained period, developed a sophisticated legal strategy that may produce a more just outcome than my reporting can produce — that is the specific fact that justifies the decision in this case. It would not justify withholding from, say, a private corporation that asked me to defer to a planned commercial strategy.

The principle I am applying is not "withhold from sympathetic actors." The principle is closer to: "evaluate whether the public interest is better served by publishing or by a process that will produce a more substantive outcome." In this case, the process is the federal court action. In most cases, no such process exists, and publication is the only mechanism by which the matter will be addressed.

**That I have been manipulated.** This is the concern that an investigative reporter must hold against themselves most closely. I have been told a story by sources who have an interest in the story I am being told. Am I being used? Possibly. The check on this is: do the substantive facts I have learned independently support the framing the nation has provided? They do. The nation is not asking me to publish a misleading account. They are asking me to delay publication of certain accurate information until other accurate information has been processed.

The risk of being manipulated is real but is, in my judgment, smaller than the risk of imposing my own publication timing on a matter where another timing would produce better outcomes.

## What I have committed to

If the federal action is filed publicly before our publication date, I will write about it. The piece, as currently structured, can accommodate this update.

If the federal action is not filed before our publication date, the piece will publish without reference to the action. The piece will acknowledge, in Chapter 7, that there is additional material relating to tribal water-rights claims that the piece is not addressing. The acknowledgment is honest and signals to readers that the matter has dimensions the piece is not exploring.

If the federal action is filed after our publication date and modifies the outcome of the Verdant Holdings consolidations, I will write a follow-up piece. The follow-up will report on the action and on its consequences. The follow-up will not retroactively justify withholding from the original piece, because the withholding was justified at the time.

## What I have not committed to

I have not committed to refraining from any reporting on tribal water-rights matters in any future piece. I have not committed to refraining from reporting on the same nation in future pieces. The commitment is specific to the material learned in the April 30 conversation and to the present piece.

## Who knows about this

Reema and David, in detail. Outside counsel, in general terms. Kayla, when fact-check begins, in the details necessary for her work. The tribal nation's general counsel and senior water resources director. No one else.

I am not telling Esther, Helen, or Hortensia about the decision, because the decision does not affect their participation in the piece and because the information is not mine to share.

## What I will tell readers

In Chapter 7, the piece states: "The piece will note that there are dimensions of the tribal water-rights question that we have chosen, after extended consultation, not to address in this piece. The choice has been made for editorial reasons that the editor and reporter consider to be in the public interest. We will return to the matter in future reporting as circumstances permit."

This is, I believe, the maximum that can be said without compromising the underlying decision. Readers will draw what conclusions they will. Some will be uncomfortable. The discomfort is the cost of the decision.

## On record

I am keeping this document on record because I believe the reasoning should be available for inspection by anyone within the magazine's editorial leadership, now or in the future, who has a legitimate interest in understanding why the decision was made. It is not a public document. It is a record for the institution.

I will, separately, write a shorter version for my personal notes that will not contain any information specific to the tribal nation's plans. The personal version is what I would be willing to discuss with a colleague at another publication if asked. This longer version is for *The Drift* only.
