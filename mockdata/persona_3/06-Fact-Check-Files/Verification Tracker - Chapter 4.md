## Notes on the most challenging items

**The Lacher quote.** I have, in my notes from Employee 2, the exact language as Employee 2 remembers it. I have asked Employee 2 to confirm the language in two subsequent conversations. The language has been consistent. I have corroboration of the substance from Employees 1 and 3, in different words. I have no documentary evidence.

This is the area where Kayla will push hardest. I am ready. My position is that the quote, given three independent corroborations of substance and the contemporaneous note from one of the three witnesses, meets the standard for inclusion in the piece with appropriate attribution language. The attribution will be "an individual who was present at Verdant Holdings during the relevant period."

If Kayla pushes back hard enough that I am not comfortable with my own position, I will reconsider. I have promised her, and Reema, that I will reconsider rather than dig in. The discipline of being willing to reconsider is the discipline that protects the piece from my own attachment to it.

**The strategy characterization.** The characterization that the strategy was "deliberately designed to evade scrutiny" is a characterization rather than a documented fact. It is supported by the cumulative weight of source testimony, the structural analysis, and the documentary record. It is not, in any single piece of evidence, an established fact.

I am going to ask Kayla to read every piece of evidence supporting the characterization and tell me whether she believes the characterization is justified. If she does, the language stays. If she has reservations, I will soften the language.

**The portfolio valuation.** The $200M-$400M range is wide. The width reflects genuine uncertainty about how to value the leases. I have considered using only the lower bound, which is the more easily defensible number. I have decided to use the range, because the range is the honest answer. The lower bound is supportable from the court filings alone; the upper bound requires assumptions that Employee 2 has explained and that are reasonable. Kayla will weigh in.
