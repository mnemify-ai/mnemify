# Quote Verification Notes — The Lacher Quote

**Author:** Carlos Morales
**Status:** Working notes for fact-check
**Date:** 2026-05-20

## The quote

The quote, as it will appear in the piece (in language tested with Employee 2):

> "They understand the value of the price. They have not been asked to understand the value of the asset. That is a different question. The asset is structured in such a way that they will not be asked."

The quote is attributed in the piece to Andrew Lacher, in a meeting in the spring of 2017, with attribution language: "according to an individual who was present at Verdant Holdings during the relevant period."

## The evidentiary chain

**Primary source:** Verdant Former Employee 2. They told me the quote in our first substantive conversation on 2025-10-19. They told me they made a note of the language in their own files at the time of the meeting in 2017. They no longer have the note. They have provided the language to me from memory in three separate sittings, with no significant variation between sittings.

**First corroborating source:** Verdant Former Employee 1. In our second substantive conversation, on 2025-09-04, I asked Employee 1 whether they had heard Mr. Lacher articulate his position on seller awareness of value. Employee 1 said yes, in different language, on more than one occasion. Employee 1 paraphrased what they had heard as: "The price was the price the sellers had been told. The value was a different number. The sellers did not have access to the second number. That was the structure."

The substance is the same. The language is different. Employee 1 has not heard the exact quote attributed to Employee 2.

**Second corroborating source:** Verdant Former Employee 3. In our third substantive conversation, on 2025-11-26, I described the substance of the Employee 2 account to Employee 3 (without using the specific quote language) and asked whether Employee 3 believed the substance was accurate to their understanding of Mr. Lacher's approach. Employee 3 said yes, but said they had not been present at the specific meeting and could not confirm the language.

**Helen Fung:** In our second day of conversation, on 2026-02-04, I asked Helen Fung whether she had ever heard Mr. Lacher discuss the question of seller understanding of the value of the assets being transferred. She paused, then declined to answer, citing her continuing professional obligations of confidentiality with respect to a former client.

I noted at the time that the decline was itself a kind of answer. I did not state this to Helen. I have not used it as such in the piece.

## Why the quote matters

It is the single piece of source testimony that most directly establishes intent. The piece otherwise documents what Verdant Holdings did. The quote documents what Andrew Lacher knew. The distinction is significant. A piece that describes a structure of acquisitions, no matter how detailed, leaves open the question of whether the parties at the top of the structure understood what the structure accomplished. The quote forecloses that question.

If the quote is in the piece, the piece is an investigation of an informed strategy. If the quote is not in the piece, the piece is an investigation of a structure whose informed-ness is implied but not established.

The difference is real. The first version is the version I want to publish. The second version is, by my own assessment, weaker.

## What I have done to verify

1. Three separate conversations with Employee 2, in which the quote was reproduced from memory each time. The language has been consistent. The cadence has been consistent. Employee 2 has, when asked, been clear about what they cannot recall: the exact date of the meeting, the names of other attendees, what was said before and after the quote.

2. Corroboration of substance from Employees 1 and 3, as described above.

3. Documentation that a meeting of the kind Employee 2 describes did, in fact, occur. I have, through other reporting, confirmed that there was a strategic meeting at Verdant Holdings's offices in March 2017 that included Andrew Lacher, the firm's then-CFO, and at least three additional senior personnel. The meeting is referenced obliquely in a 2018 internal firm document I have obtained.

4. Outside counsel review of the attribution language, which has concluded that the language ("according to an individual who was present") is supportable.

## What I have not done, and why

I have not asked Andrew Lacher whether he made the statement. I will, before publication, send him a right-to-respond letter that includes the quote and the proposed attribution. I expect he will not respond, or will issue a non-responsive statement through counsel. I will publish either way.

I have not asked the firm's other principals about the statement. I have not been able to identify any other attendees at the meeting who would be likely to speak to me. The firm's communications discipline has been, by all accounts, effective.

## What could go wrong

If Mr. Lacher publicly denies making the statement, the piece will be tested. My defense will be the consistency of Employee 2's account, the corroboration from Employees 1 and 3, and the documentary record that places Lacher at the firm during the relevant period and in meetings of the kind Employee 2 describes.

If Mr. Lacher sues for defamation, the actual-malice standard would require him to prove that we published with knowledge of falsity or with reckless disregard for the truth. We do not have knowledge of falsity. We have not been reckless. The reporting process has been meticulous.

I have, in my career, published quotes attributed to powerful individuals on the basis of less verification than I have for this quote. Those quotes have, in every previous case, survived subsequent challenge. I expect this one to survive as well.
