# Chronology Master Document

**Author:** Carlos Morales
**Status:** Living; updated as new dates are established
**Date last updated:** 2026-05-26

## Why this exists

The piece is built on dates. Every claim of significance has a date attached. The dates have to be right. They have to be consistent across chapters. They have to be defensible against a fact-checker who is going to test every one of them.

This document is the master timeline. It is my single source of truth. When I write a date in a chapter, I check it here. When I learn a new date, I add it here. The chapters are subordinate to this document.

## Foundational dates

- **1849** — Doctrine of prior appropriation first articulated in California mining camps
- **1908** — Winters v. United States establishes the doctrine of federally reserved tribal water rights
- **1922** — Colorado River Compact signed
- **1924** — Mesa Verde Irrigation District established by state law
- **1962** — Lake Talavera first appears in USGS records as an active water body
- **1971** — Last documented use of Lake Talavera for recreational fishing
- **1989** — Esther begins work at the New Mexico state water division
- **2008** — Andrew Lacher founds the private equity firm that becomes Verdant Holdings's parent

## The acquisition period

- **2014 (April)** — Verdant Holdings registers its first New Mexico LLC subsidiary in Delaware
- **2014 (October)** — First parcel acquired (a 220-acre ranching property near Las Cruces)
- **2014–2017** — Phase one of the acquisition program: 31 of the 47 parcels acquired
- **2016 (October)** — The Sandoval family sells their parcel; Manuel Sandoval signs the deed
- **2017 (spring)** — The meeting at Verdant Holdings's offices in which, according to Employee 2, Andrew Lacher articulated his position that sellers had "not been asked to understand the value of the asset"
- **2017–2019** — Phase two: remaining 16 parcels acquired; water rights consolidated into a single Delaware holding company
- **2019 (March)** — Holding company applies to the Mesa Verde Irrigation District for formal consolidation of the certificates
- **2019 (August)** — District approves consolidation. Approval processed by a single staff member who has since died.

## The board period

- **2016 (January)** — Esther appointed to the State Water Resources Board for her first three-year term
- **2019 (January)** — Esther's first reappointment for her second three-year term
- **2021 (late summer)** — The consolidation memo begins circulating among board senior staff
- **2021 (October)** — Esther reads the memo for the first time. Reads it three times before writing her first internal response.
- **2021 (October–February 2022)** — Esther writes a series of memos requesting underlying documentation. None of the requests result in placing the matter on the agenda.
- **2022 (January)** — Esther reappointed for her third three-year term
- **2022 (March)** — Esther resigns. The resignation letter exists in archive only as a cover page; the body has been "misplaced."
- **2022 (June)** — A modified version of the consolidation memo is brought to the board. Vote: four to approve, one to oppose, one to abstain. The general counsel who drafted the original memo had recused himself from the vote but participated in the drafting.

## The lease period

- **2022 (autumn)** — The consolidated entity begins quietly negotiating long-term water leases with agricultural operations in adjacent states
- **2023 (March)** — First lease executed: a thirty-year contract with an agricultural operation in El Paso County, Texas
- **2023 (later in the year)** — Three additional leases executed
- **2023 (December)** — Andrew Lacher and his wife file for divorce. The proceedings remain ongoing as of this writing. The court records, which include a valuation of Verdant Holdings's portfolio, become partially public in 2024.
