# Background Profile — Andrew Lacher

**Author:** Carlos Morales
**Status:** Working profile; for my own use
**Date:** 2026-04-18

## Why this document

I have been reporting on Andrew Lacher for sixteen months. He has not spoken to me. He has not, to my knowledge, spoken to any other journalist about the matters in question. He may eventually decide to speak; he may not. Either way, the piece will characterize him at length. This document is my working understanding of who he is, assembled from public records, court filings, alumni publications, board member biographies, and conversations with three former employees and two former colleagues from his earlier career.

The document does not, and will not, appear in the piece as such. The piece will use what I have learned about Lacher selectively, in service of specific narrative points. The full picture lives here.

## Biographical sketch

Born Andrew Robert Lacher, December 1967, in Cleveland, Ohio. Parents: an attorney and a high school art teacher. One sister, two years younger, who is a physician in Massachusetts. Attended public schools in suburban Cleveland through high school. Graduated 1990 from Yale, B.A. in history (Phi Beta Kappa, summa cum laude). Took a fellowship year in London studying European economic history. Graduated 1994 from the University of Virginia School of Law, where he was on law review. Graduated 1996 from the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, MBA.

Worked in the early years of his career at a New York investment bank specializing in real estate. Moved in 2002 to a smaller firm focused on agricultural infrastructure investments. Founded his current firm, Verdant Holdings's parent entity, in 2008. The firm has, since then, grown from approximately $30 million in assets under management to approximately $2.4 billion as of the most recent publicly available figure (2023).

Married 1998 to a former public defender. Two children: a son (born 2001) currently in graduate school at Stanford, and a daughter (born 2004) currently a junior at Columbia. The family resides in Greenwich, Connecticut.

## Board service

Lacher sits on the boards of three nonprofit organizations:

- A regional conservation group in the Adirondacks, since 2011
- An organization that supports environmental education programs for underserved students, since 2015
- An organization that funds Indigenous-led land conservation initiatives, since 2019

His donations to these organizations, where they are publicly reported, total in the low seven figures over the past decade. His role on the third organization's board is the one that has, in subsequent reporting on Lacher, generated the most public commentary. The organization's mission is consistent with Indigenous water rights. Lacher's professional activities have not always been.

I have not been able to determine, through public sources, whether the third organization's leadership is aware of the apparent tension. I am not planning to raise the question with them. It is not central to the piece.

## Reputation in his industry

Quiet. Respected. Not flashy. Not, by any account I have heard, dishonest in the ordinary sense. He does not, by any reliable account, mislead his counterparties. He does not falsify documents. He does not break clear rules.

What he does, by every account I have heard, is identify gaps in regulatory frameworks before others do, and structure transactions that operate within those gaps in ways that produce extraordinary returns for his firm. He is, in this sense, the model of a particular kind of contemporary capital allocator: not a fraudster, but an arbitrageur of the spaces between the rules.

His former employees describe him in language that is, with surprising consistency, admiring and uneasy. The phrase that recurred in three independent conversations was: "He is the smartest person I have ever worked for. I would not work for him again."

## What I have not been able to establish

I have not been able to establish whether Lacher personally believes that what he has built is morally defensible. I have not had the conversation with him. I have not had it with anyone close enough to him to answer the question definitively. His public statements — at industry conferences, in alumni magazine profiles, in occasional written commentary — are uniformly professional and disclose nothing of his interior state.

The piece will, therefore, characterize what Lacher did. It will not characterize what Lacher believes. The distinction matters. A reader may infer beliefs from actions. The reader should be permitted to do that inference work themselves.

## What three former employees said about him

Each of these characterizations has been told to me by one of the three former employees, in separate conversations, with no apparent coordination. I am noting them here for reference.

- "He builds things that work. He does not build things he believes in."
- "He is not cynical. He is just disciplined about not letting his feelings affect his work."
- "If you ask him whether he is helping the world, he will tell you that the question is meaningless without specifying which world and at what time scale. He is not being clever. He believes the question is meaningless."

I find the last of these the most revealing. It suggests a person who has thought about the question of moral consequence and has arrived at a position — that the question itself is ill-formed — that is, by my reading, defensible philosophically and devastating practically. It is the position that allows the work to continue.

## What I have written for the piece

The piece characterizes Lacher in five or six specific moments, scattered across chapters three through five. The characterization is, in each case, anchored in something specific: a transaction, a meeting (as reported by Employee 2), a board decision, a structural choice. The piece does not attempt to render Lacher psychologically.

This is a deliberate restraint. I do not know him. I have not spoken with him. The reader will know what I know. The reader will not be presented with imagined interiority.

The discipline of restraint, in profile writing, is the discipline that distinguishes good profile writing from bad. I am trying to practice it.
