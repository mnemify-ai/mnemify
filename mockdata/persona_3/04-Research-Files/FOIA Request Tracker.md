# FOIA Request Tracker — Water Rights Investigation

**Maintained by:** Carlos Morales
**Last updated:** 2026-05-26

## Overview

This document tracks every Freedom of Information Act and equivalent state-level public-records request I have filed in connection with the Mesa Verde investigation. The piece is built, in part, on the documents these requests have produced. The piece is also informed by the documents the requests did not produce, by the documents that were produced in heavily redacted form, and by the unusual patterns in what was and was not released.

I keep this tracker because, in a piece this long, I will not remember the timeline of every request. I have learned, in past investigations, that the timeline matters. The pattern of agency responsiveness is itself a piece of evidence.

## State requests (New Mexico)

### State Water Resources Board — All non-deliberative records relating to the Mesa Verde Irrigation District consolidation of 2019-2021

- Filed: 2024-11-20
- Acknowledged: 2024-12-02

### Office of the State Engineer — Records relating to water-rights transfers, three-county area, 2014-2020

- Filed: 2025-02-04
- Acknowledged: 2025-02-25
- Produced: 2025-08-14
- Responsiveness: Slow but ultimately complete. The production included transfer records I would not have known to ask for. The State Engineer's office, in my experience, is the most professionally rigorous of the state agencies I have dealt with on this piece.
- Quality of documents: Excellent. Used extensively in Chapter 4.

### Governor's Office — Records relating to State Water Resources Board, 2019-2022

- Filed: 2025-05-14
- Acknowledged: 2025-06-04
- Produced: Refused on executive privilege grounds, 2025-09-30
- Status: Filed administrative challenge 2025-10-21. Decision pending.
- Notes: The refusal itself is informative. The Governor's Office is asserting that records relating to its routine oversight of an independent state board are privileged. This is a stretch of the privilege doctrine that I have not seen before in this state. I have noted the assertion. I am consulting our outside counsel about whether to escalate.

## County-level requests

I have filed property-records requests in all three counties relevant to the investigation, covering all 47 parcels associated with Verdant Holdings acquisitions. The requests have been straightforward; county property records are public. The aggregate cost of these requests has been $4,217, all approved by the magazine's research budget.

The deeds and recorded transfers provide the documentary foundation for the chain-of-title analysis that runs through Chapters 3 and 4.

## What the FOIA pattern tells me

Several things:

1. **The federal level appears to have been aware but inactive.** The pattern of redactions in the DOI response suggests internal concern that did not translate into formal review. I cannot prove this without obtaining the redacted material, which I do not expect to obtain.

2. **The state level appears to have been actively complicit in specific places.** The Governor's Office's privilege assertion is unusual. The State Water Resources Board's "misplacement" of Esther's resignation letter is convenient. The State Engineer's office, by contrast, appears to have been a straight-arrow professional operation throughout.

3. **The county level was straightforward but unimportant.** The counties simply recorded what they were given. They did not have the institutional capacity to evaluate the larger pattern.
