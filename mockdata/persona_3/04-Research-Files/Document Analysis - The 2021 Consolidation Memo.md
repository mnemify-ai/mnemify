# Document Analysis — The 2021 Consolidation Memo

**Author:** Carlos Morales
**Status:** Working analysis; supports Chapters 2 and 3
**Date:** 2026-01-09

## What the memo is

A forty-three-page document titled, in its final form, *Recommended Administrative Action Regarding the Mesa Verde Irrigation District Consolidated Water Rights Certificate Application of October 2019, with Findings and Proposed Order*. Drafted in the latter half of 2021. Circulated to the New Mexico State Water Resources Board's senior staff in October 2021. Was, by all accounts, scheduled to be placed before the full board for action sometime in the first quarter of 2022. Esther resigned in March 2022. The memo was, after her resignation, placed before the board in a modified form in June 2022. The board approved the recommended action by a vote of four to one, with one abstention.

## Who drafted it

The memo carries the signature of the State Water Resources Board's general counsel at the time, whose name appears in public records. The general counsel had, between 2017 and 2020, been a member of the legal team at a Phoenix firm that represented Verdant Holdings. The general counsel disclosed this prior representation at the time of his appointment to the board. He recused himself from the eventual vote.

He did not recuse himself from drafting the memo that recommended how to vote.

This is, by my reading of state ethics law, a defensible position. The memo was authored in his role as legal counsel to the board, not as a voting member. The relevant ethics rules do not, in my reading, prohibit the drafting of memos by a recused official. They prohibit voting and prohibit certain forms of post-employment representation. They do not prohibit memo-writing.

The memo's content, however, suggests authorship that was either deeply incurious about, or deeply familiar with, the legal architecture Verdant Holdings had built. The memo treats that architecture as straightforward. It does not interrogate it.

## The structure of the memo

The memo is organized in six sections:

**Section 1** — A statement of the procedural history of the Mesa Verde Irrigation District's consolidation application. This section is technical and, on its face, accurate. The dates are correct. The cited filings exist. Nothing in this section is misleading.

**Section 2** — A summary of the applicable legal framework, with citations to state statute and to relevant case law. This section is technical. It is also, by my reading and by Esther's, selective. The cited cases are cases that support the proposed outcome. Cases that complicate the proposed outcome — and there are several — are not cited.

**Section 3** — A staff analysis of whether the consolidation request meets the statutory requirements. This section is the section Esther read three times. The conclusion is that the consolidation meets the requirements. The supporting analysis is brief and asserts that "the consolidating entity has demonstrated continuous beneficial use of the underlying rights" — a claim that the documentary record I have assembled does not support.

**Section 4** — A discussion of potential downstream effects. This section is two paragraphs. It states that no downstream effects have been identified. The discussion does not address whether downstream effects were investigated. (They were not, in any documented form.)

**Section 5** — A discussion of compliance with public comment requirements. This section is one paragraph. It states that public comment was "satisfactorily received." The phrase is, by my reading, the most artful sentence in the entire memo. The phrase suggests that public comment was received and considered. The phrase does not assert that the required public comment period took place. (It did not.)

**Section 6** — The proposed action. This is a one-page recommendation that the board approve the consolidation request. The recommendation is supported by a list of seven bullet points that summarize the preceding analysis.

## What the memo does not address

The memo does not address whether the original transfers from individual ranching families to the Verdant Holdings nested entities, which occurred between 2014 and 2019, were themselves compliant with the procedural requirements of state water law. The memo treats the consolidation request as a question of whether to ratify a present state of affairs, not as a question of how that state of affairs came to exist.

This is the central structural choice of the memo. The choice is consequential. If the board had been asked to evaluate the original transfers, the board would have been required to find that they had not complied with the public comment and downstream effect investigation requirements. The board was not asked. The board was asked, instead, whether to consolidate what had already happened.

## What I have done with the memo

I have:

- Obtained the full memo through a public records request (state records, April 2025)
- Compared its claims, line by line, against the documentary record I have assembled from county property records, Delaware Secretary of State filings, and the Mesa Verde Irrigation District's own files
- Identified twelve specific factual assertions in the memo that are either incomplete or misleading
- Asked Esther to review my analysis of the memo. She has done so and has confirmed it is consistent with her own reading at the time.
- Asked Dr. Maldonado-Reyes to review the memo's hydrological claims. She has identified additional factual issues that I have incorporated into my notes.

## How I will use the memo in the piece

The memo will be quoted, in the piece, in chapters two and three. The quotations will be sparse — the memo is not interesting reading on its own. What will be on the page is my analysis of what the memo does and does not assert, supported by direct quotation where it is the most efficient way to make the point.

I will not name the general counsel by name in the piece without his having had a meaningful opportunity to respond. I have written to him three times. He has not responded. I will write a fourth time, this time through his attorney, in advance of publication.

## Open issue

The most consequential open issue is whether to publish the full memo as a supplementary document. The magazine has the technical capacity to do this. The legal team has not weighed in on whether publishing it would create new legal exposure beyond what publishing my analysis of it creates. I have asked Reema to begin that conversation with the legal team.

Publishing the full memo would, in my judgment, allow readers to evaluate my characterization independently. It would also expose any sources who pointed me toward specific aspects of the memo, since some of those aspects are not obvious from a casual reading. The decision is non-trivial.
