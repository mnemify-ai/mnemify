## Why this source matters

The Mesa Verde consolidations were not, strictly speaking, subject to federal review. They were structured to fall under state jurisdiction. However, because the watershed includes federally managed waters in adjacent areas, and because tribal water-rights claims are matters of federal jurisdiction, there are federal officials whose work has touched, peripherally, the relevant administrative environment.

This source is one such official. They have worked in a specific federal agency for over a decade. Their role does not involve direct review of state-level water-rights consolidations. They have, however, had visibility into the broader regulatory landscape that the Verdant Holdings acquisitions navigated. They are useful to me as a sanity check on what was, and was not, surprising about the state-level outcomes.

## How we met

I was introduced to this source through a former colleague who had once covered the federal agency in question. The introduction was made on the condition that the source would speak with me only after I had completed substantial reporting through other channels. The source did not want to be among the early people I talked to. They wanted to come in late, as a check.

We have spoken three times. Each conversation has been in a city other than the one where they work.

## Agreed terms

The strictest level of attribution. No name. No descriptor specific enough to identify the agency, let alone the role within it. The information may inform my reporting and my characterization of the regulatory environment, but may not appear in the piece in a form that would suggest a federal source.

This is, on its face, a very low return for the level of access. What this source provides, however, is essential to me as a reporter even if it does not appear in the piece. It is the test that ensures my understanding of how the state-level process should have worked, and did not work, is consistent with how the broader regulatory landscape looks from inside the federal agencies that touch it.

## What they have provided

- A reading of the state-level processes that was, in their professional judgment, consistent with my own
- An understanding of how the tribal claims interact with federal water-rights administration in ways that I would not otherwise have known
- Names of two other federal staffers who have, in subsequent reporting, agreed to speak with me on the same level of attribution

## What they have explicitly declined

To discuss anything that pertains to the current internal posture of their agency toward the Mesa Verde matter. To discuss any current investigation, audit, or review by their agency or any other federal entity. To discuss conversations they have had with state-level officials on the matter.

These declinations are reasonable. I have not pressed.

## My assessment

The source is well-informed, careful, and motivated by what appears to be professional concern about the regulatory landscape rather than personal interest in the specific firm or families involved. They have not characterized any individual in our conversations. They have spoken about systems, not people.

This is, in some ways, the most ethically clean source I have. They have nothing to gain from my reporting. They are not pursuing a personal grievance. They are providing context because, in their judgment, the context is in the public interest.

The downside of a source like this is that they cannot be quoted, cited, or referenced in ways that would compromise their position. What they give me is private knowledge that improves my reporting without becoming visible to readers.

## What I owe them

That they will never be identified. That I will not, in subsequent reporting, attempt to use what I have learned from them to leverage other federal officials. That if I am ever subpoenaed regarding my sources for this piece, I will defend their identity at whatever personal cost is required.

These commitments are not contingent. They are not subject to renegotiation. They will outlast my employment at *The Drift* and, if necessary, my career as a reporter.

I have made similar commitments to perhaps a dozen people across twenty-one years of reporting. I have not, to date, been forced to test them.

## Status

Active in a limited sense. They have told me they will continue to take occasional calls from me, but have asked that I not initiate contact more than once a quarter. I have agreed.
