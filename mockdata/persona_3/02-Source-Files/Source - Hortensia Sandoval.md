# Source — Hortensia Sandoval

**Real name:** Hortensia Sandoval
**Level of attribution as of 2026-05-26:** On the record, with family approval
**Date of first contact:** 2025-08-14
**Date of most recent conversation:** 2026-04-19 (Lake Talavera trip)
**Number of meetings:** Seven
**Author of this file:** Carlos Morales

## Background

Seventy-eight years old. Widow of Manuel Sandoval, who died in May 2021. Mother of four children: Yolanda (lives in Belen), Roberto (lives in Albuquerque), David (lives in Phoenix, estranged), and Lucia (deceased, 2019). Has lived in the same adobe house on the edge of Belen, New Mexico, since 1972.

Manuel and Hortensia owned 184 acres of ranchland north of Belen, inherited from Manuel's grandfather, with associated water-rights certificates issued by the Mesa Verde Irrigation District in 1924. The land had not been actively grazed since approximately 2004. The water rights remained associated with the land until October 2016, when Manuel, acting alone, sold both to a representative of Verdant Holdings for $312,000.

Manuel did not tell Hortensia about the sale until after the papers had been signed. He came home with a copy of the deed in a manila envelope.

## How we met

Through her daughter Yolanda, who had read my 2023 reporting on private equity in nursing homes and had reached out, in early 2025, to ask whether I would be interested in a related story about water. We exchanged emails for two months before Yolanda introduced me to her mother.

The first meeting was at Hortensia's kitchen table. She made coffee. She did not, at first, want to be quoted. She wanted to know what kind of person I was.

## Agreed terms

Hortensia is on the record. She made this decision herself, after our second meeting. She did not consult her children. (She has since told them. Yolanda is supportive. Roberto is concerned. David, who is estranged, has not been consulted.)

She has asked for two things:

First, that I describe Manuel fairly. She does not want him portrayed as a victim of a scheme. She has emphasized that Manuel was a careful man, that he had been doing business with the same neighbors for fifty years, and that the people who took advantage of his cognitive decline are not Manuel's fault. I have agreed.

Second, that any photograph used in the piece be one Yolanda approves. I have agreed.

## What she has provided

- Manuel's notes from the months before and after the sale, which had been in a wooden box in her bedroom for five years before she gave me copies
- Her own letters to state officials, the irrigation district, the consolidated holding entity, and the Phoenix law firm — 407 of them as of April 2026, all of them written by hand, all of them copied onto thin paper and kept
- Her recollection of the day Manuel came home with the deed
- A detailed family history, much of which I will not use but which has helped me understand the context
- Permission to drive with her to the depression where Lake Talavera once was

## What I have asked her for and what she has not provided

I asked her, in our fourth meeting, whether she would be willing to be photographed. She declined. She does not want her face in the piece. The agreement is that we will use a photograph of one of Manuel's notebooks instead, or of her hands holding a copy of one of her letters. Yolanda is taking the photographs.

I asked her whether I could speak to David. She said no. He is estranged and the estrangement is private. I have respected this.

## My assessment of her reliability

High, with the caveat that she is recalling events that occurred between five and ten years ago, and that the most consequential events — the sale itself, and Manuel's behavior in the months around it — she did not witness directly. Where her recollections involve Manuel's behavior, I have corroborated them with Yolanda, who was present for some of the relevant moments, and with Manuel's notes, which document his own awareness of his cognitive lapses.

Hortensia herself is extremely careful. She distinguishes, when she speaks, between what she remembers and what she has been told, and between what she believes and what she knows. She is, in this sense, an unusually disciplined witness.

## What I have promised her

That she will see the chapter that includes her before it is published. That her wording, if I quote her, will be exactly her wording or paraphrased with her approval. That the piece will not portray Manuel as a fool.

She has asked, separately, whether the piece will result in her recovering anything. I have told her no. She has accepted this. She is participating, she has told me, because she wants the record to show that the letters were written.
