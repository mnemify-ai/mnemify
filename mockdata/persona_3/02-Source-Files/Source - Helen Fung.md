# Source — Helen Fung

**Real name:** Helen Fung
**Level of attribution as of 2026-05-26:** On the record, with caveats discussed below
**Date of first contact:** 2025-09-22
**Date of most recent conversation:** 2026-02-04 (two-day visit in Tucson)
**Number of meetings:** Four in-person sessions; six phone calls
**Author of this file:** Carlos Morales

## Background

Retired attorney, sixty-six years old, formerly of the Phoenix law firm Halsey Vincent & Brand, where she practiced from 1982 to 2023. Specialized in commercial real estate for the first three decades of her career; specialized in transactional water-rights work in the western United States for the final fifteen years.

She was, between 2014 and 2019, the lead attorney on the legal work that structured the Verdant Holdings acquisitions and the Mesa Verde Irrigation District consolidations. She did not work for Verdant Holdings directly. She worked for the law firm that represented Verdant Holdings on the transactions. The distinction is technical but, as she has emphasized to me, matters to her.

Retired in 2023 to Tucson. Lives alone with two cats. Does not, by her own description, miss the work.

## How we met

I wrote to her, by letter, in August 2025, after her name appeared in the property records I had been gathering. I told her in the letter what I was working on and what I knew. I asked, in the most general terms, whether she would speak with me. She did not respond for six weeks. Then she called me on a Tuesday morning in late September and said: "I will speak with you. I want to do it in person. I want to do it in my house. Come when you can come."

I went in early November. I have been back three more times since.

## Agreed terms

She is on the record. She has been on the record since our first conversation. She told me, before I had asked, that she did not want pseudonymous attribution because, as she put it: *"I did the work. I should be the one whose name is on it."*

She has, however, asked for two specific accommodations:

First, that I read back to her any direct quote I intend to use in the piece, before publication, so that she can review the wording and the context. I have agreed.

Second, that I make clear, in the piece, that her legal judgment at the time was that the transactions were lawful, and that her current reflections on the morality of those transactions are reflections, not legal admissions. I have agreed to this as well, on the condition that I retain the editorial right to characterize the reflections in my own language. She has accepted this.

## What she has provided

- A detailed walk-through of the legal architecture she designed for the consolidations
- Names of three other attorneys involved at various points, all of whom I have since independently contacted
- A reading of Andrew Lacher's character that I have, with her permission, paraphrased in the piece
- An understanding of how the relationship between Verdant Holdings and the Phoenix law firm was structured
- A long, careful explanation of why she now believes that "legal" and "right" diverged in this case in ways she did not see at the time

## What she has not provided

She has not provided documentation. Specifically: she has not provided me with internal memos, billing records, communications between her firm and the client, or notes from her own files. She has explained that the documents are not hers to provide — they belong to her former firm, and her continuing ethical obligations to her former clients prevent her from sharing them.

I have not pressed. I have obtained much of the documentary record through other channels.

## My assessment of her reliability

Exceptionally high. She is precise in her language. She corrects herself when she is imprecise. She has, in four separate sessions, made small admissions against interest that no person concerned only with self-presentation would have made.

She has, twice, told me things that I subsequently confirmed through other sources. In both cases, what she told me was accurate to the smallest detail.

I have, in twenty-one years of reporting, met perhaps six sources of Helen Fung's caliber. She is one of the reasons this piece will be the strongest investigative work of my career.

## What I owe her

I owe her the piece I have promised to write: one that does not flatter her, does not condemn her, and does not collapse the moral complexity of what she did into a simpler story than the story she actually inhabits.

This is harder than it sounds. The temptation, with a source as articulate and self-aware as Helen, is to let her own framing become the article's framing. I have to resist this. I have to bring my own judgment to bear, and I have to render her in a way that allows readers to form their own views, including views that diverge sharply from hers.

I have not yet shown her any draft language. I will, before publication.
