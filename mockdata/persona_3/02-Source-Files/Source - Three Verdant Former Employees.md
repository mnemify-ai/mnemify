## Why three sources in one file

Operational hygiene. If this file is compromised, no individual source's information should be sufficient to identify them. I am keeping them in a single file with no information that allows differentiation. The actual details — names, dates, specific quotes attributed to specific individuals — are in a separate encrypted document that lives offline.

I am writing this file knowing that it will be the file an adversary, if one were to look, would look at first.

## What they have in common

All three worked at Verdant Holdings or its subsidiaries during the 2014-2020 period. All three left the firm voluntarily, at different times, for different reasons. None of them is a whistleblower in the formal sense — none has filed any legal action. All three were initially approached by me, not the reverse. All three required multiple conversations before they were willing to provide substantive information.

All three are on background. None has indicated willingness to be named, and I have not pressed. The information they have provided is internally consistent and is, where it has been testable against documentary records, accurate.

## What they have collectively provided

- Confirmation of the strategic plan as I understood it from external sources: that Verdant Holdings was, between 2014 and 2019, deliberately accumulating water rights in the Mesa Verde district as part of a longer thesis about the underpricing of western water rights
- Confirmation that the structure of the acquisitions was designed by Andrew Lacher personally, with Helen Fung's law firm executing the legal architecture
- An understanding of the firm's communications discipline during the acquisition period, including specific instructions to employees about how to respond to inquiries from journalists, regulators, or members of selling families
- Confirmation that the firm understood, at the time, that the tribal claims in the watershed were a long-term risk to the value of its acquisitions, and that internal analyses had been conducted regarding this risk
- Two specific anecdotes about Andrew Lacher's personal involvement in particular transactions that, on their own, do not amount to actionable evidence, but which inform my characterization of him as the architect

## What they have not provided

None of them has provided documents. All three have explained that internal documents from Verdant Holdings or its subsidiaries remain the property of the firm and that their employment agreements include confidentiality provisions they are not willing to violate.

What they have provided is their memory. I have, in each case, cross-referenced their memory against the documentary record I have assembled from public sources, court filings, and the materials Helen Fung has helped me understand. Their memory has been, in every testable instance, accurate.

## My assessment

All three are reliable. The triangulation across three independent sources, each of whom was not aware until late in the reporting that the others existed, gives me confidence that the picture they describe is accurate.

The risk with sources of this type — former employees with no public stake in the outcome — is that they may shade their accounts to favor themselves, or to settle personal grievances against the firm or specific individuals. I have looked for this. I have found, in each case, an absence of personal grievance. None of the three was fired. None left under contentious circumstances. None has subsequently sued the firm or been sued by it.

Two of the three have told me, in different language, that they regret their participation in the firm's strategy. The third has not characterized her feelings to me. I do not press.
