## Background

A former member of the New Mexico State Water Resources Board. Served two consecutive three-year terms plus a partial third before resigning in March 2022. The resignation was reported in local press at the time as having been for "personal reasons." The personal reason, as she has explained it to me, was a vote she had not yet been asked to cast but expected to be asked to cast, and a refusal to remain on the board if cast it.

Career prior to the board: thirty-three years with the state's water division, the last eight as a senior staff analyst. Her professional reputation in the state's water community is, by every account I have heard, that of a careful, conservative, technically skilled administrator. Her resignation was not seen at the time as a controversial act. It was seen as a quiet exit.

She has, since then, been quietly approached by two other journalists, neither of whom she chose to speak with at length. I was the third. She has not told me why she chose me.

## How we met

I had been working a separate story in southern New Mexico — a labor dispute at a fruit-packing facility — and I was introduced to her by a mutual acquaintance who knew that she had once worked at the water board and that I had once written about western water issues. The introduction was made over dinner. We did not discuss water at that dinner. I did not raise the topic for three weeks after.

When I did raise it, by letter, she responded by inviting me to her kitchen for coffee.

## Agreed terms

At our first meeting, in November 2024, we agreed on deep background. She would speak; I would not name her; she would not be characterized in any way that would allow identification by process of elimination.

We have, in subsequent meetings, renegotiated this. As of our most recent conversation, she has indicated she may be willing to be named, on the record, in the published piece, provided that she has time to consult with her own attorney about the implications. We have not yet had that conversation. I have given her until July 1 to decide. After that, the piece will run with deep-background attribution unless she explicitly approves a stricter or more permissive level.

## What she has provided

- Approximately 340 pages of notes from her last six months on the board. These notes are the documentary backbone of the investigation.
- Names, titles, and contact information for nine other former board staff who have, in turn, agreed to speak with me on various levels of attribution.
- An understanding of how the consolidation memo of late 2021 came to be drafted, and by whom.
- A reading of the political environment in the state at the time the memo was circulating that has been independently confirmed by three other sources.

## What I have not asked her for

I have not asked her to identify, by name, the individuals in the governor's office who may have applied pressure on the board's general counsel to keep the consolidation memo off the agenda. She knows their names. She has indicated, without my asking, that she will not name them, because their conduct — by her assessment — did not cross a clear line, and because naming them would expose her own past staff members to political consequences. I have respected this.

I have, separately, identified two of those individuals through other reporting, and the piece will name them. I have not told Esther this. I will tell her before publication.

## My assessment of her reliability

High. She is internally consistent across eleven separate conversations. She admits, when asked, the gaps in her own knowledge. She corrects herself when she misremembers a date. She offers documents to support what she says, without being asked.

The risk with a source like Esther is not that she is lying. The risk is that she is so internally consistent because she has been telling herself the same version of events for four years, and that version of events is, in some small way, simplified for her own moral comfort. I am alert to this. I have looked for evidence of self-flattering simplification. I have not found it. I do not, however, claim certainty.

## What she has asked me to remember

She has asked me, in several conversations, to remember that her resignation was not heroic. She has emphasized that she stayed in her position for nine months after she first read the memo, during which she did not go public, did not contact a journalist, and did not file any formal complaint. She has asked that the piece, if it characterizes her at all, characterize her honestly: as a person who acted late, and quietly, and only after exhausting the administrative channels she had been trained to use.

I have agreed.
