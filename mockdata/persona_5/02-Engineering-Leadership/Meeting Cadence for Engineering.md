# Meeting Cadence for Engineering

**Author:** Maya
**Status:** Updated; reviewed quarterly
**Date:** 2026-04-22

## Why I'm writing this down

I keep getting asked "should we have a meeting for X?" by someone who's been at Stencil less than 3 months. The answer is usually no but the reasoning isn't documented. Writing it down so I don't re-litigate every quarter.

## Meetings we have

### Weekly: All-eng standup (30 min, Monday 10 AM)

Everyone in engineering. Three things per pod:
- What shipped last week
- What's shipping this week
- What's blocking us

No status theater. No status PowerPoint. Either you have something interesting or you pass. The TLs run their pods' updates.

Why everyone, not just TLs: visibility across pods. Mobile engineers learn what Marcus's team broke and vice versa. Saves a lot of side-channel confusion.

### Weekly: TL sync (45 min, Tuesday 11 AM)

Marcus, Nadia, Devon, me. Coordination across pods.
- Dependencies coming
- Hiring updates
- Tech debt that crosses pods
- Anything that needs my decision

### Weekly: My 1:1s

- Aleks: Monday morning, 45 min. Strategic + product + investor context.
- Each TL: 30 min during the week. Their picks of topic. I push back if it's all status.
- Lin: 30 min Friday. Design + cross-functional issues.
- Renee: 30 min Tuesday. Product roadmap + delivery sync.
- Direct-report ICs (Devon for now): 30 min.

### Monthly: Engineering all-hands (60 min, first Wednesday)

Whole team. Different shape than weekly standup:
- A talk from someone in the team (rotates) — could be a tech deep-dive, a postmortem, a customer story
- Q&A with me — 20 min, open
- Hiring update, comp updates if any, business-level news from Aleks

Why: at 14 engineers I knew everyone's work. At 25 I won't. This is the forum where context flows down.

### Quarterly: Engineering offsite (full day)

In person. Even the remote engineers fly in.
- Half-day technical retro
- Half-day strategy session
- Dinner

Quarterly is enough. Twice a year is too few.

## Meetings we don't have

### Daily standup

We don't do it. Daily standups are theater at our team size. Async update in Slack is enough. If a TL wants a daily for their pod they can run one — but they'd rather not, I've asked.

### Sprint planning as a separate meeting

Embedded in the weekly TL sync and individual pod work. We don't do two-week sprint ceremonies. Sprint scope conversations happen organically.

### Retrospectives at fixed cadence

Postmortems happen after incidents. We don't do "let's reflect on what went well/poorly" as a recurring meeting. We do them after notable events. Forced retros at our scale produce manufactured content.

### "Architecture review board"

No. Architecture reviews happen in ADRs and pod-level discussion. A standing board would slow us down.

### Engineering-wide planning meetings

Quarterly offsite covers this. We do not do month-long planning cycles.

## Meetings I'm killing this quarter

- Cross-functional "tech-product sync" weekly. Last 3 were 15 minutes of status that's already in Slack. Done.
- "Hiring sync" weekly. Folded into TL sync. We can talk hiring there.
- "Security review weekly" — replaced with async Slack thread + monthly meeting. The weekly was performative.

## Meetings I'm protecting this quarter

- Engineering all-hands. Tempting to cancel for "no agenda" months. I refuse.
- TL 1:1s. Always. Even when calendars are crushed.
- The Aleks weekly. If founder + CTO drift, the company drifts. This stays sacred.

## What I tell new engineers

- We have less meeting overhead than your last company. Notice if that changes.
- If you're in a meeting where you're not contributing and not learning, leave. Politely.
- If you want to schedule a recurring meeting, write a doc justifying why async won't work. If you can't, don't schedule it.

## What I'm watching

- Meeting bloat. Easy to creep. I audit my own calendar monthly.
- Meeting hierarchy. New engineers shouldn't feel obligated to attend meetings they could skip.
- Async health. If Slack channels are silent, people are pulling everything into meetings instead.

## Personal note

When my calendar is more than 60% meetings in a week, something's broken. I look at it Friday afternoon. If I'm over the line, I cancel something the following week.
