# Performance Review Framework

**Author:** Maya
**Status:** First-time formal framework; reviewed with Aleks
**Date:** 2026-04-10

## Why we have a framework now

Through 2025 we didn't have formal performance reviews. We had 1:1s and informal feedback. At 14 engineers that worked. Past 20, two things break:
- New engineers want to know "how am I doing" with structure
- Pay decisions need to be defensible, not vibes

This framework is the minimum viable version. Twice a year. Not 360-degree. Not calibration committees. Not a PIP factory.

## The framework

### Levels

- **E2 — Mid-level engineer.** Solid IC. Ships features end-to-end with guidance on scope.
- **E3 — Senior engineer.** Owns large projects. Mentors others. Influences across pods.
- **E4 — Staff engineer.** Drives architecture across the org. Hires bar-raiser. Force multiplier.

(We don't have an E1 right now. We don't hire below E2. We'd add an L1 path if we start hiring new grads.)

### Six-month cycle

1. **Self-review** (1 page). Engineer writes what they shipped, what they learned, where they want to grow.
2. **Manager review** (1 page). I write what I observed, calibrated against the level.
3. **Discussion meeting** (60 min). We compare drafts. Honest conversation.
4. **Final review document** (1 page). Combined. Signed by both.
5. **Comp adjustment** (if applicable). Tied to the review but explicit.

## What we evaluate

Three dimensions. Each has expected behaviors per level.

**Impact.** Did your work move the company forward? Measured against the work, not the hours.

**Craft.** Code quality, design quality, decisions you made. Including: pushing back when something was wrong.

**Multiplier.** Did you make the team better? Mentorship, code reviews, knowledge sharing, documentation.

The first two get you promoted within a level. The third is what crosses level boundaries.

## What we don't evaluate

- Hours worked
- Visibility / self-promotion
- "Leadership" abstractly defined
- Politics
- Whether you're "intense" enough

If those show up in my review of anyone, call me on it.

## Promotion logic

Promotion happens when an engineer has been operating at the next level for 6+ months consistently. Not "potential" — observed behavior.

I don't believe in "potential-based" promotions for engineers. Promote on what they've done, not what they might do. We've all been burned by potential.

## Pay logic

- Market data sourced quarterly (Levels.fyi, Pave benchmarks)
- Comp band per level, with overlap between bands
- Adjustments happen at review time and on level change
- Aleks and I review and approve all comp changes

## What I tell engineers about the framework

- It's not a quota. Multiple people can get the highest rating in the same cycle.
- It's not a tool to manage you out. Underperformance is handled in 1:1s, not reviews.
- The discussion meeting is the most important part. The doc is supporting evidence.
- I will tell you exactly where you stand. No surprises.

## What I tell myself

- Calibration across the team is my job, not a committee's. I have to be honest about leveling differences across pods.
- I am biased toward engineers I see every day. The data/ML team works differently than the backend team. Don't penalize different work patterns.
- The review is not a substitute for ongoing feedback. If anything in a review is news to the engineer, I screwed up.

## Year 1 risks

- **Calibration drift.** With me as the sole reviewer, my biases compound. Mitigation: I share draft reviews with Aleks before finalizing for sanity check.
- **Review fatigue.** 14 engineers × 1 hour discussion × 2 cycles = 28 hours/year. Real time. Plan for it.
- **The framework becomes more important than the work.** Watch for this. The framework serves the team, not the reverse.

## What changes when we hire a VPE

The VPE owns level calibration across pods. I own VPE and direct-report tech-lead reviews. Pod TLs participate in their pod's reviews. Same framework, distributed ownership.

## Open questions

- Do we add 360 feedback? Probably not until 30+ engineers. Adds value, costs time.
- Do we publish the framework externally for recruiting? Yes, eventually. It's a hiring asset.
- Do we share comp bands internally? Considering. Aleks has thoughts.
