# Conference Takeaways — Money 20/20

**Conference:** Money 20/20 USA, Las Vegas
**Dates:** 2026-04-27 to 2026-04-30
**Attendance:** Aleks, Maya
**Author:** Maya

## Why we went

First time I attended Money 20/20. Aleks has been twice. The reason this year: Series B prep + sponsor bank market intelligence + meeting potential card-issuing partners. We had 14 scheduled meetings across four days, plus the sessions we picked.

## What I took away — strategic

### The BaaS shakeout is real but slowing

Five vendors at Money 20/20 are pivoting or downsizing their BaaS business. The 2024-2025 regulatory pressure on BaaS sponsors is settling into a "smaller number of survivors with more disciplined risk management" pattern. Pillar Bank is well-positioned in this new world (they were always more conservative). Good signal for us.

### Investing is the dominant feature add for consumer fintechs in 2026

I expected this. The talks confirmed: every consumer fintech is either adding investing this year or last. Our timing is right but not differentiated. We need a sharper "why our investing" story for Series B.

What I heard work: integration with the existing savings flywheel (which is our angle), low-friction onboarding (we're solid), tax-aware automation (we're not building this yet).

### Card issuing economics are tightening

Multiple panels on the changing economics of debit cards. Interchange revenue compression is real. The "free debit card as customer hook" model is harder to justify than two years ago.

Implication for us: the Lithic partnership is right but the economic model needs work. Round-up-to-invest may need to do more of the value lift than I'd modeled. Need to revisit with Aleks.

### Compliance is a competitive moat (still)

Several investor panels emphasized: post-2024-2025, investors are prioritizing fintechs with mature compliance posture. Our diligence room story is more important than I'd realized. Time spent here pays back.

## What I took away — tactical

### Lithic conversation

Met their VP of Customer Success. Confirmed contract terms. They're moving aggressively into fintech-card-program space. Good sign.

Asked about their fraud monitoring sophistication. Got a thorough demo. Better than I'd guessed.

### Plaid update

Met with their account team. Renewal in October. Pricing should stay flat (they need us as a customer; the BaaS shakeout reduces their account count). Worth using this for slight contract improvement at renewal.

Their Layer product (which we adopted in Q1) is now their most-pushed feature. Adoption is widespread. Our being on it doesn't differentiate, but we're not penalized either.

### Vendor we should consider for fraud monitoring

Heard a panel about Unit21. Specialized fraud platform for fintechs. Better than what we'd build, cheaper than building. Worth a pilot conversation once investing launches.

### Talked to four potential VPE candidates

Money 20/20 is a great place for senior fintech leadership networking. I had soft conversations with four people. Two are interesting. One reached out for a follow-up call. None are actively looking but two are within 12-month flight risk at their current companies. Building relationships.

## What I learned from talks I attended

### "Building a compliance program that scales"

Speaker was CCO at a Series C fintech. Their framework:
- Compliance as a function of engineering, not a separate org
- Compliance reviews as code reviews
- Audit trails as architecture
- A small dedicated compliance team supporting a broader engineering competency

This is roughly what we're doing. Validation.

### "Sponsor bank selection criteria"

A panel of fintech operators talking about how they evaluated sponsor banks. Their framework:
- Risk appetite alignment
- Operational maturity
- Compliance posture stability
- Pricing sustainability
- Executive accessibility

Pillar checks 4 of 5 (operational maturity is good but not great). Worth the periodic evaluation.

### "Mobile-first vs. mobile-only product strategy"

Strong session on the long tail of mobile-only fintech apps that hit a ceiling because of missing web/desktop functionality. Confirmed our intuition: maintain a thin web companion app, don't deprecate it, but don't over-invest.

- Should have gone to fewer panels. The hallway conversations were more valuable.
