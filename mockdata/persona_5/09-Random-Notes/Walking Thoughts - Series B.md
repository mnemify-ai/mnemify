# Walking Thoughts — Series B

**Date:** 2026-05-18 (Sunday morning walk)
**Format:** Voice memo transcript, lightly cleaned up

## What I was thinking about

Walking through Golden Gate Park. The thing I keep coming back to with Series B is the narrative we're going to sell vs. the company we actually are.

Investors want the narrative of "consumer fintech with a clear flywheel and Series C path." That narrative is true, but it's not the whole truth. The whole truth includes: April incident, KYC vendor switch driven by cost, an iOS codebase that needs rebuilding, a VPE we don't have yet, and a hiring market that's making senior backend hires brutal.

Aleks's job is the narrative. My job is the truth. Where those overlap is where I get comfortable.

## The thing I'm circling

Most engineering metrics we'd put in a Series B deck are vanity. LOC, deploy frequency, P95 latency. None of those convince a serious investor of anything important. What actually matters:

- Did the company run on solid infrastructure? (Mostly, with one bad week.)
- Did the team make good architectural decisions? (Mostly. Some I'd revisit.)
- Did the team ship the product roadmap? (Yes.)
- Is the team set up to do this again at 3x scale? (Open question.)

The fourth one is the only one investors should really care about. It's also the one I'm least confident about.

The team that built the first 14-engineer org isn't automatically the team that builds the 30-engineer org. Some of the people who are great at 14 will struggle at 30. Some will thrive. I don't know yet which is which.

## What I keep getting wrong about hiring

I keep hiring for "people who are great at the current stage." That's wrong. I should be hiring for people who are great at the next stage.

Marcus is the exception. He was great at 4 engineers. He's great at 14. He'll be great at 25. Maybe great at 50. He's that kind of engineer.

Sarah K, if she joins, is probably also that kind. Her references suggested she grows.

But most of the engineers I've hired in the past 12 months were great at the stage we were at when I hired them. That works for now. It compounds.

What I should be doing: building a hiring loop that explicitly tests for "would this person be great at 50 engineers?" Most loops test for "can this person do the job today." Those are different questions.

I'll think about how to embed this into the loop. Maybe a specific question or scenario. Maybe a different reference probe. Worth iterating.

## On the VPE hire

The conversation with the therapist last week was right. I'm slow on the VPE because I'm worried about hiring the wrong person, and the cost of the wrong person is high. So I'm waiting for the perfect person.

The perfect person doesn't exist. The 80th percentile person is fine. The 90th percentile person is great. Don't wait for the 99th percentile.

This is a "perfection is the enemy of done" moment for me.

## What I'd tell a younger version of myself

Worth a thought experiment: if 28-year-old Maya were starting Stencil now, what would she do differently?

She would:
- Hire a VPE earlier
- Spend more time on culture from day one (we got lucky with the early hires)
- Invest in observability earlier
- Be less afraid to fire when something's not working
- Take Sundays off

She would not:
- Found with anyone other than Aleks (he's the right partner)
- Pick a different product space (consumer fintech is the right space for me)
- Raise less money (Series A timing was right)
- Build differently in any meaningful way

That's a pretty good track record. Most of what I'd change is operating-the-company stuff, not the company-itself stuff.
