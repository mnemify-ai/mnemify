# Founder Therapy Notes — 2026-05-12

**Date:** 2026-05-12
**Context:** Monthly session with my therapist; she sees a lot of founders

## What we talked about

The pace of the work. The Series B pressure. The constant context-switching. The fact that I've stopped reading for pleasure. The way I'm sleeping less and exercising less than I want.

She asked: "Do you feel like you're getting better at the job, or just getting more done?"

Honest answer: more done. Not necessarily better. The volume goes up; the judgment hasn't sharpened proportionally.

## What she said that landed

"At some point you'll either learn to delegate or you'll burn out. Right now you're choosing the latter and not noticing."

That stung. I pushed back. I told her I'm hiring a VPE. I told her about the structure changes coming after Series B. She listened. Then she said: "Those are organizational changes. I'm asking about you. Right now, today, what are you doing with your time that someone else should be doing?"

I tried to answer. I couldn't list three things. The right answer would have been: PR reviews on areas I don't deeply own, calendar management, certain board prep work, mid-level engineer 1:1s I'm doing because I haven't elevated TLs to do them.

She made me write them down. I have a list now.

## What I'm not telling Aleks

That I'm worried about Series B not closing. He's been bullish. Bessemer is supportive. The narrative is strong. But I've watched two Series A companies in my network struggle to close Series B this year. The market is choppier than the narrative says.

If we don't close, our runway is fine through Q2 2027. But the narrative gets harder. Investing feature ships, we have a year to find product-market expansion. Doable but tight.

I haven't said this to Aleks because saying it could become self-fulfilling. He's optimistic, his energy carries the company, I don't want to dent that. But the suppression has weight.

She said: "Find someone you can be pessimistic with." Not Aleks. Not the team. Someone outside the company. She suggested I reactivate my friendship with an old Stripe colleague who now runs eng at another fintech. She's probably right.

## What I'm worried about with the team

Devon is the most worried-about. He's getting recruiter outreach. He told me. He hasn't taken any calls. But the offer that gets him is a research position at an AI lab. We can't compete with that on certain dimensions. I can only hope that the work matters to him more than the lab brand.

Lin is the second. She's burned out and trying not to show it. I've authorized the contract designer. That'll help. But she's been quietly building open-source side projects, which I read as her processing something.

Nadia is moving into management. Going to be great. Won't be a worry.

Marcus is the most stable. He's been having the best year of any of us. The April incident was the worst week he's had at Stencil and he kept it together.

## What I'm worried about with myself

I haven't been to my parents' place in 5 months. They're getting older. I tell myself I'll go after Series B closes. That's exactly the kind of justification that compounds. I'm going next weekend, no matter what.

I haven't had a relationship-quality conversation with my partner in weeks. He's been patient. I should not test that patience.

I haven't read a non-work book in 3 months. I used to read 2-3 per month. The thing I do for fun, gone.

## What I'm going to change

- Visit parents next weekend (booked flight already)
- Weekly date night with my partner, non-negotiable
- 30 minutes reading per night before bed (not work reading)
- One full weekend day per week with no laptop (Sunday seems right)
- The delegation list from therapy — work through it with VPE candidates eventually

## What I'm taking from this session

The thing about being a founder/CTO is that everything feels urgent because nothing has a clear end. There's no "I finished the project, now I rest" rhythm. The work just continues.

What I have to provide myself is the rhythm. The end-of-day. The Sunday. The week off. If I don't impose structure, the work consumes everything.

I'm 34. I'm not 24. The body remembers when I treat it badly. I can pull a couple of all-nighters but I can't sustain the pattern. The four-year sprint of building Stencil is real, but the four-year sprint is composed of weeks, and each week has to be sustainable.

Next month we'll talk about whether I'm actually doing any of this. She'll know if I'm bullshitting.
