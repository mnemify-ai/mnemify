# Pricing Strategy for Investing Feature

**Author:** Maya (with Aleks + Kavi)
**Status:** Recommendation for board review
**Date:** 2026-05-22

## The question

How do we price the investing feature when it launches in Q3?

## Options

### Option A: Free, included in existing Pro subscription

Subscription is currently $4.99/month for Pro tier (Pro vs. Free splits ~30/70). Investing becomes a Pro perk. No new revenue line.

Pros: friction-free adoption. Subscription conversion lift expected. Clean story for users.

Cons: leaves money on the table. We're paying DriveWealth platform fees + transaction costs. Margin compresses.

### Option B: New subscription tier — Pro Plus, $9.99/month

Pro Plus includes investing + everything in Pro. Free + Pro users see investing as a Pro Plus upgrade.

Pros: revenue stream tied to feature value. Clear upgrade path. Pro Plus narrative is positive (board likes it).

Cons: adoption tax. Some Pro users would invest if free but won't pay extra. We get less data on real investing behavior in year 1.

### Option C: Free for everyone, charge transaction fees

Investing is free. Transaction fees per buy/sell (modeled at 0.25% of transaction).

Pros: maximally accessible. Aligns with Robinhood/Cash App user expectations.

Cons: regulatory minefield. Charging per-transaction fees in retail brokerage has a fraught history (PFOF, etc.). Brand risk.

### Option D: Hybrid — Free with transaction fees on certain actions

Buys are free. Sells beyond a free monthly allowance cost $0.99. Or: ETF purchases free, individual stock (not yet, but later) costs per trade.

Pros: behavioral nudge (encourage buys, discourage churn-y selling). Some revenue.

Cons: complex to explain. Likely confuses users.

## My recommendation

**Option B (Pro Plus at $9.99)** for launch, with a 30-day free trial for all users at launch.

## Why

1. **Revenue clarity.** Series B narrative is cleaner with a new revenue line than with margin compression.
2. **Behavioral signal.** Users who pay are users who care. Year 1 cohort gives us clearer retention data.
3. **Upgrade narrative.** Pro Plus as a "graduation" from Pro is a positive story.
4. **30-day trial.** Removes the adoption tax for genuinely interested users. They'll convert at meaningful rates if the product is good.

## What I'm worried about

- **Conversion ceiling.** If only 5% of Pro users upgrade, the revenue story is weak. Kavi modeling 12-18% conversion. We'd accept 8% as okay.
- **Free-tier resentment.** Free users get investing in trial then lose it. Could feel punitive. Lin is working on UX framing.
- **Competitive pressure.** Cash App offers free investing. We're not Cash App. Different value prop.

## The compliance side

DriveWealth has done this dance before. Our pricing model doesn't change the regulatory posture. We just need to update disclosures.

I'd be more nervous about Option C (transaction fees) — that's where regulatory scrutiny lives. Sticking with subscription is the safer compliance story.

## What I want from the board

- Sign-off on the $9.99 price point. Aleks and I are aligned but board should weigh in.
- Permission to revisit pricing 90 days post-launch with real cohort data.

## What I'm explicit about

- Pricing decisions made today aren't permanent. We will adjust.
- The first 6 months of investing feature data will teach us things we can't predict.
- Series B investors will ask about pricing. The answer should be "we made a deliberate call, here's why, here's our learning plan."
