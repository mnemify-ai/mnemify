# VP Engineering Search Brief

**Author:** Maya
**Status:** Draft; search kicks off post Series B
**Date:** 2026-05-18

## What the role is

Stencil's first VP Engineering. Reports to me. Owns engineering operations — hiring, performance, process, cross-pod coordination, technical strategy execution. I keep architecture, board, strategic vendor relationships, and a small set of direct reports.

This is not a CTO-in-waiting role. I'm the CTO. The VPE is a partner.

## What I want from this person

### Background

- **Series A/B fintech experience.** Has built engineering operations at our stage before. Will not hire someone whose only background is bigco.
- **IC engineer before manager.** Has written real code. Has shipped real systems. Not a career manager.
- **Built teams 15-50.** Has been the engineering leader during the team growth we're about to experience.
- **Closed senior engineering hires personally.** Hiring is the most important thing they do in year 1.

### Disposition

- **Direct communicator.** I'm direct. They need to match.
- **Comfortable with ambiguity.** Stencil's environment is choppy. Series B is in flight. Investing is launching. They need to operate without certainty.
- **Strong opinions, loosely held.** Will tell me when I'm wrong. Will change their mind when evidence shifts.
- **Cares about engineers.** Not in a HR-language way. In a "knows when an engineer is struggling and does something about it" way.

### Anti-patterns I'm screening for

- **Process maximalist.** People who solve every problem with more meetings, more frameworks, more rituals. We're not that company.
- **Manager-only career.** Someone who hasn't shipped code in 5+ years. They lose technical credibility with the team fast.
- **Conflict-avoidant.** This role requires hard conversations. Constantly.
- **Status player.** Anyone whose questions in the interview are about title, comp band hierarchy, or org politics is wrong for us.
- **The "I've done this before" overconfident profile.** Every company is different. Humility about that matters.

## The hiring loop

5 rounds, total time roughly 8 hours over 2-3 weeks.

1. **Maya screen** (45 min) — Why Stencil, why now, the basics. I'm assessing fit and disposition.
2. **Aleks** (60 min) — Founder fit. Aleks needs to be able to work with this person.
3. **Marcus + Nadia + Devon panel** (90 min) — TLs assess "will I want to work with this person." Their veto matters.
4. **Live case study** (90 min) — A specific problem from Stencil's recent history (anonymized). They walk through how they'd think about it. Tests judgment, not just experience.
5. **Reference deep-dives** (offline, by me) — Talk to 3+ people who have worked with them in their last role. Get specific.

No coding interview. The VPE doesn't need to prove they can code in a 45-minute window. Their resume and references prove that.

## Comp philosophy for this hire

- Top of band. Maybe over band. The cost of a bad VPE hire is much higher than the cost of paying 10% more.
- Significant equity. This role's success is the company's success. Aligning equity matters.
- No signing bonus games. Just straightforward comp.

## What I'm NOT doing

- **Hiring a "founder VPE."** Not looking for someone who wants to be a co-founder. The co-founders are set.
- **Hiring based on impressive resume alone.** Bigco VPEs often don't translate. Won't be the heuristic.
- **Hiring before Series B closes.** Comp story isn't solid yet. Wait.
- **Using a recruiter.** I'll source through network + board + my own work. Recruiters add fees for searches I can run.

## Risk: this hire takes 9+ months

Possible. If it does, I keep running engineering ops with the help of TLs. Not ideal but survivable.

## Risk: the VPE doesn't fit after starting

90-day check-in built into expectations. If it's not working, we know early and act fast.

## Personal note

This is the most important hire of 2026 for the engineering organization. Treating it like that. I will be involved in every step and won't compromise on fit to fill the slot.
